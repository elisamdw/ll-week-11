01

The move updates state

A move is a small record of what a player did; the game state is what you get by applying every move so far, in order, to the starting position.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/the-move-updates-state/print02

Asymmetrical information

Players act on the same game state but see different parts of it: each seat holds something the others do not, and the table has only what is said and done.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/asymmetric-information/print03

Growth and upkeep

A loop in which each new unit adds the same output but more upkeep than the one before, so growth pays up to a point and then costs; and who pays the upkeep decides whether the grower ever feels it.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/growth-and-upkeep/print04

Prices that move

No printed price: each purchase raises the price of the next, and a designer can change the rule during play; past sales stay at what was paid.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/prices-that-move/print05

Coupling and cascade

Places depend on each other through links; a failure crosses a tight link at once and a loose link a tick later, so how tightly a system is coupled decides how far one failure spreads and whether anyone has time to act.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/coupling-and-cascade/print06

Nested turn loops and the vote

A game is a loop of rounds, a round a loop of phases, a phase a loop of turns; run fast, the steps look continuous, and the one place the loop waits for people is the vote, until the AI stops asking.

Play → print: /mechanics/studies/nested-loops/print
