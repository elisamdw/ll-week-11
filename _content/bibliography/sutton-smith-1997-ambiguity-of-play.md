# The Ambiguity of Play

**Citation:** Sutton-Smith, Brian. *The Ambiguity of Play*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1997.

**What it argues:** Play resists a single theory because each field that studies it brings its own values. Sutton-Smith sorts the theories into seven rhetorics: four older ones, play as fate, power, communal identity, and frivolity, and three modern ones, play as progress, as the imaginary, and as the self. Each rhetoric tells you less about play than about what its users want play to justify, from child development to the work ethic.

**Why it's canonical:** Its first chapter, "Play and Ambiguity," is the usual excerpt in game-studies courses (Carnegie Mellon 2018; the creativegames.org.uk module) and is reported to be reprinted in *The Game Design Reader* (unverified). It is the standard survey of play theory between Caillois and the digital game studies of the 2000s.

**Key terms:** the seven rhetorics of play (progress, fate, power, identity, the imaginary, the self, frivolity), ambiguity, play as adaptive variability.
