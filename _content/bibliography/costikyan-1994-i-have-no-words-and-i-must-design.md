# I Have No Words & I Must Design

**Citation:** Costikyan, Greg. "I Have No Words & I Must Design." *Interactive Fantasy* 2 (1994). Revised as "I Have No Words & I Must Design: Toward a Critical Vocabulary for Games." In *Proceedings of Computer Games and Digital Cultures Conference*, edited by Frans Mäyrä, 9–33. Tampere: Tampere University Press, 2002. https://doi.org/10.26503/dl.v2002i1.29.

**What it argues:** A game is not a puzzle (static), a toy (no goals), or a story (linear); "The thing that makes a game a game is the need to make decisions" (1994, seen on the memento-mori.com republication). It works through decision making, goals, opposition, managing resources, game tokens, and information to reach "A game is a form of art in which participants, termed players, make decisions in order to manage resources through game tokens in the pursuit of a goal" (1994, seen). The 2002 revision recasts this as "A game is an interactive structure that requires players to struggle toward goals" (p. 21, seen) and adds endogenous meaning: value, like Monopoly money, that the game's structure creates for its players (p. 22, seen).

**Why it's canonical:** It is one of the first attempts at a designer's vocabulary for all games, taught under "Histories and Definitions" (MIT 2006) and reprinted, per a snippet of its contents, in *The Game Design Reader*; the 2002 version opened the first DiGRA-era proceedings and is the more cited.

**Key terms:** decision making, goals, opposition, managing resources, game tokens, information, endogenous meaning, struggle, game vs. puzzle, toy, and story; color, simulation, narrative tension as strengtheners.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, editor) Costikyan's checklist as an editor check: each faction must name its goal, its opposition, the resources it manages, its token on the map, and what it cannot see; the AI deck is the one faction whose goal points at nothing outside the game, so its data centers are pure endogenous meaning.
