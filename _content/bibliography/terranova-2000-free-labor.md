# Free Labor: Producing Culture for the Digital Economy

**Citation:** Terranova, Tiziana. "Free Labor: Producing Culture for the Digital Economy." *Social Text* 18, no. 2 (2000): 33–58. https://doi.org/10.1215/01642472-18-2_63-33. Republished in *electronic book review* (2003) and in Trebor Scholz, ed., *Digital Labor: The Internet as Playground and Factory* (New York: Routledge, 2013).

**What it argues:** The digital economy runs on **free labor**: building websites, modifying software, moderating mailing lists, and volunteering as AOL community leaders, work that is "[s]imultaneously voluntarily given and unwaged, enjoyed and exploited" (ebr text, seen). Terranova reads this through the autonomist idea of **immaterial labor** and the **social factory** and argues that free labor is not an anomaly but "structural to the late capitalist cultural economy" (ebr text, seen); the volunteers act from a desire for cultural production that capital then captures.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the founding text of digital-labor studies, cited close to two thousand times, anthologized as chapter 2 of the Scholz reader, and the direct source of game studies' account of modders and players as unpaid workers (Kücklich's playbour, Dyer-Witheford and de Peuter's immaterial labor).

**Key terms:** free labor, immaterial labor, social factory, the digital economy, collective intelligence, NetSlaves.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, debrief) The AI's yield on every node counts a hidden "free labor" input (players' data, moderators, raters); when a workers' faction strikes, that input goes to zero and the AI's growth stalls, so the debrief can ask whose unpaid work the machine was running on.
