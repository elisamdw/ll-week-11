# Playing Games Across the Superintelligence Divide

**Citation:** Aaron Isaksen, Julian Togelius, Frank Lantz, and Andy Nealen, "Playing Games Across the Superintelligence Divide," in *AI, Ethics, and Society: Papers from the 2016 AAAI Workshop*, Technical Report WS-16-02 (Palo Alto, CA: AAAI Press, 2016), 89–97.

**What it argues:** If a superintelligence stood to us as an adult to a child, an expert to a novice, or a human to an animal, the games it would play with us can be guessed from the games we play across those gaps. The authors sort them into **friendly, learning, observational, and domination** games and warn that domination games "would pose a significant risk to humanity" (abstract, p. 89; quoted on the stored article page, seen by TH).

**Why it's canonical:** It is not canonical by citation (two in OpenAlex). It is on this list because it is the one paper by game-studies figures (Lantz, Togelius, Nealen) that treats superintelligence as a game-design question, written the year before *Paperclips*.

**Key terms:** superintelligence divide, friendly games, learning games, observational games, domination games.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) The AI's hidden "turn" card names one of the four relations (friendly, learning, observational, domination), and that stance sets which rows of its rule table fire.
