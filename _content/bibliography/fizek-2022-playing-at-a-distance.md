# Playing at a Distance: Borderlands of Video Game Aesthetic

**Citation:** Sonia Fizek, *Playing at a Distance: Borderlands of Video Game Aesthetic* (Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2022), open access (CC BY-NC-ND).

**What it argues:** Interactivity no longer describes much of play: in idle games, bots, auto battlers, and streams, the machine acts and the player watches, tunes, and returns later. Fizek calls this **play at a distance** or **dis-play** and gives one chapter each to interpassive, ambient, automated, intra-active, and spectated play, reading this automatic turn in games as a mirror of life with automation.

**Why it's canonical:** Three years after publication it was assigned on an introductory game-studies syllabus (OCAD, 2025), has three journal reviews, and is starred on the Stafford workshop list this game serves; it is the book-length version of her widely cited 2018 article.

**Key terms:** play at a distance, dis-play, interpassive play, ambient play, automated play, intra-active play, spectated play, distracted habituation.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) A delegation card: a human faction hands a node to an automated manager to save actions, and cannot see that node's state until it fails.
