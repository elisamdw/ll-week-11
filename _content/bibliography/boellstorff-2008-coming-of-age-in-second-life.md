# Coming of Age in Second Life

**Citation:** Boellstorff, Tom. *Coming of Age in Second Life: An Anthropologist Explores the Virtually Human*. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 2008. Paperback with a new preface, 2015.

**What it argues:** After more than two years of fieldwork inside Second Life as the avatar Tom Bukowski, using the methods anthropologists use offline, Boellstorff describes the world's culture (gender, race, sex, money, conflict and griefing, place and time, self and group) and argues that virtual worlds draw on a human capacity for culture as old as humanity: people have always been virtual in some sense (summary from the Princeton UP page; the book's text was not opened). Second Life is a world, not a game, which is why the book matters for game studies: it shows how much of what players do happens outside any win condition.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the founding anthropology of a virtual world, the book that made fieldwork inside one a method, taught in anthropology of games and of cybercultures (Edinburgh 2016-17, MIT 2009), and widely cited (1,418 on OpenAlex, on a record that may merge the book with its reviews).

**Key terms:** virtual worlds, the virtually human, techne, residents and avatars, participant observation in-world, griefing, virtual land and currency, place and time online.

**For the game (TH proposal):** some nodes on the map host residents rather than factions, a population with no win condition whose only goal is to keep its place; players decide whether to count them, and the debrief asks who the game treated as a player and who as scenery (B, debrief).
