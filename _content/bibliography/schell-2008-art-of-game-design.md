# The Art of Game Design

**Citation:** Schell, Jesse. *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*. Burlington, MA: Morgan Kaufmann, 2008. 2nd ed., A K Peters/CRC Press, 2014; 3rd ed., CRC Press, 2019. (First-edition date and imprint from the Elsevier page; the later editions from catalog search results and Schell Games' link, unverified; the city from memory.)

**What it argues:** Schell, a professor at Carnegie Mellon's Entertainment Technology Center (Elsevier, seen), argues that the principles of psychology that make board, card, and athletic games work are the same ones behind good videogames, so anyone can learn design without technical expertise (publisher's description, seen). The book follows a map from designer to experience, through game and player, and sets out 100 "lenses," each a set of questions to ask of a design, such as #7 the Lens of the Elemental Tetrad (aesthetics, mechanics, story, technology), #30 Fairness, and #46 Balance (summary from Schweizer's review, seen; the book's text was not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** Alongside Fullerton it is the textbook design courses choose, read as the practical counterweight to *Rules of Play* (Cornell 2025; MIT 2015), and it is the most cited design book in this batch on OpenAlex. Its review in the *American Journal of Play* faults its organization but places it in the introductory course, and Schell Games sells the lenses as a card deck for workshops.

**Key terms:** lenses, the elemental tetrad (aesthetics, mechanics, story, technology), the experience vs. the game, balance, indirect control, interest curves (unverified), the designer as listener (unverified).

**The tetrad, and why it matters here:** Schell treats **mechanics** as one of four basic elements of any game, beside **story**, **aesthetics**, and **technology**, none ranked above the others (Lens #7, via Schweizer's review, seen). This is the frame Marlon works from (Nov 14, voice chat): "the design of the game is much, much more than the rules." Schell's chapter on mechanics, as TH recalls it and has not checked against the book, sorts them into space, objects (with attributes and states), actions, rules, skill, and chance, so rules are one kind of mechanic among several; the objects-with-attributes-and-states category is the bridge to game state, templates, and instances in the [glossary](../glossary/README.md) (unverified).

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, debrief) a lens deck for remixing: before a group asks Claude Code to change the game, it draws one lens card (the tetrad, fairness, balance) and names which of the four elements its change touches; most groups will reach for mechanics, and the debrief asks what changed in the story, aesthetics, or technology.
