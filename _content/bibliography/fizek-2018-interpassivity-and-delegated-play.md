# Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games

**Citation:** Sonia Fizek, "Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games," *Transactions of the Digital Games Research Association* 3, no. 3 (2018): 137–63, https://doi.org/10.26503/todigra.v3i3.81.

**What it argues:** Idle games (incremental, clicker, self-playing) are fun even though they mostly play themselves, because they delegate enjoyment to the machine. Fizek borrows Pfaller's and Žižek's **interpassivity** (the VCR that watches for you, canned laughter) and calls idle games the first genre built on **delegated play**.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard scholarly account of idle games, cited about fifty times (OpenAlex) and used as a frame by later work such as Gekker's 2018 article on Let's Play spectatorship; Fizek's 2022 book, now on game-studies syllabi, grows out of it.

**Key terms:** interpassivity, delegated play, delegated enjoyment, idle game, elimination of drudgery.
