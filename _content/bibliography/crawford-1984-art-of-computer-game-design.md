# The Art of Computer Game Design

**Citation:** Crawford, Chris. *The Art of Computer Game Design*. Berkeley, CA: Osborne/McGraw-Hill, 1984. Electronic edition, Washington State University Vancouver, 1997.

**What it argues:** Its premise is that "computer games constitute a new and as yet poorly developed art form" (Preface, seen). Chapter 1 finds "four common factors: representation, interaction, conflict, and safety" in all games, a game being "a closed formal system that subjectively represents a subset" of the world (seen, WSUV edition). Later chapters cover why people play, a taxonomy of computer games, and a design sequence that starts from a goal and a topic.

**Why it's canonical:** It is one of the first design books for computer games, and Wikipedia (citing *The Video Game Theory Reader*) calls it the first on their theory; Juul includes its definition among his seven. WSU Vancouver put it online in 1997 for classroom use, which is how a generation of students read it. Note for the brief: the free edition was made by WSU Vancouver with Crawford's permission, not posted by Crawford himself; the WSU site was taken down around 2011 (Wikipedia) and survives in the Wayback Machine.

**Key terms:** representation, interaction, conflict, safety, closed formal system, game as art form, the game design sequence.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (debrief) Crawford's "safety": the game gives the experience of conflict and danger without its consequences, so the debrief asks what the table's safety hides, since the energy and water the AI uses on the map are spent off the map too.
