# Rules of Play

**Citation:** Salen, Katie, and Eric Zimmerman. *Rules of Play: Game Design Fundamentals*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2003. (Often cited 2004; Open Library and Juul give 2003.) Companion anthology: Salen, Katie, and Eric Zimmerman, eds. *The Game Design Reader: A Rules of Play Anthology*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006.

**What it argues:** A design textbook that treats a game three ways, as rules (a formal system), as play (an experience), and as culture (a context), through a series of "schemas," each a lens such as games as emergent systems, games of information, or games as social play. Its aim is "meaningful play," the relation between a player's action and the system's response. It defines a game as "a system in which players engage in an artificial conflict, defined by rules, that results in a quantifiable outcome" (p. 96, via Juul, seen).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the most assigned design text in the courses checked (MIT CMS.608, Carnegie Mellon, San Jose State) and the most cited book in this batch on OpenAlex. Its anthology, *The Game Design Reader*, is where many classrooms get Caillois, Suits, and Sutton-Smith.

**Key terms:** meaningful play (discernable and integrated, unverified wording), the magic circle as a design concept, rules/play/culture, schemas, emergence, the lusory attitude (from Suits), games as systems.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor) a meaningful-play check in the ruleset editor: a rule is flagged unless its effect shows on the map that round (discernable) and changes what someone can do later (integrated).
