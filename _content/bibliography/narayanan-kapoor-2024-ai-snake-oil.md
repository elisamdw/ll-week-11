# AI Snake Oil

**Citation:** Narayanan, Arvind, and Sayash Kapoor. *AI Snake Oil: What Artificial Intelligence Can Do, What It Can't, and How to Tell the Difference*. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 2024.

**What it argues:** "AI" lumps together different things: predictive AI (forecasting crime, grades, defaults) is where most of the snake oil is, generative AI is useful and overhyped, and the superintelligence frame (ch. 5) is rejected. The companion essay, "AI as Normal Technology" (2025), generalizes this: AI is a general-purpose technology like electricity, whose effects arrive through slow diffusion, and the defenses belong downstream, in hardening the systems an AI might attack.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the main skeptical alternative to both doom and utopia, assigned in courses opened (Swarthmore 2024, Rutgers 2026) and cited in the SEP; its essay is the usual pairing for AI 2027. The essay's subsection "Games provide misleading intuitions about the possibility of superintelligence" (https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology, seen by the theory entry's helper) is aimed at projects like ours.

**Key terms:** predictive vs. generative AI, snake oil, normal technology, diffusion, methods/applications/adoption, downstream defenses.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) Normal-technology ruleset: the AI's deck can only place a data center where a human faction has adopted it (sold power, approved a permit), so spread is set by adoption, not by the AI's will.
