# Games of Empire: Global Capitalism and Video Games

**Citation:** Nick Dyer-Witheford and Greig de Peuter, *Games of Empire: Global Capitalism and Video Games*, Electronic Mediations 29 (Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2009).

**What it argues:** Video games are the exemplary media of **Empire** (Hardt and Negri's planetary capitalism): born of the military-industrial matrix, they train players for networked work, war, and governance. The book follows the game machine down to its labor (studio crunch, **immaterial labor**, coltan mines, e-waste), reads *Full Spectrum Warrior*, *World of Warcraft*, and *Grand Theft Auto* as Empire's genres, and asks whether modding, piracy, and activist games make **games of multitude**.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard political-economy book on games, assigned in game-culture courses (CMU and John Cabot, both 2018) and cited about a thousand times across its OpenAlex records; later work on game labor answers it.

**Key terms:** Empire, multitude, immaterial labor, cognitive capitalism, biopower play, ludocapitalism, banal war, counterplay, games of multitude.
