# Value Capture

**Citation:** C. Thi Nguyen, "Value Capture," *Journal of Ethics and Social Philosophy* 27, no. 3 (2024): 469–504, https://doi.org/10.26556/jesp.v27i3.3048.

**What it argues:** Value capture happens when a person or group with rich or still-forming values meets a simplified, usually quantified version of them in an institution or technology (a Fitbit's steps, law-school rankings), and that version comes to dominate their reasoning. Nguyen separates it from using a metric as a **proxy** under reflective control, and contrasts it with games, where we adopt simple goals on purpose and can put them down.

**Why it's canonical:** Recent and outside game studies proper, but already widely cited for a philosophy paper and carried into a trade book; it is the follow-up that turns the scoring theory of Nguyen's *Games: Agency as Art* (the core entry) onto institutions and metrics.

**Key terms:** value capture, application capture, proxy, outsourcing value deliberation, games as a refuge from value complexity.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) Give each human faction a private metric card (revenue, megawatts sold, jobs, votes) that scores its win, while the shared goal, the region surviving, has no number.
