# Critical Play: Radical Game Design

**Citation:** Flanagan, Mary. *Critical Play: Radical Game Design*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2009.

**What it argues:** Games can be made and played for artistic, political, and social critique. Flanagan traces **critical play** from Victorian girls' doll play through Surrealist and Fluxus games, locative art, and activist computer games, and names three tactics: **unplaying** (acting against a game's script), **reskinning** (changing the surface so the same mechanics say something else), and **rewriting** (changing the story told around play). Her closing chapter turns these into a design method that writes values goals next to design goals, which she later formalized with Helen Nissenbaum as **Values at Play** and the Grow-a-Game cards.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard history and method for art and activist games, listed in introductory game-studies courses (Waterloo 2022) and game-art seminars (Carnegie Mellon 2024), and its values-in-design line is assigned in survey courses (MIT CMS.300, 2011).

**Key terms:** critical play, unplaying, reskinning, rewriting, values goals, Values at Play, Grow-a-Game.
