# Becoming-state: The Bio-cultural Imperialism of Sid Meier's Civilization

**Citation:** Pobłocki, Kacper. "Becoming-state: The Bio-cultural Imperialism of Sid Meier's *Civilization*." *Focaal: European Journal of Anthropology* 39 (2002): 163–177.

**What it argues:** An anthropologist reads *Civilization* (1991–2001) as the clash-of-civilizations thesis in software. Its **technology tree** rebuilds the nineteenth-century model of societies climbing through fixed stages (Pobłocki uses Charles Tilly), so the game's promised contingency always arrives at the same end: the modern Western state, with the United States as the telos. Play means identifying with that state, a "mega-powerful cyborg state-body" that disciplines its population (p. 173, seen); "In a sense, the state is the only actor in the game" (p. 172, seen in the repository PDF).

**Why it's canonical:** It is one of the first critiques of *Civilization*'s model of history and is cited by the standard later work on the series (Chapman, Voorhees, Lammes); it is a specialist text rather than a syllabus staple, which is why the tier is adjacent, but it is the reading Stafford's workshop uses for any tech tree students build.

**Key terms:** becoming-state, bio-cultural imperialism, technology tree as natural history, the cyborg state, orientalism and occidentalism, counterfactual history.
