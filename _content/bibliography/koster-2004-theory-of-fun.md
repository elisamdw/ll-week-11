# A Theory of Fun for Game Design

**Citation:** Koster, Raph. *A Theory of Fun for Game Design*. Scottsdale, AZ: Paraglyph Press, 2004. Second (10th anniversary) edition, Sebastopol, CA: O'Reilly Media, 2013. (Paraglyph's city from memory, unverified.)

**What it argues:** A short illustrated book, half drawings, arguing that fun in games is the pleasure of learning: a game is a set of patterns, the brain enjoys working them out, and boredom comes when a pattern is mastered or cannot be grasped. Games are therefore teachers, and what they teach depends on their patterns, which is why Koster asks designers to make games about more than shooting. The often-quoted line "Fun is just another word for learning" was seen only in secondary sources (unverified).

**Why it's canonical:** It is a common first design book for undergraduates (assigned whole in George Mason HNRS 353, 2010; listed at San Jose State, 2016) and is cited across game-based learning research, with over a thousand citing works on OpenAlex. Its claim that fun is learning is also the most argued-with sentence in the design canon (Koster answered critics on his blog in 2005).

**Key terms:** fun as learning, pattern recognition, chunking, grokking, boredom as mastery, the ethics of what games teach.
