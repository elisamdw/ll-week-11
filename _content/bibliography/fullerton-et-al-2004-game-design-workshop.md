# Game Design Workshop

**Citation:** Fullerton, Tracy, Christopher Swain, and Steven Hoffman. *Game Design Workshop: Designing, Prototyping, and Playtesting Games*. CMP Books, 2004. Editions: 2nd, *Game Design Workshop: A Playcentric Approach to Creating Innovative Games* (Elsevier Morgan Kaufmann, 2008), "Tracy Fullerton; with Christopher Swain and Steven S. Hoffman"; 3rd (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2014), 4th (CRC Press, 2018), and 5th (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2024), by Fullerton alone (editions from Open Library and Routledge; the 4th's year from an Open Library listing only).

**What it argues:** A workshop textbook built on exercises: design is learned by making paper prototypes and playtesting them early and often, before code or art. Its "playcentric approach" "focuses on involving the player in your design process" (5th ed. excerpt, seen on Game Developer), and the 5th edition adds "setting emotion-focused experience goals" (Routledge page, seen). The book moves from the structure of games (formal elements, dramatic elements, system dynamics) to ideation, prototyping, playtesting, balance, and working in the industry (5th ed. contents, seen on Routledge). The book's own text was not opened beyond the excerpt.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard studio textbook: MIT's design course builds its prototyping session on it (CMS.608, 2014), Cornell's game development course uses it as the main text (2025), and MIT's education-games course assigns its playtesting chapter (2015). It is the most cited design textbook in this batch after Schell's on OpenAlex.

**Key terms:** playcentric design, experience goals, formal elements, dramatic elements, system dynamics, paper prototyping, playtesting, iteration, balance.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor, debrief) a playtest loop: no ruleset in `/game/edit` can be saved as a new version without an experience goal ("the regulator should feel outrun by round 4") and a one-line playtest note on whether a table of strangers felt it.
