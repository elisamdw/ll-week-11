# Advanced Game Design: A Systems Approach

**Citation:** Sellers, Michael. *Advanced Game Design: A Systems Approach*. Boston: Addison-Wesley, 2018. (Copyright 2018; the place of publication is unverified.)

**What it argues:** A textbook that teaches game design as systems thinking: a game is parts, the loops among them, and the whole they make, and the designer must "move up and down the organizational levels" between the three (p. 173, seen). The contents (seen) run from a history of systems thinking through "The Four Principal Loops" and "Three Kinds of Gameplay Loops" (the index lists engines, economies, and ecologies; reinforcing and balancing loops, pp. 61-62, 220-221) to balance, progression curves, and transitive and intransitive systems. Chapter 5 warns the "inventor" designer that a mechanism left to run is not a game: "games must have human involvement as an integral part of the system" (p. 174, seen). Sellers designed *Meridian 59* and was lead designer on *The Sims 2*, and has published research on social artificial intelligence, partly funded by DARPA (About the Author, seen).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the current textbook statement of the systems view that runs from *Rules of Play* through Machinations, required in Utah's graduate game systems course (2018) and tested on Indiana's design students. Its classroom reach beyond those is unverified, so its tier rests on how closely it fits our game.

**Key terms:** parts, loops, wholes; the game+player system; reinforcing and balancing loops; core loops; engines, economies, ecologies; storytellers, inventors, toymakers; comprehensible, consistent, predictable, extensible, elegant (Achterman's five qualities, p. 175); "An arc is a broken loop that you exit immediately" (Daniel Cook, quoted p. 176, seen).

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, editor, debrief) each rule in `/game/edit` is tagged as a reinforcing or balancing loop and the editor refuses a ruleset whose AI engine has no human balancing loop against it; the debrief puts Sellers's inventor warning to the table: once the AI runs the map by itself, where is the player in the system?
