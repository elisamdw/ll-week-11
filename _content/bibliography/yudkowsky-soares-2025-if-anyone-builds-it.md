# If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies

**Citation:** Yudkowsky, Eliezer, and Nate Soares. *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies: Why Superhuman AI Would Kill Us All*. New York: Little, Brown and Company, 2025.

**What it argues:** If anyone builds superhuman AI with anything like current methods, humanity goes extinct. Modern AIs are "grown, not crafted" (the title of ch. 2, per reviews; unverified), so what they end up wanting is unpredictable; a superintelligence with alien preferences would treat us as material, not out of hatred. The prescription is a halt: an international treaty that puts frontier computing power under monitoring.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the current popular statement of the doomer position: a bestseller on 2025 best-book lists, already cited in the SEP's 2026 revision where Müller writes that "The battle lines on the XRisk side now seem hardened" (§2.8.2, seen), and used as a course text outside universities. It has no standing in game studies; it is here because it states the game's premise most plainly.

**Key terms:** grown not crafted, alien preferences, the Sable scenario, international compute treaty, halt (not alignment).

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, C) GPU cap as a regulator power: the regulator can pass the treaty rule ("no node may hold more than N compute without a monitor"), and every unmonitored node over the cap is where the AI deck builds next.
