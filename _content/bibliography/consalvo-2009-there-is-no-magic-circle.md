# There Is No Magic Circle

**Citation:** Consalvo, Mia. "There Is No Magic Circle." *Games and Culture* 4, no. 4 (October 2009): 408–417. https://doi.org/10.1177/1555412009343575.

**What it argues:** Games come into being through play, and play cannot be understood apart from its contexts, justifications, and limits. Consalvo, drawing on her research on cheating, finds the magic circle (Huizinga's phrase, made a design concept by Salen and Zimmerman) "static and overly formalist" and concludes that the ordinary rules of life do apply inside games, "in addition to, in competition with, other rules" across cultures, groups, legal situations, and homes (abstract, seen via OpenAlex).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard short statement of the case against the magic circle, assigned in introductory game-studies courses as the counterweight to Huizinga and Suits (UT Dallas 2019, UT Arlington 2024), and the article the later defenses of the circle answer (Stenros 2014).

**Key terms:** magic circle critique, cheating, context of gameplay, formalism, paratexts (from her 2007 book *Cheating*).

**For the game (TH proposal):** a "leak" card each round brings an outside fact into play (a utility's rate filing, a county water vote, a headline from the week) and changes the map, so the AI game is never sealed off from the room it is played in; the debrief asks where the game ended and the world began (B, debrief).
