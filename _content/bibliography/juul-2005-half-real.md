# Half-Real

**Citation:** Juul, Jesper. *Half-Real: Video Games between Real Rules and Fictional Worlds*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2005. Paperback, 2011.

**What it argues:** A video game is half-real: its rules exist and act on the player (winning and losing happen to us), while its fiction is imagined, and the two can disagree. Juul's **classic game model** gives six features shared by games over millennia, and he sorts challenge into **emergence** (simple rules combining into variation, as in strategy games) and **progression** (challenges presented in sequence). The book places video games in the long history of games and treats the ludology/narratology dispute as a question of how rules and fiction cue each other.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard book-length formal theory of video games from the ludology side, assigned in introductory courses (MIT CMS.300, Waterloo ENGL 294) and the source of the classic game model that later definitions answer.

**Key terms:** half-real, classic game model, rules and fiction, emergence and progression, incoherent worlds, valorization of outcomes, negotiable consequences.
