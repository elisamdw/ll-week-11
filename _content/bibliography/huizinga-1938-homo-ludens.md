# Homo Ludens

**Citation:** Huizinga, Johan. *Homo Ludens: Proeve eener bepaling van het spel-element der cultuur*. Haarlem: H. D. Tjeenk Willink, 1938. English translation: *Homo Ludens: A Study of the Play-Element in Culture*. London: Routledge and Kegan Paul, 1949; New York: Roy Publishers, 1950. (Dutch publisher and the 1949 imprint from memory, unverified; the 1950 Roy edition seen on archive.org.)

**What it argues:** Play is older than culture, and culture arises in the form of play: law, war, poetry, philosophy, and art all keep its traces. Play is free, set apart from ordinary life in its own time and place, governed by its own rules, and absorbing; it creates order. The book's first chapter gives the definition every later theory starts from.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the first reading in introductory game-studies courses (MIT CMS.300, 2011; UT Dallas ATCM 3366, 2019) and the first definition Juul tests in his survey of game definitions; the SEP names it as the main philosophy of play behind the philosophy of sport. Later work answers it: Caillois corrects it, Salen and Zimmerman turn its passing "magic circle" into a design concept, and Consalvo argues "there is no magic circle" (2009).

**Key terms:** play-ground, magic circle (one mention, in a list), play as free and "not serious", the spoil-sport versus the cheat, order and tension, agon (contest) as a source of culture.
