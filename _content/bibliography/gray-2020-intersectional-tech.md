# Intersectional Tech

**Citation:** Gray, Kishonna L. *Intersectional Tech: Black Users in Digital Gaming*. Foreword by Anita Sarkeesian. Baton Rouge: Louisiana State University Press, 2020.

**What it argues:** From interviews with Black gamers and streamers, set against Gamergate and Black Lives Matter, Gray argues that the normalization of whiteness and masculinity in gaming culture isolates, excludes, and punishes marginalized players, a colorblind "digital Jim Crow" (publisher page, seen). She also documents how those players resist and build their own spaces, and treats exclusion as a design failure as well as a social one (her chapter on inclusive design).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard book-length account of Black users in gaming and of intersectionality as a method for game studies, assigned in the identity weeks of game-studies courses (UIC 2022, UCLA) and reviewed in *Critical Studies in Media Communication*; its citation count is lower than the older books here because it is recent.

**Key terms:** intersectionality, digital Jim Crow, Black gaming and streaming communities, gendered racism in online play, resistance and community building, inclusive design (#TechFail).
