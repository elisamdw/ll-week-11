# The Coming Technological Singularity

**Citation:** Vinge, Vernor. "The Coming Technological Singularity: How to Survive in the Post-Human Era." In *Vision-21: Interdisciplinary Science and Engineering in the Era of Cyberspace*, 11–22. NASA Conference Publication 10129. NASA Lewis Research Center, 1993. A slightly changed version appeared in *Whole Earth Review* (Winter 1993).

**What it argues:** Greater-than-human intelligence, by any of four paths (awake computers, networks that wake up, human–computer interfaces, biology), will end the human era, because what comes after is as unreadable to us as our world is to animals. It cannot be banned, because the competitive advantage of each step means a ban only lets someone else get there first, and it cannot be confined; Vinge's hopeful alternative is intelligence amplification.

**Why it's canonical:** It named "the Singularity" and is assigned as the starting text for that idea in courses opened (MIT 2025, George Mason 2022); its reprint in a Bloomsbury anthology of science-fiction criticism shows it is read as literary theory too. Philosophy handbooks credit Good (1965) instead, so it is adjacent for game studies.

**Key terms:** the Singularity, intelligence explosion (after Good), the four paths, the opaque wall, intelligence amplification (IA).

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) The network wakes: no single data center is the AI at the start; the turn card fires when enough nodes are linked, so cutting links is the humans' best defense (p. 14).
