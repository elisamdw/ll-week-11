# Cow Clicker

**Citation:** Ian Bogost, *Cow Clicker*, Facebook game, July 2010–September 7, 2011; launch essay "Cow Clicker: The Making of Obsession," bogost.com, 2010, https://bogost.com/blog/cow_clicker_1/.

**What it argues:** A satire of FarmVille-style social games built as "an example that would act as its own theory" (https://bogost.com/blog/cow_clicker_1/, seen by TH): click a cow, wait six hours, pay to skip the wait, click friends' cows for credit. Bogost names what it mocks (friends turned into resources, compulsion, optional play bought off, destroyed time), and the game ended in the Cowpocalypse, a countdown players could delay by paying.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the field's standard example of theory made as a game and of the critique of compulsion loops, taken up in game-studies classes from 2011, analyzed in a media-studies journal, and the practical side of Bogost's "Gamification is Bullshit" and "Exploitationware" essays; *Cookie Clicker* and *Paperclips* inherit its form.

**Key terms:** carpentry (theory as artifact), enframing, compulsion, optionalism, destroyed time, Cowpocalypse, exploitationware.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) A public Cowpocalypse clock to the AI's endgame that any player can delay by paying resources, so the cost of postponing is visible and shared.
