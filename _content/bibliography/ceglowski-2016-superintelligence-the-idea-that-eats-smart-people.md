# Superintelligence: The Idea That Eats Smart People

**Citation:** Cegłowski, Maciej. "Superintelligence: The Idea That Eats Smart People." Talk, Web Camp Zagreb, October 29, 2016. Text published at idlewords.com.

**What it argues:** A reply to Bostrom's *Superintelligence* in two halves: eleven arguments titled "The Argument From ..." against its premises (wooly definitions of intelligence, complex motivations, and others), then arguments about what the belief does to the people who hold it, including its resemblance to religion. The cost Cegłowski stresses is attention drawn away from present harms: surveillance, data hoarding, and "how AI and machine learning affect power relationships" (seen: https://idlewords.com/talks/superintelligence.htm).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the most widely shared non-academic rebuttal to Bostrom and the one game designers and technologists tend to know, but the evidence for classroom use is thin, so it is adjacent; the academic versions of these objections are Thorstad and Mitchell.

**Key terms:** AI alarmism, "The Argument From Wooly Definitions," superintelligence as religion, present harms.
