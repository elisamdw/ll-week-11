# Gaming at the Edge

**Citation:** Shaw, Adrienne. *Gaming at the Edge: Sexuality and Gender at the Margins of Gamer Culture*. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2014. https://doi.org/10.5749/minnesota/9780816693153.001.0001. (Publisher and Crossref date the paperback January 1, 2015.)

**What it argues:** From interviews with players who are women, queer, and people of color, Shaw argues that players do not simply identify with characters who look like them: identification, interactivity, and fantasy come apart, and many such players do not call themselves gamers at all. Representation still matters, she argues, not because each group needs its mirror but because games show what kinds of people and worlds are possible, and the market logic of serving target audiences narrows that (summary from the publisher page; the book's text was not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard audience study of identity and representation in games, assigned in the identity weeks of game-studies courses (UCLA), awarded by the ICA's Popular Communication Division, and the base for Shaw's later work on queer game studies.

**Key terms:** identification versus representation, the gamer identity, marginal players, intersectional audience research, market logic of representation, diversity in user-generated content.

**For the game (TH proposal):** factions are dealt, not chosen, and each node on the map carries a "who lives here" line apart from its numbers; the debrief asks which faction anyone identified with and whether that tracked who they are (B, editor, debrief).
