# Rise of the Videogame Zinesters

**Citation:** Anthropy, Anna. *Rise of the Videogame Zinesters: How Freaks, Normals, Amateurs, Artists, Dreamers, Drop-outs, Queers, Housewives, and People Like You Are Taking Back an Art Form*. New York: Seven Stories Press, 2012.

**What it argues:** "Part critical essay, part manifesto, part DIY guide" (publisher page, seen), it argues that the videogame industry's corporate production and narrow audience have left most human experience out of games, and that the remedy is for everyone to make them, small and personal, like zines. Its practical chapters point readers to tools that need no programming, GameMaker, Twine, and Inform 7 among them, so that creation is open "to everyone, not just to publishers and programmers" (Scott Juster's review, PopMatters, June 28, 2012, seen).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the manifesto of the 2010s indie and queer games scene, assigned whole or in part in game-studies and digital-narrative courses (UCLA, Davidson 2016, UVic 2017), where Anthropy's own *Dys4ia* is also a standard first-week play (UCLA; UChicago Critical Video Game Studies 2021 and 2022).

**Key terms:** videogame zines, personal games, amateur and hobbyist authorship, free authoring tools (Twine, GameMaker), games about marginalized experience.
