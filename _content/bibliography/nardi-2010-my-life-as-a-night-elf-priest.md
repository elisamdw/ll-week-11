# My Life as a Night Elf Priest

**Citation:** Nardi, Bonnie A. *My Life as a Night Elf Priest: An Anthropological Account of World of Warcraft*. Ann Arbor: University of Michigan Press, 2010. Series: Technologies of the Imagination: New Media in Everyday Life. https://doi.org/10.3998/toi.8008655.0001.001.

**What it argues:** From more than three years of participant research in *World of Warcraft* in the United States and in Chinese Internet cafes, Nardi argues for reading play through **activity theory** and as **active aesthetic experience**, and works through what that explains: why work-like raiding is still play (her chapter "Work, Play, and the Magic Circle"), addiction, gender, the theorycraft and mods players write to play better, and how the same game means different things in China and North America (summary from the Fulcrum page and its table of contents; the chapters were not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** With Taylor's *Play Between Worlds* it is the standard MMO ethnography, free to read, and assigned in game-studies courses on labor and culture (UT Dallas 2019, its China chapter beside Geertz) and in anthropology of play (Edinburgh 2016-17).

**Key terms:** activity theory, active aesthetic experience, performative play, theorycraft, mods and addons, work and play, gold farming (with Kow), WoW in China.

**For the game (TH proposal):** a faction may write a "mod" in `/game/edit` that automates one of its actions (an addon), saving moves but locking in its play; the company that runs the servers can ban mods it dislikes, and the debrief compares the players' automation with the AI's (B, C, editor).
