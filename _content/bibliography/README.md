# Game studies: a master bibliography

> **Update, Nov 14, 14:47 (Marlon, voice chat):** the rule editor (`/game/edit`) is out ("this is just too opinionated"), and "rules" is not the key term: he thinks in **mechanics and game elements** (Schell): "the design of the game is much, much more than the rules." Students will remix the game's repo with Claude Code. So every "For the game" line below tagged **(editor)**, or proposing a per-rule field such as a "rhetoric" field, is **not adopted**; the other lines stand as proposals. See the [rules and mechanics handout](../handouts/rules-and-mechanics.md) and the [glossary](../glossary/README.md).

Marlon, Nov 14, 12:05: "can we work on a master bibliography of game studies where we bit by bit assemble the most canonical readings?" Earlier the same day: "The goal is to create game mechanics that align well with the readings/ideas."

One file per reading. Each gives the full citation, what it argues, **why it's canonical** (the readers that reprint it and the syllabi that assign it, from pages that were opened, in `canonical_evidence`), the key terms it gave the field, how to get it (`access`), and one **For the game (TH proposal)** line naming a mechanic. The file name is the reading's **id**; HI's [mechanics inventory](../mechanics/README.md) links mechanics to readings by id.

**Tiers:** **core** is on nearly every game-studies syllabus or reprinted in the standard readers; **important** is widely taught or central to our game; **adjacent** is outside game studies proper but read by it, or needed for this game (Bostrom, Perrow, the AI futures). An entry marked *(unverified)* has no evidence that was opened; most details still to check are listed in each file.

**Ids:** `<lastname>-<year>-<short-title>`, lowercase; two authors are both named, three or more are `<first>-et-al`; the year is the first publication. Ids are fixed once listed.

**Where the evidence comes from most often:** MIT OpenCourseWare CMS.300, *Introduction to Videogame Studies* (Fall 2011), whose readings page assigns most of the core; game-studies syllabi from Carnegie Mellon, UT Dallas, George Mason, Swarthmore, Edinburgh, Waterloo, and San Jose State; the tables of contents of *First Person* (2004) and *The Game Design Reader* (2006); the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; and OpenAlex citation counts (checked Nov 14).

Beside them, already in this repo: the [theory reading path](../theory/README.md) and the [stored articles](../theory/articles/README.md).
