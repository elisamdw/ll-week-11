# Uncertainty in Games

**Citation:** Costikyan, Greg. *Uncertainty in Games*. Playful Thinking. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2013.

**What it argues:** A short book (152 pages) by a veteran designer: games need uncertainty to hold us, from *Super Mario Bros.* and rock-paper-scissors to *Diplomacy*, *CityVille*, and poker, and the struggle to master it is their appeal. Costikyan sorts uncertainty by source, among them randomness, hidden information, narrative or development anticipation, and uncertainty of perception (Publishers Weekly, seen), and performative uncertainty and analytic complexity, which he treats critically, and solver's uncertainty (the 2016 book club thread, seen). The summary comes from the review and the book club; the book's text was not opened, and the full list of sources and its chapter order are unverified.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard short treatment of chance and the unknown in design, read by working designers (the 2016 book club) and assigned in a 2025 critical game studies course under "Uncertainty and Secrecy." It extends the line of *Characteristics of Games* on luck, skill, and hidden information, and it is one of MIT Press's short Playful Thinking books.

**Key terms:** sources of uncertainty, randomness, hidden information, performative uncertainty, solver's uncertainty, analytic complexity, player unpredictability (unverified), narrative and development anticipation, uncertainty of perception.
