# Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs

**Citation:** Bartle, Richard A. "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades: Players Who Suit MUDs." *Journal of MUD Research* 1, no. 1 (1996). Reprinted in *The Game Design Reader: A Rules of Play Anthology*, edited by Katie Salen and Eric Zimmerman. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006. Online at https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm.

**What it argues:** From years of running *MUD1*, Bartle sorts what players enjoy along two axes (acting on versus interacting with; the world versus other players) and gets four types: "So, labelling the four player types abstracted, we get: achievers, explorers, socialisers and killers" (https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm, seen). A healthy multi-user world keeps the four in balance, and the paper models how changing one population (more killers, fewer socialisers) shifts the others. The page notes it is an April 1996 extension of his 1990 "Who Plays MUAs."

**Why it's canonical:** It is the first and most widely taught player typology, reprinted in *The Game Design Reader* and assigned in the players week of introductory courses (MIT 2011, UT Arlington 2024), where the online Bartle Test is often set alongside it; later player-motivation models start from it.

**Key terms:** player types, achievers, explorers, socialisers, killers, the interest graph (acting/interacting, world/players), population balance.

**For the game (TH proposal):** deal each human faction one of Bartle's suits as a secret second goal (achiever: top your metric; explorer: reveal the hidden turn card first; socialiser: broker the most trades; killer: wreck the most AI nodes), and let the AI's deck target whichever suit is ahead, so the class sees which player type an optimizer exploits (B, debrief).
