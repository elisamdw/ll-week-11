# Extrastatecraft

**Citation:** Easterling, Keller. *Extrastatecraft: The Power of Infrastructure Space*. London: Verso, 2014. Assigned: "Introduction" and "Disposition."

**What it argues:** The rules of shared space, the free trade zone, the broadband network, the ISO quality-management standard, form an "infrastructure space" that governs as much as law does, and much of that power is exercised outside, alongside, or in partnership with states: extrastatecraft (Publishers Weekly review, seen). "Disposition" is her tool for reading it: the latent tendencies and active potentials of a space or an organization, its "accidental, covert, or stubborn forms of power," and especially the gap between what a zone says it is doing and what it does (as summarized in the 2018 Failed Architecture interview, seen). She tells activists to work on that space by indirect means (gossip, humor, misdirection) as well as direct opposition. We did not open the book; this comes from the review and the interview.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard text on infrastructure space in architecture and media studies, widely reviewed and cited, and on Stafford's Nov 9 syllabus; our Stafford annotated bibliography already names it as the course's reading on infrastructure as a place of power.

**Key terms:** infrastructure space, extrastatecraft, disposition, the zone, active form, what a thing says vs. what it does.
