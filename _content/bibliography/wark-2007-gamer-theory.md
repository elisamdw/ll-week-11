# Gamer Theory

**Citation:** McKenzie Wark, *Gamer Theory* (Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 2007); first posted online as *GAM3R 7H30RY* version 1.1 (Institute for the Future of the Book, 2006) and revised as *Gamer Theory 2.0*.

**What it argues:** The world outside games has become **gamespace**, where everything is ranked and scored under a **military entertainment complex**, and games are its purest, fairest form. Games are **allegorithms**: their surfaces double an algorithm that models the hidden algorithms of daily life, so the **gamer as theorist** plays within the game but against gamespace. Nine chapters each read one game, from *The Sims* and *Civilization III* to *SimEarth*.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard critical-theory book on games, assigned chapter by chapter in game-culture courses (CMU and John Cabot, both 2018), reviewed across media studies, cited over four hundred times, and an early networked book.

**Key terms:** gamespace, military entertainment complex, allegorithm, gamer as theorist, atopia, agony, the digital as ranking.
