# Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight

**Citation:** Geertz, Clifford. "Deep Play: Notes on the Balinese Cockfight." *Daedalus* 101, no. 1 (Winter 1972): 1–37. Reprinted in *The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays*, 412–53. New York: Basic Books, 1973; and in *Daedalus* 134, no. 4 (Fall 2005): 56–86. (Winter 1972 issue seen on the American Academy page; the 1972 volume, issue, and pages and the 1973 pages from memory, unverified. The id uses the 1973 book reprint; `year` gives first publication.)

**What it argues:** Geertz reads the Balinese cockfight as a text. Borrowing Bentham's term "deep play" for wagers whose stakes are too high to be rational, he argues that the large center bets are not about money but about status, honor, and kin, and that the fight dramatizes the village's hierarchy without changing it. The cockfight is a story the Balinese tell themselves about themselves.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard anthropology reading in game-studies courses, often in a week on games as culture (UT Dallas 2019; Carnegie Mellon 2018), and Daedalus chose it for its 2005 anniversary reprint. It is also the founding example of interpretive "thick description," so game scholars cite it for method as well as for play.

**Key terms:** deep play, shallow play, center bet and side bets, status gambling, thick description, culture as an ensemble of texts.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) side bets in public trust, not money: before the AI's turn, factions may wager trust on whether a node holds, and the payout is standing at the table rather than resources, so the bets show who is invested in the AI's growth.
