# Gaming: Essays on Algorithmic Culture

**Citation:** Galloway, Alexander R. *Gaming: Essays on Algorithmic Culture*. Electronic Mediations. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2006.

**What it argues:** Video games are actions, not texts or images, and both the operator and the machine act. Chapter 1 crosses operator/machine with diegetic/nondiegetic to get **four moments of gamic action**, including the **ambience act**, where the machine keeps running while the player waits. The other chapters read the first-person shooter against film, ask what **social realism** in games would be, read *Civilization* as an **allegory of control** (informatic control, after Deleuze), and propose **countergaming**, the game equivalent of avant-garde counter-cinema.

**Why it's canonical:** Chapter 1 is a standard early-week reading in introductory courses (MIT, GMU, Swarthmore, Davidson), often with most of the book assigned, and chapter 4, on *Civilization*, is the reading Stafford assigns on strategy games.

**Key terms:** gamic action, four moments, operator and machine, diegetic and nondiegetic, ambience act, allegory of control, informatic control, social realism, countergaming.

**For the game (TH proposal):** the ambience act as an async rule: when a faction misses its turn window, the machine plays that turn (the AI builds instead), so the AI acts whenever the humans are away (B, C).
