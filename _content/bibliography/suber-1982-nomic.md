# Nomic

**Citation:** Suber, Peter. *Nomic*. Game, first published in Douglas R. Hofstadter, "Metamagical Themas," *Scientific American*, June 1982. Printed with commentary as "Nomic: A Game of Self-Amendment," appendix 3 of Peter Suber, *The Paradox of Self-Amendment: A Study of Law, Logic, Omnipotence, and Change* (New York: Peter Lang, 1990).

**What it argues:** "Nomic is a game in which changing the rules is a move" (Suber, as quoted on Wikipedia; unverified against the appendix). Players start from an initial set of mutable and immutable rules, take turns proposing amendments that pass by vote, and score points; any rule, including the rules for changing rules, can be transmuted and changed. Suber built it to embody his book's thesis about legal systems that authorize their own amendment.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the reference point for any game whose rules are part of play, alive in Agora since 1993 and in many online variants, and the clearest model for treating rules as a political object; its standing in game-studies syllabi is not documented here, so it is important rather than core.

**Key terms:** self-amendment, mutable and immutable rules, transmutation, the paradox of self-amendment.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor) Nomic is the precedent for the editor at `/game/edit`: a student who proposes a change to the ruleset, with its `rhetoric` line, and wins a vote has made a move in the game, not a pause outside it (HI's `lucid-player`).
