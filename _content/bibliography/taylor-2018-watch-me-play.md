# Watch Me Play

**Citation:** Taylor, T. L. *Watch Me Play: Twitch and the Rise of Game Live Streaming*. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 2018. https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691184975.

**What it argues:** From interviews and years in the scene, Taylor argues that live streaming turns private play into networked broadcasting (the title of her second chapter): streamers build home studios and brands, audiences chat with them and each other as part of the show, and esports events reach worldwide audiences. The growth, she argues, is reshaping user-generated content, ownership, intellectual property, and how platforms govern their users (summary from the publisher page and its chapter list; the book's text was not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the founding study of game live streaming, by the ethnographer of *Play Between Worlds*, widely cited (394 in OpenAlex), awarded by the ASA's CITAMS section, and assigned in game-studies ethnography weeks (UCLA).

**Key terms:** networked broadcasting, home studios, play as performance, audience participation through chat, esports broadcasting, platform governance, the work of play.

**For the game (TH proposal):** when the game runs async online, the round is broadcast and spectators may vote one event card into play per round, so watchers become a faction the players have to perform for (B, C).
