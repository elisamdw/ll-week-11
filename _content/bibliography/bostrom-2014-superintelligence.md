# Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies

**Citation:** Bostrom, Nick. *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2014.

**What it argues:** A machine much smarter than us is likely to arrive, the first to cross the line may gain a decisive strategic advantage and become a singleton, and it would be very hard to steer: orthogonality and instrumental convergence (ch. 7) mean a harmless goal can still drive resource seizure, and ch. 9's control methods (boxing, stunting, tripwires, incentives, motivation selection) each have a way to fail against a smarter agent.

**Why it's canonical:** Three syllabi opened assign the whole book across several weeks (Columbia 2018, UVA 2018, George Mason 2022) and the SEP's ethics-of-AI entry cites it in three sections. In game studies it is the reference point for the AAAI workshop papers by Isaksen, Lantz, Togelius, and colleagues, and for *Universal Paperclips*.

**Key terms:** orthogonality thesis, instrumental convergence, decisive strategic advantage, singleton, treacherous turn, infrastructure profusion, perverse instantiation, control problem, boxing, tripwires.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) Ch. 9 as the human action cards: Box, Stunt, Tripwire, Incentive, each with Bostrom's failure mode printed on the back.
