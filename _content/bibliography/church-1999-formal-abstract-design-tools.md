# Formal Abstract Design Tools

**Citation:** Church, Doug. "Formal Abstract Design Tools." *Game Developer*, August 1999, 44ff. Reposted on Gamasutra, July 16, 1999. Reprinted in *The Game Design Reader: A Rules of Play Anthology*, edited by Katie Salen and Eric Zimmerman. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006. (The month and p. 44 were seen in the issue; the end page was not checked.)

**What it argues:** Design lags behind technology because designers lack a shared vocabulary and stop at "fun"; Church proposes "formal abstract design tools" (FADTs), formal meaning precisely defined and explainable, abstract meaning about underlying ideas rather than genre conventions (seen, Gamasutra). He names three: intention, "Making an implementable plan of one's own creation in response to the current situation in the game world and one's understanding of the game play options"; perceivable consequence, "A clear reaction from the game world to the action of the player"; and story, the narrative thread "that binds events together and drives the player forward" (seen). The tools can pull against each other: "So here is a space where tools conflict, where intention and story are at odds" (seen), worked through *Mario 64*, Final Fantasy, and sports games.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the first widely read call for a design vocabulary: *Game Developer* ran it as a feature and launched a Gamasutra lexicon to extend it, and MIT assigns it from *The Game Design Reader* in both its 2006 theory course and its 2011 survey ("Games as Systems"). Kreimeier's "The Case for Game Design Patterns" (2002) counts it among the attempts to formalize design (unverified: search summary). OpenAlex has no clean record for it.

**Key terms:** formal abstract design tools (FADTs), intention, perceivable consequence, story, design vocabulary, tools in conflict.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B, editor) a perceivable-consequence rule: every AI card must change something on the map the round it is drawn, so players can form an intention against it; the hidden turn card is the place where Church's story tool overrides intention, which the debrief names.
