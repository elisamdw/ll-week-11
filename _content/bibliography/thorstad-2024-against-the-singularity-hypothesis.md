# Against the Singularity Hypothesis

**Citation:** Thorstad, David. "Against the Singularity Hypothesis." *Philosophical Studies* 182, no. 7 (2025): 1627–51. Published online May 10, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-024-02143-5.

**What it argues:** Granting that machines may approach general intelligence, Thorstad attacks only the claim that self-improving AI will grow fast enough, for long enough, to leave us far behind. He gives five reasons for doubt (extraordinary claims, ideas getting harder to find, bottlenecks, physical constraints, sublinear returns to compute), tests Chalmers's and Bostrom's growth models against them, and concludes that without a fast takeoff the doom argument has to show how ordinary capabilities would destroy us.

**Why it's canonical:** It is recent and its standing is in philosophy: the SEP's 2026 revision of its ethics-of-AI entry already lists it, and it circulates as the companion reading to Chalmers on the singularity. It is here as the critic whose limits are the B map's own inputs.

**Key terms:** singularity hypothesis, fishing-out, bottlenecks, physical constraints, sublinearity, proportionality thesis, optimization power over recalcitrance.
