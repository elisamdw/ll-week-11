# Atlas of AI

**Citation:** Crawford, Kate. *Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence*. New Haven, CT: Yale University Press, 2021.

**What it argues:** AI is an extractive industry, not an immaterial one. Chapter by chapter (Earth, Labor, Data, Classification, Affect, State) Crawford follows it back to lithium mines, energy and water, underpaid workers, scraped data, and state power, and argues that "artificial intelligence" names a system of power more than a technique.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard critical book on AI's material costs: prize-winning (Sally Hacker Prize, 2022), heavily cited, required whole in a Swarthmore course and excerpted (ch. 1, "Earth") in a political-economy syllabus, both opened. For this game it is the empirical counterweight to Bostrom's frictionless "resources."

**Key terms:** extraction, planetary costs, the atlas as method, Earth/Labor/Data/Classification/Affect/State, AI as a registry of power.
