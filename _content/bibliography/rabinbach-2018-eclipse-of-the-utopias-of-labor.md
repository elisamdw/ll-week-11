# From Mimetic Machines to Digital Organisms

**Citation:** Rabinbach, Anson. "From Mimetic Machines to Digital Organisms: The Transformation of the Human Motor." In *The Eclipse of the Utopias of Labor*, 1–27. Forms of Living. New York: Fordham University Press, 2018. The earlier book it continues: Rabinbach, *The Human Motor: Energy, Fatigue, and the Origins of Modernity* (New York: Basic Books, 1990).

**What it argues:** The publisher's description (via Project MUSE and the Princeton Alumni Weekly listing, seen) says the book traces the move from the eighteenth-century idea of the human being as a machine to the late-twentieth-century idea of digital organisms, from Vaucanson's *Digesting Duck* through Marx's *Capital* and Helmholtz's social thermodynamics to the post-Fordist workplace, and the decline after 1945 of the hope that a more efficient workplace would make a more satisfying society. The assigned opening chapter, by its title, carries *The Human Motor*'s figure (the working body as an engine converting energy into work, with fatigue as its limit) forward to automata that imitate bodies and to digital organisms. We did not open the chapter; this is from the publisher, the table of contents, and the 1990 book's framing.

**Why it's canonical:** *The Human Motor* is the canonical history of the science of work and fatigue; this volume is its sequel and is less cited, so the tier is adjacent. Its reviews in Thesis Eleven and its place on Stafford's workshop-day syllabus are the evidence here.

**Key terms:** the human motor, fatigue, energeticism and social thermodynamics, labor power, utopias of labor, mimetic machines (automata), digital organisms, post-Fordism.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B)/(editor) Fatigue: each human faction has an energy track that each action drains and only a rest round refills, while AI nodes never tire; in the editor, a rule's `rhetoric` field must say which model of the worker it assumes (motor, organism, or code), so the class can see which one their map rewards.
