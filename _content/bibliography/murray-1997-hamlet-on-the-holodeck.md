# Hamlet on the Holodeck

**Citation:** Murray, Janet H. *Hamlet on the Holodeck: The Future of Narrative in Cyberspace*. New York: Free Press, 1997. Updated edition, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2017 (unverified: from a retailer listing in search results).

**What it argues:** The computer is a new medium for storytelling, the way print and film were, and its stories will come from what the medium does well. Digital environments are procedural, participatory, spatial, and encyclopedic, and they offer three aesthetic pleasures: **immersion**, **agency**, and **transformation**. Agency is "the satisfying power to take meaningful action and see the results of our decisions and choices" (ch. 5, quoted in Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009, seen; page unverified). The author of such works writes the rules that generate many versions of a story, which she calls procedural authorship.

**Why it's canonical:** It gave game scholarship its standard definition of agency (Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009) and three of its chapters are separate sessions of MIT's intro course; it is also the narrativist position that Frasca, Aarseth, and Eskelinen wrote against, which makes it half of the field's founding debate.

**Key terms:** agency, immersion, transformation, procedural authorship, multiform story, cyberdrama, the four properties of digital environments (procedural, participatory, spatial, encyclopedic).

**For the game (TH proposal):** agency as a rule: every faction action is logged with its visible result on the map the next round, so no move disappears into the AI's turn (B); debrief asks where students felt agency and where it was missing (debrief).
