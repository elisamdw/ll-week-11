# Man, Play and Games

**Citation:** Caillois, Roger. *Les jeux et les hommes: Le masque et le vertige*. Paris: Gallimard, 1958. English translation by Meyer Barash: *Man, Play and Games*. New York: Free Press of Glencoe, 1961; reprint, Urbana: University of Illinois Press, 2001. (Gallimard details from memory, unverified.)

**What it argues:** Caillois accepts Huizinga's starting point and corrects it: play is free, separate, uncertain, unproductive, governed by rules, and make-believe, and it includes gambling and material stakes, which Huizinga had set aside. He sorts games into four kinds, agôn (competition), alea (chance), mimicry (role play), and ilinx (vertigo), each running from paidia (free improvisation) to ludus (rule-bound effort).

**Why it's canonical:** It follows Huizinga as the second session of MIT's CMS.300 (2011) and opens George Mason's HNRS 353 (2010); Juul includes its six-part definition among the seven he compares. Its four categories are still used as a classroom tool (Pedercini's Caillois workshop at Carnegie Mellon, 2018) and as tags in mechanics inventories.

**Key terms:** agôn, alea, mimicry, ilinx, paidia, ludus, the six formal qualities of play, corruption of games.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor) each rule in a ruleset carries a Caillois tag, so the class sees the mix at a glance: the AI deck is alea, the factions' race is agôn, faction roles are mimicry, and the cascade when coupled nodes fail is the closest the game comes to ilinx.
