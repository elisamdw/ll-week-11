# Unit Operations: An Approach to Videogame Criticism

**Citation:** Bogost, Ian. *Unit Operations: An Approach to Videogame Criticism*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006.

**What it argues:** Games, and any other medium, can be read as configurations of discrete, interlocking units of behavior ("unit operations") rather than as wholes explained by one totalizing structure ("system operations"), an idea Bogost takes from object-oriented programming and cellular automata. Procedural criticism lists what a game lets its units do and asks what those operations mean together. Part III names the simulation gap, the space between a model's rules and the player's understanding of the system it models.

**Why it's canonical:** It started the procedural line of criticism that *Persuasive Games* turned into "procedural rhetoric," and its uptake shows in the citation counts and in Short's long review; syllabi assign the 2007 book more often, so this one is important rather than core.

**Key terms:** unit operations, system operations, procedural criticism, simulation gap, simulation fever, simulation resignation, simulation denial.
