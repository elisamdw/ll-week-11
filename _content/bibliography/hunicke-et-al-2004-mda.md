# MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research

**Citation:** Hunicke, Robin, Marc LeBlanc, and Robert Zubek. "MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research." In *Proceedings of the AAAI Workshop on Challenges in Game AI*. San Jose, CA, 2004. (Venue from memory, unverified; the PDF names only the GDC workshop where the framework was taught.)

**What it argues:** A five-page paper that splits a game into three causally linked lenses: mechanics, "the particular components of the game, at the level of data representation and algorithms"; dynamics, "the run-time behavior of the mechanics acting on player inputs and each others outputs over time"; and aesthetics, "the desirable emotional responses evoked in the player" (p. 2, seen). Designers work from mechanics toward aesthetics, players the other way. In place of "fun" it offers eight aesthetics: sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, submission (seen).

**Why it's canonical:** MIT assigns it in both its game-studies survey (CMS.300, 2011) and its design studio (CMS.608, 2014), and it came out of four years of the GDC design workshop, so designers and scholars share its vocabulary. It is short, open, and the usual first framework in a design course.

**Key terms:** mechanics, dynamics, aesthetics, the eight kinds of fun, designer and player perspectives, games as artifacts rather than media, tuning.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor) each rule gets an `aesthetic` tag from the eight beside its `rhetoric` line, and a playtest note on the dynamics it produced, so students see that the JSON is mechanics and what they argue about is dynamics; the AI faction's aesthetic is submission.
