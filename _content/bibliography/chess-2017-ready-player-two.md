# Ready Player Two

**Citation:** Chess, Shira. *Ready Player Two: Women Gamers and Designed Identity*. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2017. https://doi.org/10.5749/minnesota/9781517900694.001.0001.

**What it argues:** The games industry designs for an imagined woman, "Player Two," which the publisher's page calls "the industry idealization of the female gamer" (seen): white, middle class, heterosexual, cisgender, and able-bodied. Chess reads that designed identity in casual, hidden-object, and time-management games through their treatment of time, caregiving, social networks, consumption, and bodies, and argues that developers and players need to rethink whom they design for (summary from the publisher page; the book's text was not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** It gave game studies the term "designed identity" for the player an industry imagines and builds for, and it is the standard book on games marketed to women, assigned in courses on games and social identity (UIC 2022) and reviewed in *Feminist Media Studies*.

**Key terms:** designed identity, Player Two, the imagined player, casual and time-management games, women's leisure time, the industry's audience logic.

**For the game (TH proposal):** every ruleset saved in `/game/edit` carries a "designed for" field naming the player it imagines (how much time they have, what they already know); the debrief sets that beside who played it, and the async version is scored for players with ten minutes a day (editor, debrief).
