# Control and Freedom

**Citation:** Chun, Wendy Hui Kyong. *Control and Freedom: Power and Paranoia in the Age of Fiber Optics*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006. Assigned: "Scenes of Empowerment," 130–144 (the chapter runs 129–170).

**Date note:** Stafford's syllabus says 2005 and so does MIT Press's Crossref record (print date December 23, 2005). The book is usually cited as 2006, and Open Library's main record gives 2006 (January 1, 2006) beside separate records dated 2005 (seen). The likely reason is that a book released in the last week of December often carries the next year on its copyright page; we did not see the copyright page. Both dates name the same first edition; the id keeps 2006.

**What it argues:** The publisher's abstract (seen via Crossref) asks how the Internet, "a medium that thrives on control," came to be accepted as a medium of freedom. Drawing on Deleuze and Foucault, Chun argues that the myths of total freedom and total control both come from turning political problems into technological ones, and that the Internet's democratic promise lies not in individual empowerment but in exposing us to others, and other machines, that we cannot control. The abstract also says she shows how promoters conflated technological empowerment with racial empowerment; the title "Scenes of Empowerment" suggests that is this chapter (unverified: we did not open it).

**Why it's canonical:** A founding book of critical internet and new media studies, steadily cited, by a scholar whose later books (*Programmed Visions*, *Discriminating Data*) extend it. Stafford opens his course with it, so it is the frame the students have had longest.

**Key terms:** control and freedom, paranoia, empowerment, open/closed (replacing public/private), fiber optics as metaphor and as material.
