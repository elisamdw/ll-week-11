# Games: Agency as Art

**Citation:** Nguyen, C. Thi. *Games: Agency as Art*. New York: Oxford University Press, 2020.

**What it argues:** Games are the art form whose medium is agency: the designer specifies what the player wants and what she can do, and players take on these **disposable ends** temporarily, a capacity Nguyen calls **agential fluidity**. Building on Suits, he separates achievement play (playing to win) from **striving play** (wanting to win for the sake of the struggle). The last part of the book turns to the danger in games' clarity: gamification and **value capture**, where a simplified, often quantified, value supplied from outside takes over a person's or group's reasoning.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the main philosophical account of games of the last decade, recognized with the APA's 2021 Book Prize and taught as a whole-term text in philosophy-of-games courses (Minnesota 2023), where the published replies by Hurka, Kukla, and Noe are read alongside it. Its standing in game-studies (as opposed to philosophy) syllabi was not checked, hence tier "important."

**Key terms:** agency as art, sculpted agency, disposable ends, agential fluidity, striving play, achievement play, value capture, seductions of clarity.

**For the game (TH proposal):** each human faction plays to a private metric card (revenue, megawatts sold, jobs, votes) while the shared goal has no number, and only humans get a "revise your metric" action, which the AI never gets (B, debrief).
