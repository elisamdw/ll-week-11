# Play Anything

**Citation:** Bogost, Ian. *Play Anything: The Pleasure of Limits, the Uses of Boredom, and the Secret of Games*. New York: Basic Books, 2016.

**What it argues:** Play is not an escape from a dull world but close attention to a limited one: in the book's words, as LaLone quotes it, "a way of operating a constrained system in a gratifying way" (unverified: no page; via LaLone 2017). Fun comes after, as a by-product of taking a thing's limits seriously. Bogost replaces Huizinga's magic circle with the playground, any bounded place whose materials and rules we accept, and diagnoses the present as "ironoia," an ironic distance that keeps the world at arm's length.

**Why it's canonical:** It is a trade book, read as Bogost's late answer to the magic circle and to gamification; a modest citation count, two journal reviews, and one graduate syllabus place it as adjacent rather than core.

**Key terms:** playground, ironoia, the pleasure of limits, fun as a by-product of respecting constraints.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (editor) Design by subtraction: a student may improve a ruleset only by deleting one rule or resource and saving the copy, then plays a round to test whether the cut made it better (HI's `design-by-subtraction`).
