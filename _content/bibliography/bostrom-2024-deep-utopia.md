# Deep Utopia: Life and Meaning in a Solved World

**Citation:** Bostrom, Nick. *Deep Utopia: Life and Meaning in a Solved World*. Ideapress Publishing, 2024.

**What it argues:** *Superintelligence* asked how the AI transition goes wrong; this book asks what people would do if it goes right, in a "solved world" where human effort is not needed for any practical purpose. Bostrom treats job loss as the easy problem and spends the book on the purpose problem, defending meaning in layers, one of which is artificial purpose: goals we set ourselves, with rules that forbid the shortcuts, as games do.

**Why it's canonical:** It is not canonical yet: two years old, recommended (not required) in one course opened (MIT MAS.342, 2025), with academic reviews (NDPR) but no game-studies citations found. It is listed because its argument lands on the philosophy of games.

**Key terms:** solved world, post-instrumental condition, technological maturity, plasticity and autopotency, shallow vs. deep redundancy, the purpose problem, artificial purpose.
