# Play Between Worlds

**Citation:** Taylor, T. L. *Play Between Worlds: Exploring Online Game Culture*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2006.

**What it argues:** An ethnography of *EverQuest*, written from Taylor's own play and a visit to the game's Fan Faire, arguing that massively multiplayer games are social spaces whose life runs across online and offline networks rather than inside a sealed world. Its chapters on **power gamers** (instrumental, work-like play that is still play), on women players who do not fit the stereotypes, and on who owns a game world when player culture meets the company that runs the servers set the agenda for later work on player communities (summary from Taylor's 2006 Microsoft Research talk page and the Edinburgh handbook; the book's text was not opened).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the founding ethnography of MMO culture, still the most cited book in this area (770 in OpenAlex), assigned as essential reading in anthropology-of-play courses (Edinburgh 2016-17), and the base for Taylor's later "assemblage of play," which game-studies syllabi assign beside Consalvo.

**Key terms:** power gamers, instrumental play, play between worlds (online/offline), guilds and social networks, player ownership and intellectual property, women players.

**For the game (TH proposal):** the company faction holds the server: once per game it may patch a rule in `/game/edit` mid-play, and the other factions may answer with a petition or a boycott, staging Taylor's question of who owns a game world (B, editor).
