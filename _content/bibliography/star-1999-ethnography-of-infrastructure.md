# The Ethnography of Infrastructure

**Citation:** Star, Susan Leigh. "The Ethnography of Infrastructure." *American Behavioral Scientist* 43, no. 3 (1999): 377–391. https://doi.org/10.1177/00027649921955326. (The article runs 377–391, per the journal and the scan; the syllabus assigns 377–386.)

**What it argues:** "This article is in a way a call to study boring things" (p. 377, seen in the OCR text in The Notebook): plugs, standards, classifications, and forms, where the politics of a system sit. Infrastructure is relational, not a thing: "One person's infrastructure is another's topic, or difficulty" (p. 380, seen). Star lists its properties (pp. 381–382), among them embeddedness, transparency, being learned as part of membership, being built on an installed base, and becoming visible upon breakdown, and gives the researcher's tricks: identify master narratives and the "others" they leave out, surface invisible work, and read the paradoxes of infrastructure.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the founding methods paper of infrastructure studies, with thousands of citations, a 2016 MIT Press reprint, and a standard place in the first week of infrastructure seminars. For this game it is the method behind Stafford's index-card database: the cards are an infrastructural inventory.

**Key terms:** infrastructure as relational, study the unstudied, infrastructural inversion (after Bowker), the properties of infrastructure, visible upon breakdown, installed base, invisible work, master narrative, "symbolic sewers" (p. 379).
