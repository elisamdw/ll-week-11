# Play Matters

**Citation:** Sicart, Miguel. *Play Matters*. Playful Thinking. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2014.

**What it argues:** Play, not the game object, is what matters: a mode of being in the world that is contextual, carnivalesque, appropriative, disruptive, and autotelic yet negotiated. Sicart separates play from **playfulness** (carrying play's attitude into other contexts), reads toys and playgrounds as props and spaces that invite play without fixing its purpose, and casts the designer as an "architect of play." It is the book-length form of his 2011 case against procedural rhetoric: meaning is made by players, not only encoded in rules.

**Why it's canonical:** Its first chapter, "Play Is," is the standard opening reading on play in current game-studies and game-art courses (UT Dallas 2019, Carnegie Mellon 2024, Edinburgh 2016-17), usually set beside or against Huizinga and Caillois.

**Key terms:** play as being in the world, appropriative play, carnivalesque play, playfulness, toys, playgrounds, architects of play, dark play.
