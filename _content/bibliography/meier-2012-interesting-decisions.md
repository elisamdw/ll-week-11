# Interesting Decisions

**Citation:** Meier, Sid. "Interesting Decisions." Lecture, Game Developers Conference, San Francisco, March 2012. Video, GDC Vault. https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015756/Interesting.

**What it argues:** Meier takes his own line, "Games are a series of interesting decisions," as a working lens for design: ask what decisions a design puts to the player and whether they are interesting (report by Leigh Alexander, Game Developer, March 7, 2012, seen; the video itself was not watched). Interesting decisions involve a tradeoff, are situational, let players express a style, and are persistent, affecting the game "for a certain amount of time"; risk against reward is the classic case; a choice that is always the first option or could be random is not interesting (same report, seen).

**Why it's canonical:** The line is one of the most quoted in game design, cited as familiar dictum in *Game Developer* (Johnson, 2009), and this free GDC talk is Meier's own account of it, assigned by section in at least one course reader. **Attribution:** the line is Meier's, usually dated to a GDC (then the Computer Game Developers Conference) talk in 1989; the 2012 talk opens from it (Escapist report, unverified: search snippet; the page returned 403). No record of the 1989 wording was found, it circulates as "decisions" and as "choices," and his 2020 memoir, *Sid Meier's Memoir! A Life in Computer Games*, returns to it (Time and a review, seen; the memoir not opened).

**Key terms:** interesting decisions, tradeoffs, situational decisions, personal style, persistence, risk and reward, information and feedback to the player.

**For the game (TH proposal):** (B) each faction's turn must offer at least one tradeoff that depends on the map (harden a node or fund oversight, never both), and the AI's tiers in its strategy ladder are measured by which human decisions they make uninteresting.
