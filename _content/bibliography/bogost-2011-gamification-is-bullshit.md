# Gamification Is Bullshit

**Citation:** Bogost, Ian. "Gamification Is Bullshit." Blog post, bogost.com, August 2011, a position statement for the Wharton gamification symposium; also published in *The Atlantic*, August 9, 2011. Expanded as "Why Gamification Is Bullshit," in *The Gameful World: Approaches, Issues, Applications*, edited by Steffen P. Walz and Sebastian Deterding, 65–80. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2015.

**What it argues:** Gamification, as sold by consultants, takes the incidental parts of games (points, badges, levels, leaderboards) for what makes games work, and uses them to extract behavior cheaply. Bogost proposes "exploitationware" as the honest name. The longer Gamasutra column the same year adds that pretend incentives strip away both value and trust.

**Why it's canonical:** It is the standard critique that gamification courses and game-studies surveys pair with Deterding et al.'s definition, assigned at Waterloo and (via its companion column) at Wharton, and its book version is widely cited.

**Key terms:** exploitationware, gamification as marketing, pointsification (Margaret Robertson's term, which Bogost credits).
