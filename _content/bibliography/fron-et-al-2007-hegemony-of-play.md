# The Hegemony of Play

**Citation:** Fron, Janine, Tracy Fullerton, Jacquelyn Ford Morie, and Celia Pearce. "The Hegemony of Play." In *Situated Play: Proceedings of DiGRA 2007 Conference*, 309–318. Tampere: Digital Games Research Association, 2007. https://doi.org/10.26503/dl.v2007i1.283.

**What it argues:** A small, mostly white and male corporate and creative elite of publishers, retailers, developers, and self-selected "hardcore gamers" decides which technologies are used, which games get made and by whom, and which players and play styles count, and so has "narrowed the playing field" for women, "non-gamers," and others who are in fact the majority (abstract, seen in the DiGRA PDF). The authors trace this through the production environment (the 2005 IGDA survey), the technologies of play, and the cultural positioning of "gamers," set it against the history of pre-digital games, and read the Nintendo DS and Wii as signs it can change; the term came from an April 2005 Bernie DeKoven lecture at USC (seen in the PDF).

**Why it's canonical:** It is the short, free, much-cited name for the critique that the industry defines who a player is, the reference point for later feminist game studies, taught in critical game-studies modules (Leicester) and borrowed in later titles (Woodhouse 2022). The evidence opened is thinner than for the books in this area.

**Key terms:** hegemony of play, the "hardcore gamer" and the "non-gamer," production environment, technologies of play, cultural positioning of players.
