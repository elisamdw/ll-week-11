# The map tells the story

## Definition

The places in a game, and the routes through them, tell its story: the player learns what happened by where things are and what they find there. The map can also become a record of what the player did.

In one line (EG's row): "Where things are, and the route through them, carry the story; the map can also record what happened." [1]

## Description

Henry Jenkins calls this 'narrative architecture', with game designers as builders of story spaces. [4]

In games:

- **[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild](../games/zelda-breath-of-the-wild.md)**: Link wakes a century after Ganon turned the Guardians against Hyrule, and regains his memories by returning to places from his past. [2]
- **[Dark Souls](../games/dark-souls.md)**: Item descriptions and the look of the world tell the history of Lordran, a decaying kingdom, without pulling the player out of the game for story. [3]

**Shows it** (EG's row): The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild; Dark Souls; Risk boards after a game [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): An AI's spread across a map is a story told by where it is. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): partly: the region map shows where the AI has spread [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Draw a map of five places on 11x17 paper; each place reveals one line of a story when visited. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Places as nodes with text; visiting a node logs it, and the log is the story. [1]

## Why to

The ENGL 189VG planning: "maps spatialize development of plot", and can be "a record of the story (this character did this, then that, then that)". [5]
