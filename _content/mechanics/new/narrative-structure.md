# Narrative structure

## Definition

The shape of a game's story as the player moves through it: linear, a string of pearls (open areas joined in a fixed order), branching, a web or network, an open world, or a loop. [2]

In one line (EG's row): "The shape of a game's story: linear, string of pearls, branching, web, open world, or loop." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Final Fantasy X](../games/final-fantasy-x.md)**: The journey runs mostly along a single path from place to place, and an airship late in the game lets the player travel Spira faster. [3]
- **[Mass Effect](../games/mass-effect.md)**: Dialogue and mission choices branch the story and carry into the sequels. [4]
- **[Baldur's Gate (series)](../games/baldurs-gate.md)**: The ENGL 189VG brainstorm's example of an open world, where "how you interact with characters and what you explore determines what you experience". [5]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Linear: Half-Life; branching: Mass Effect; open world: Baldur's Gate 3 [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): An AI-run story can change shape as it is played. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): partly: a loop with a hidden turn [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Lay out six story cards in each of the six shapes and ask which one a game you know uses. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Story beats as nodes and the player's choices as links; the shape is the graph. [1]

## Why to

The six shapes are listed as a station idea in the ENGL 189VG planning. [2]
