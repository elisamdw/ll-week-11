# Tutorial and onboarding

## Definition

The game teaches its own rules as you play, introducing one action at a time, often through the story's opening scenes rather than a manual.

In one line (EG's row): "The game teaches its own rules in play, often through the story's first scenes." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Super Mario Bros.](../games/super-mario-bros.md)**: World 1-1 introduces the first enemy, a Goomba, and the first power-up, a mushroom from a ? block, getting the player ready for the rest of the game. [2]
- **[Portal](../games/portal.md)**: The test chambers introduce one challenge after another, and the game's support is slowly removed as the player progresses. [3]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Super Mario Bros.' first screen; Portal's test chambers [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): An AI tutor that teaches the rules as you play (see The tutor face). [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Teach a game without the rulebook: the first round has only one rule, and each round adds one. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): A first level that unlocks one action at a time, with the fiction explaining each. [1]

## Why to

The ENGL 189VG planning: "tutorials are almost like the call to action"; the brainstorm asks how a game introduces its mechanics "by way of setting, theme". [4][5]
