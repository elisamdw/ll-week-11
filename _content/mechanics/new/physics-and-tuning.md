# Physics and tuning

## Definition

The numbers that govern how things move (gravity, jump velocity, friction, speed) and the practice of changing them and playing again to change how the game feels.

In one line (EG's row): "The numbers that set how movement feels (gravity, jump velocity, friction), changed and replayed." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Super Mario Bros.](../games/super-mario-bros.md)**: How high Mario jumps depends on how long the jump button is held, and he speeds up smoothly from a walk to a run. [2]
- **[Portal](../games/portal.md)**: Momentum carries through a portal, so the player gains speed by falling into one portal and flies out of the other. [3]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Super Mario Bros.' jump; any Unity scene with a gravity setting [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): Ask an AI to tune a game until it 'feels right' and see what it optimizes for. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Toss a ball, then a balloon, then a paper airplane: same throw, different physics. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Open a Unity scene, change jump velocity or gravity in the Inspector, and play it again. [1]

## Why to

The ENGL 189VG planning: "we could have game velocity in unity up and have them change it"; the vibe-coding station: "vary a property (like jump velocity or gravity)" and "play the scene". [4][5]
