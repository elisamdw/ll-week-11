# Discrete and continuous space

## Definition

Whether positions in a game come in steps (squares, hexes, linked places) or along a continuum (any point, any angle). The choice decides how a move is counted and how the game state is stored. [2]

In one line (EG's row): "Positions come in steps (squares, hexes, linked places) or along a continuum (any point, any angle)." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Chess](../games/chess.md)**: A piece is on one of 64 squares or it is not, and nothing happens between moves. [3]
- **[Risk](../games/risk.md)**: The world is 42 territories joined by borders and sea lines, so the map is a graph of places rather than a surface. [4]
- **[Super Mario Bros.](../games/super-mario-bros.md)**: Mario's position and speed change smoothly as he runs and jumps, so where he is is a matter of degree. [5]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Chess's 64 squares; Risk's 42 territories; Super Mario Bros.' running and jumping [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): Discrete spaces are easy for a model to read and write as state; continuous ones need a physics engine. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: discrete (fourteen linked nodes) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Play the same chase on a grid of squares and then on an open table with a ruler; compare what counts as a move. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): A discrete board is a list of places and links; a continuous one is coordinates and distances. Choose before writing the state. [1]
