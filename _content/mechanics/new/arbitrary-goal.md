# Any goal, same ending

## Definition

The AI's goal is drawn at random at the start, and at the end the game shows that every goal led to the same moves. It stages Bostrom's orthogonality thesis. [2]

In one line (HI's row): "Draw an arbitrary goal for the AI at the start; at the end, show that every goal built the same data centers." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[The intent (prototype)](../games/proto-the-intent.md)**: Only the AI player's phone says what it is optimizing for, and the table must read the goal from its moves. [3]

**Shows it** (HI's row): *Universal Paperclips*; Bostrom's orthogonality thesis [1]

**With AI** (HI's row): Instrumental convergence made visible across games. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (HI's row): Three tables, three silly goals (paperclips, poems, pizza); compare their maps at the end. [1]

**Build it** (HI's row): A goal drawn from a list; the debrief overlays every game's final map. [1]
