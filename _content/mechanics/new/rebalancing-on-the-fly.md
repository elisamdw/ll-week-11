# Rebalancing on the fly

## Definition

Changing a game's numbers or rules while it is being played, or between one playthrough and the next, to correct what is too strong, too slow or no fun, and carrying on with the change. [2][3]

In one line (EG's row): "Change a number or a rule while the game is being played, and keep playing with the change." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Monopoly with live rents (prototype)](../games/proto-monopoly-with-live-rents.md)**: The rents move off the printed deeds onto a screen, so a designer can rebalance them between games or let them rise with every landing. [4]
- **[League of Legends](../games/league-of-legends.md)**: Riot Games publishes balance patches every few weeks that change champions' and items' numbers, so the same game plays differently from one patch to the next. [5]
- **[Fluxx](../games/fluxx.md)**: Cards played during the game change how many cards you draw and play, and even what the goal is. [6]

**Shows it** (EG's row): House rules; live-service balance patches; Fluxx [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): An AI can propose the change from the log of play; the table decides. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): partly: rulesets are data files and remixes are saved, but not changed mid-game [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): After round two, the table votes to change one number (a cost, a threshold) and plays on. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Rules as a data file the engine re-reads each round, edited during play through Claude Code. [1]

## Why to

Marlon, Nov 16, 17:31 (voice agent's record): with a card's properties held digitally, "You can rebalance the game as gameplay develops. In a game like Monopoly, that means inflation, or differential pricing". [2]

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Play a round, let the class vote one number change, and play again: the change is the lesson.
2. Use it to show that a game's balance is a choice someone made, and can be argued with.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Prices written on sticky notes on the board, not on the cards, so they can be changed between rounds.
2. A house rule announced at the start of each round.

### Digitally

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A ruleset file edited through Claude Code between playthroughs (the week's game).
2. A shared spreadsheet of values the game reads.
