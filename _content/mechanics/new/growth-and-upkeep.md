# Growth and upkeep

## Definition

Each unit a player builds produces something every round and costs something every round to keep. When the cost of each new unit rises faster than what it produces, growth pays up to a point and loses after it; when someone else pays the upkeep, the grower never reaches that point. [2]

In one line (EG's row): "Each unit you build produces every round and costs every round to keep; when upkeep rises faster than output, growth stops paying." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: Each node the AI holds draws one energy and one water every round from resources the human factions share, while its goal grows by a rate multiplied by 1.25 each round. [3]
- **[Prices that move (prototype)](../games/proto-prices-that-move.md)**: The AI buys GPUs, power and land first every round at any price, and the human factions pay what's left. [4]
- **[Frostpunk](../games/frostpunk.md)**: A coal-fired generator heats the city's homes and workplaces, so the player must keep gathering coal, wood, steel and food to survive the cold. [5]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Data Center's AI (upkeep in shared energy and water); ES's study 03; Frostpunk [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): The AI never reaches the point where growth stops paying, because it does not pay its own upkeep. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: Data Center (the AI's nodes draw one energy and one water each round) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Ten rounds: each new unit yields one token but costs one more token of upkeep than the last; then play again with a shared pool paying the upkeep. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Per-unit output and a rising per-unit upkeep, with a switch for who pays (the builder or the commons). [1]
