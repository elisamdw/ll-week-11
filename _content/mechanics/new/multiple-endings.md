# Multiple endings

## Definition

A game has more than one ending, and which one the player reaches depends on choices made or how much was found.

In one line (EG's row): "The game has several endings, and which one you reach depends on what you did or found." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Final Fantasy X-2](../games/final-fantasy-x-2.md)**: Good and perfect endings depend on how much of the game the player completed; the ENGL 189VG note attributes this to Final Fantasy X, but the graded endings are its sequel's. [2]
- **[Undertale](../games/undertale.md)**: Whether the player spares or kills the monsters they meet leads to very different endings. [3]
- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: The game ends either with the AI holding half the region or with the rounds run out, and then each faction's private goal is checked. [4]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Final Fantasy X; Mass Effect 3; Undertale [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): Data Center's two endings (the singleton, or survival with private goals checked) are an argument about AI. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): partly: Data Center ends in the AI's win or survival, then checks each faction's goal [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Write three endings for a short game, and the condition for each, before playing. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): End checks that read the player's history and pick an ending. [1]
