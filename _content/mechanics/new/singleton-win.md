# Singleton win

## Definition

One side wins once no other player can still make a decision that changes the outcome, rather than by reaching a score. The name is Bostrom's term for a world with a single decision-maker. [2]

In one line (HI's row): "The AI wins when no human faction can still make a decision that changes the map." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: The game ends when the AI holds half the region: 'No faction can make a decision that changes the map anymore.' [3]
- **[Risk](../games/risk.md)**: In the classic game a player wins by eliminating every other player, so the winner is the only one left who can act. [4]
- **[Universal Paperclips](../games/universal-paperclips.md)**: The game ends when the AI has turned all the matter in the universe into paperclips. [5]

**Shows it** (HI's row): Data Center's end at half the map [1]

**With AI** (HI's row): Losing is not being destroyed but being made irrelevant. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: Data Center (half the region) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (HI's row): Stop the game the moment no player has a move that matters, and ask who decided that. [1]

**Build it** (HI's row): An `end.aiShare` threshold, or a check that no faction action can still change ownership. [1]

## Why to

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Ask what it means to lose not by score but by losing the ability to choose.
2. Compare with Normal Technology, which has no such ending.
