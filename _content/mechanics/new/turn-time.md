# Turn time

## Definition

How long one turn takes to resolve, for a person or for an AI player. Because a model takes seconds or minutes to answer, turn time decides which games it can play or run: turn-based and strategy games, not reflex games. [2]

In one line (EG's row): "How long one turn takes, for a human or an AI player, decides which kinds of games can be played." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Chess](../games/chess.md)**: Time controls range from blitz, with ten minutes or less per player, to correspondence chess, with at least a day per move. [3]
- **[Civilization](../games/civilization.md)**: The computer players take their turns between the human's, and players expect to wait for them. [4]
- **[Pokémon Red and Blue](../games/pokemon-red-and-blue.md)**: The battles are turn-based and wait for the player's command, and Anthropic's 'Claude Plays Pokémon' stream (2025) showed a model reasoning step by step through Pokémon Red. [5]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Claude plays Pokémon (turn-based battles); correspondence chess; real-time strategy as the opposite [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): A model that takes seconds to answer fits turn-based, strategic play, not reflex games. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: turn-based; an AI seat would take a turn of its own [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Play one round where every move must be made in five seconds, and one where each player has a minute; compare. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Give the AI player a turn in the engine and measure how long it takes; design the game so waiting for it is part of play. [1]
