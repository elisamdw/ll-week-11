# The tap as the move

## Definition

A player moves by touching a phone to a tagged object (an NFC card, a figure, a sticker), and the tap sends that move to the game state everyone shares. The object stays the same while what it means in the game can change. [2]

In one line (EG's row): "Touching a phone to a tagged card or object is the move, and the tap updates the shared game state." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Skylanders: Spyro's Adventure](../games/skylanders.md)**: Setting a toy figure on the Portal of Power brings that character into the game, and the figure carries its level and upgrades to the next console. [3]
- **[Every tap is a row (prototype)](../games/proto-every-tap-is-a-row.md)**: Ninety seconds of tapping badges and supplies to build data centers: every tap writes a row, and the board is only those rows replayed. [4]

**Shows it** (EG's row): The LL's NFC cards (Nov 16–17); Skylanders' portal; Nintendo's amiibo [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): The tap can hand a move to an AI player, or ask the AI what the card means in this game. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no (proposed: NFC cards for dealing hands; the first proof of concept, Nov 17) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Lay one NFC card per seat on the table; each player taps their phone on their card to see a private hand. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): An NFC tag holds a URL with the card's id; the page it opens posts the move to the game's API, which updates the state everyone sees. [1]

## Why to

Marlon, Nov 16, 17:31 (voice agent's record): "The tap is how you update state. ... Tapping isn't just looking; it's the move that changes the game." And at 17:35, think about "where it feels natural and pleasing, as opposed to clumsy and annoying." [2]

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Deal a hand: one card per seat, each tap shows that player's private information on their phone (Marlon's proof of concept, Nov 17, 06:01).
2. Give a physical board digital properties that change in play (prices, health), so the card stays and the game rebalances.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A stack of NFC stickers on index cards and the students' own phones; the tag opens a web page.
2. Without tags: QR codes on the same cards, scanned with the camera.

### Digitally

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A web page per card, opened from a link list, posting the move to a shared state.
2. A Slack message per tap, as a log of moves.
