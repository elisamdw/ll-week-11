# The input device

## Definition

The object the player acts through (a controller, keyboard, touchscreen, motion sensor, tagged toy) and the mapping from its inputs to moves in the game. The same game feels different through a different device.

In one line (EG's row): "The controller, keyboard, touchscreen or tag the player acts through, and how its buttons map to moves." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Wii Sports](../games/wii-sports.md)**: The Wii Remote is swung like a tennis racket or a bowling ball, so the move is the gesture. [2]
- **[Guitar Hero](../games/guitar-hero.md)**: A guitar-shaped controller with colored fret buttons and a strum bar makes playing along feel like playing. [3]
- **[Skylanders: Spyro's Adventure](../games/skylanders.md)**: Toy figures placed on a portal are the input: which figure you set down decides who you play. [4]

**Shows it** (EG's row): The NES controller; the Wii Remote; Guitar Hero's guitar [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): Natural language as the input device: what moves does a text box allow? [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no (proposed: NFC cards as the input, see The tap as the move) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): Play the same simple game with a keyboard, then with one button only, then by voice. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): Map one action to three inputs in Unity's Input System, or in a web page's key and touch handlers. [1]

## Why to

The ENGL 189VG planning names input devices as a station idea: "how users execute actions", "the tactile side of developing/experiencing the plot", "reflecting on these as objects". [5]
