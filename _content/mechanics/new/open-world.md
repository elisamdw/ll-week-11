# Open world

## Definition

A game world the player can explore in almost any order, where what you visit and whom you meet decide what you experience and unlock.

In one line (EG's row): "What you explore and whom you meet decide what you experience and unlock." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild](../games/zelda-breath-of-the-wild.md)**: After the opening plateau the player can go anywhere, including straight to the final boss. [2]
- **[Dungeons & Dragons](../games/dungeons-and-dragons.md)**: The dungeon master's world has no fixed route: where the players choose to go decides what they meet. [3]
- **[Baldur's Gate (series)](../games/baldurs-gate.md)**: Named in the ENGL 189VG brainstorm as open world, with "infinite possibilities in terms of how you move through the world and develop relationships". [4]

**Shows it** (EG's row): Baldur's Gate 3; The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild; D&D campaigns [1]

**With AI** (EG's row): An AI game master can keep an open world consistent as players wander. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): no [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (EG's row): A map with eight places and no set order; each group chooses its route and reports what it found. [1]

**Build it** (EG's row): A world of places with conditions, not a sequence; the player's state decides what each place shows. [1]

## Why to

The ENGL 189VG brainstorm contrasts "book style - beginning, middle, end" with open world. [4]
