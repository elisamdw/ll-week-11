# Chess

## Definition

A two-player game on a board of 64 squares, in which each side tries to checkmate the other's king. [2]

## Description

**Made by:** Traditional · **Form:** Board

Mechanics it shows in this inventory:

- **[Goal and win condition](../gm/goal-and-win-condition.md)**: Checkmate ends the game at once, whatever material either side still has, so a player can be far ahead and still lose. [1]
- **[Turn order](../gm/turn-order.md)**: White moves first and the players alternate, one move each. [2]
- **[Movement and position](../gm/movement-and-position.md)**: Each piece moves in its own pattern, and what it threatens depends on where it stands. [2]
- **[Strategy ladder](../new/strategy-ladder.md)**: Chess programs offer levels that play stronger as the level rises. [3]
- **[AI advisor](../new/ai-advisor.md)**: In 'advanced chess', which Garry Kasparov introduced in 1998, each human player consults a chess program during the game. [4]
- **[Proving ground](../new/proving-ground.md)**: IBM's Deep Blue beat world champion Garry Kasparov in a match in 1997. [5]
- **[Turn time](../new/turn-time.md)**: Time controls range from blitz, with ten minutes or less per player, to correspondence chess, with at least a day per move. [6]
- **[Discrete and continuous space](../new/discrete-and-continuous-space.md)**: A piece is on one of 64 squares or it is not, and nothing happens between moves. [2]
- **[The move updates state](../new/the-move-updates-state.md)**: A game can be written down as its list of moves in notation, which lets a reader play through the game again. [7]

**In the LL:** Nov 16, 2026: the first test of computer vision that reads game boards in the studio started with chess (Marlon's day-one report, voice agent's record). [8]
