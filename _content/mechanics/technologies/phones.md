# Phones

## Definition

Smartphones used in a session in two ways: as cameras (handheld, on a gimbal or on a stand) and as each person's own screen, which can show what only that person may see and can read NFC tags or open links.

## Description

From the record:

- **As cameras.** TDM 155AI class 3, Sept 23, 2026, the AV tools class: "Shooting with phones, and shooting with studio cameras — two different stations" [3]. Marlon, Aug 8, 2026: "We definitely need more iPhone rigs, enough Osmos that every group can have one, I think is definitely essential" [4].
- **As private screens.** Nov 16, morning: "Things involving asymmetrical information on the phone: good" [2]. Nov 17, 06:01: "Clearly this could just be done with phones; people play those Jackbox-type games where they input codes and share that kind of information", and the proposed first NFC test is "a card for each position around the table, people tapping their phones there and then seeing whatever the secret information is on their phone" [1].
- **As NFC readers.** The NTAG215 cards worked with phones on Nov 16 [1]. "iPhone XS and later support background tag reading"; Android phones with NFC on open URL tags in the browser [5]. The iPhone "reads at the top edge of the back" [1].

## How to

1. **Join by link or tag.** Every phone opens the same game URL; each player is told apart by a code, a tag at their seat or a join step [1][5].
2. **Same Wi-Fi as the laptop** when the game runs on it, at the laptop's LAN IP and port [5].
3. **Phones must be unlocked and in use** for a tag to be read; on iPhone a banner must be tapped before the page opens [5].
4. **As a camera on a stand:** pointed down for an overhead view, or on a gimbal for movement [3][4].

What goes wrong: mixed iPhone and Android behaviour, the iPhone banner, phones with NFC off, and phones that cannot reach a laptop's address [5].

## Why to

From Marlon: asymmetrical information, "a revealing of asymmetrical information" through each player's own screen (Nov 17, 06:01) [1]; and phones as the camera every group can have (Aug 8) [4].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** what the phone is for in a non-game session.

1. Each student's private answer before the room's reveal (a vote or a guess hidden until all are in).
2. Each group's camera, posting stills to a shared wall.
3. Each student's role card in a role-play, readable only by them.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

Everything here works in any room where students have phones and a shared network or mobile data, if the game is deployed rather than on a laptop [5].

### Digitally

Online, the phone stays the private screen beside the call; on a Mac, an iPhone can also serve as the overhead camera through Desk View [6].
