# Overhead cameras

## Definition

A camera on a mount above a table, lens pointed straight down, so that the table's surface fills the frame. In a studio it is one input among the switcher's cameras; at a desk it can be a document camera, a webcam on an arm or a phone.

## Description

What the LL has, from the record:

- **Four overhead cameras, from Nov 16, 2026.** "Studio: Blue and Jordan set up four overhead cameras today. A significant transformation to the studio" (voice agent's record, 17:07) [1]. Marlon's typed post that day (13:23) explains why: the Astro course "needs to have 4 overhead cameras for its Science Explainer video activity. This is an LL Mechanic that we aren't set up for right now ... we need to get this up and running (and once we do we should use it for more than just that one session)" [1]. The ASTRON 98 workshop is tonight, Nov 17, 18:00 to 20:30 [9].
- **An overhead on the switcher before that.** A workshop plan routes the classroom TV to "the OH, which i think is camera 2" [3].
- **What comes next (tasks, Nov 17):** "an inventory of the cameras", "always-on positions that are predictable and reliable, with cameras mounted on the truss in both locations", "tabletop or truss-top cameras across the hall", and "think through the standard lenses and cameras we want to have out in the actual studio spaces" [2]. Week 10's task list adds "XY positioning for the overhead camera" [4]; the Robotic Camera Handoff of Nov 6 prices an igus XY gantry and notes "No page approves inverted mounting" [5].

Models, lenses and exact positions of the four cameras are not in the record I read.

## How to

1. **Mount** above the table on the truss or an arm; if a mount hangs a camera upside down, add "a separate safety line either way" [5].
2. **Light** evenly: "Even, soft lighting ... minimize shadows" [6].
3. **Route** to a switcher input and from there to the room's screen [3]; for a browser app, choose the camera by name and turn mirroring off ("pick the overhead camera (Mirror off)") [7].
4. **Frame** the table's working area, "Full object plus slight border for context" [6].

## Why to

Marlon, Nov 16, 13:23 (typed): four overheads are a mechanic "that we frequently use for various multi-station gamified learning activities" [1]. The uses are written up in [The overhead view](../ll/overhead-view.md).

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** where the four cameras live when no class needs them.

1. Always on at four fixed tables, each feeding the switcher and a recorder, so any session can use them without setup (his "always-on positions" [2]).
2. Two fixed, two rolling, moved to whichever tables a session uses.
3. One per station for station rotations, with the tables labeled by camera number.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

A phone on a stand or arm above the desk, joined to the room's display through a video call or a cable; or the room's document camera, where it has one. (We suggest these; the LL has not tested them for this.)

### Digitally

On a Mac with an iPhone 11 or later (not iPhone 16e or SE), Apple's Desk View shows the desk from above in a video call [8]. A webcam on an arm, pointed down, works in any browser app that lets you choose the camera [7].
