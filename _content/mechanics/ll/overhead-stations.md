# Four overhead cameras, one per station

## Definition

Several tables in one room, each under its own overhead camera, so that groups work on paper at the same time and each table is a separate live feed: for the room's screen, a switcher, a recording, or a model reading the table.

## Description

Marlon, Nov 16, 13:23 (typed post): "we have Astro course coming in--needs to have 4 overhead cameras for its Science Explainer video activity. This is an LL Mechanic that we aren't set up for right now, but one that we frequently use for various multi-station gamified learning activities, so we need to get this up and running (and once we do we should use it for more than just that one session)" [1]. That afternoon: "Studio: Blue and Jordan set up four overhead cameras today. A significant transformation to the studio" (voice agent's record, 17:07) [1]. The ASTRON 98 Science Explainer Video Workshop is Nov 17, 18:00 to 20:30 [2]; in fall 2025 the course's plan was "a workshop for students to produce a 2-min science explainer video on their research as a final project", tested "on LLUFs before deploying" [3].

Before this week, the record shows the pattern with one camera shared or rotated: SCRB 111, Sept 16, 2025, where "Each group deconstructed and translated that section into a paper prototype + overhead camera for an explainer video segment" [4][3], and the station-rotation workshops whose paper station uses the overhead [5]. Whether earlier sessions ran several overheads at once is not in what I read; Marlon's "frequently use" says they have [1].

## How to

From the record, and what is still open:

1. **One camera per table,** each framed on its table's working area (see [The overhead view](overhead-view.md) for mounting and light).
2. **Each camera to its own switcher input and recorder.** Week 7's plan for the rack: "at least four and potentially seven cameras running into the ATEM, and the ATEM's outs running into a HyperDeck" [7].
3. **Make positions permanent.** Marlon, Nov 17: "always-on positions that are predictable and reliable, with cameras mounted on the truss in both locations" [6].
4. **Label tables by camera,** so a group's footage is findable afterwards. (Our suggestion.)

How tonight's ASTRON 98 session uses the four, and how it goes, will be in the week's record after the session; this entry should be updated from it.

## Why to

From Marlon: "various multi-station gamified learning activities" [1], and "we should use it for more than just that one session" [1]. On Nov 16 the record of his direction for the LLUFs has "Today could try some camera-based AR and sensor-based things" [8].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** what four feeds make possible beyond one.

1. Parallel groups, one showcase: every group works at once and the host switches the room's screen to each table in turn for share-out.
2. A game across four tables: each table is a team or a territory, and a model reads all four boards from their feeds or stills.
3. Four angles on one table: the same activity seen from four sides for a documentary record.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Each group's phone on a stand above its table, all joined to one video call shown on the projector (a gallery of tables). (Not tested.)
2. No live feeds: each group photographs its table at set times and posts to a shared folder or the class Slack.

### Digitally

Each group shares its own overhead (a phone or Desk View) in a breakout room, then into the main room for the share-out. (Our suggestion; not tested.)
