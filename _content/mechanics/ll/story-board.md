# The story board

## Definition

A large vertical board onto which the week's physical traces (images, drawings, notes, prints) are gathered as one story, which people then tell to a camera standing in front of it; the recordings go into an archive for storytelling.

## Description

Marlon, Nov 13, 2026 (dictated): "all term long, the Learning Lab undergrad fellows, or LLUFs, have worked with Jordan to collect all the materials, images, drawings, notes that people create in the Learning Lab studio over the course of the week, and then put those together onto one of the large four-by-eight vertical whiteboards that we have in the studio as a kind of story of the week. And then we stand in front of the board and record ourselves talking about it. And that's one of the core mechanics of storytelling" [1]. There are three boards, rotated; when one is retired, "between two and seven" of its stories go onto "a large gray wall next to the boards", some as "pasted constellations or little micro installations" [1].

From the term's record: week 3, Sept 25: "Every week we do a giant whiteboard installation. Get the next one up" [2]; week 7: "The origin stories board is probably the best-looking one we have" (for an admissions shoot) [3]; week 9: "the week 6 studies and skills board looks done on the dev board; move it up to the main board" [4]; week 10's plan to "have each week in progress on MK's whiteboards and door" [5]. On Nov 17, 05:58: "we'll continue to do is just work on the boards ... work on the boards as the key workflow ... for installations", with "the digital piece of it we want to completely automate" (voice agent's record) [6].

## How to

From Marlon's account [1]: through the week, collect what people make in the studio; print the digital things; arrange them on a 4×8 board as a story; stand in front of it and record; archive the recording; rotate the board; move a few stories to the gray wall when it is retired.

## Why to

Marlon: "a core storytelling mechanic", "putting together tactile kind of paper, printed, artworky representations of what we do during a given week on the board, and then talking about them and then having that get into a kind of digital archive that we can use for storytelling" [1].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** the classroom use of the board.

1. A course's week on a board: students assemble and present the week's work, a portfolio review standing up.
2. The board as an exam: students talk in front of a board they built, a form of the oral exam the week's concept list points to.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

A bulletin board, a wall with tape, or a whiteboard, and a phone on a tripod to record the telling. (Our suggestion.)

### Digitally

A shared online board of the week's images, presented on screen and recorded in a video call. (Our suggestion.)
