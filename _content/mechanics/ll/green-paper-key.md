# Green paper as a green screen

## Definition

Chroma keying on a tabletop: a sheet of green paper lies under a camera, the switcher or software removes the green, and whatever sits on the paper (a drawing, a cut-out figure, a hand) appears over another picture, video or live feed.

## Description

The studio's green screen is a wall and floor a person stands on; green paper brings the same trick down to a table, where a group can work on it together under a camera. Marlon, Nov 17, 07:13 (voice agent's record), names "using green paper as a green screen... and the various things that makes possible" among the LL's mechanics, and at 07:26: "the green screen (and green paper) — chroma keying — is literally the way we connect and layer multiple video feeds" [1].

He had already described it on Aug 8, 2026, in a dictation for TDM 155AI (machine transcript, quoted as transcribed): "how our use of green screen to largely work with horizontally ... green paper is a misuse of the tool, but it creates all these opportunities for creativity and collaboration that are not typically the name of the game when you think of digital compositing" [2].

Keying in the LL's sessions, from the record:

- **HUM 2, Oct 21, 2025.** The prep checklist sets the switcher's keyers, "usual chromas on 1 and 2 / lumas on 3 and 4", loads "card stock ... for paper dolls", and during class puts "paper dolls on cam once they exist" [3].
- **The puppetry residency for TDM 129B** (April 8–10; the year is not in the note). The LLUFs were asked to stage and photograph "green screen layering" and "multi-plane camera rigs" for a visual guide [4]. The paper dolls are made from green screen footage: "Take footage of yourself on the green screen in the main studio", cut the still out in Photoshop, "Print - and be sure to print on thick!" [5].
- **On the full green screen stage:** AFVS 173F, spring 2025, students "embodied plant movements on a green-screen stage"; TDM 119B, spring 2025, a "full-body long shot on our green screen stage" with AI-generated backdrops; SLAVIC/TDM 121, spring 2024, poses on the green screen read by computer vision and fed to Stable Diffusion [6]. The Greatest Hits report (a model's synthesis of the HackMD record) adds "Luma key effects for creative compositing" in AFVS 173F [7].
- **The luma key relative.** Marlon's studio notes from 2022–23 list "invert paper + black marker for luma key over subject" among the studio's moves [8]; on Nov 5, 2026, he asked for TDM 98's challenge cards to pop up on "fullscreen black ... we'll run that through atem as luma key" (built; "Not yet tested on a real second display or the ATEM") [10].

Green paper under a camera in a named session: not found in the record I read. Marlon's Aug 8 dictation speaks of it as the LL's practice [2].

## How to

In the studio, from the record and the studio's routing:

1. **Camera.** An overhead camera above the table (see [The overhead view](overhead-view.md)), framed so the green paper fills the picture.
2. **Keyer.** Route the camera to a switcher input with a chroma key set; HUM 2's checklist keeps chroma keys on inputs 1 and 2 and luma keys on 3 and 4 [3]. In software, OBS's Chroma Key filter "Removes any instances of a given color from the Source ... Use this filter for green screens", with the key color green by default [11].
3. **Background.** Put the picture, video or second camera that should show through behind the keyed layer.
4. **Material.** Draw or cut out on non-green paper; lay it on the green. Card stock for figures that will be handled [3][5].

What goes wrong, from the record: anything green on the table disappears. The 2023 PSY 980T plan sets out the art supplies with "(avoid green paper )" [9], and by the same logic (ours, not the record's) green markers and green sleeves vanish too. We expect uneven light and shadows on the paper to make the key patchy, as with any key; the record's rule for top-down shots is even, soft light (see the overhead view's how-to). A luma key, the alternative, needs strictly black and white: TDM 98's stage cards mark which looks are "Luma-safe" [10].

## Why to

From the record:

- **Collaboration and play on a table, not a stage.** Marlon, Aug 8, 2026: green paper "creates all these opportunities for creativity and collaboration that are not typically the name of the game when you think of digital compositing" [2].
- **Connecting and layering feeds.** Marlon, Nov 17, 07:26 (voice agent's record): chroma keying "is literally the way we connect and layer multiple video feeds"; 07:26 also: "So green plays a role to connect, overlay, layer things" [1].
- **Puppetry and paper figures.** The residency guide was to show "TDM students ... which techniques to use for their own projects" [4].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** what to say green paper is for in a teaching or presenting context.

1. Putting yourself in the source: students' cut-outs or hands placed over the film still, map or diagram they are analyzing, as a step toward a video essay.
2. A presenter's layer: a speaker draws on green paper and the drawing appears over their slides or over their own camera.
3. A shared table scene: several people move paper figures over one projected background, a puppet show made by the group.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Green paper, a phone on a stand pointed down, and a laptop running OBS with its Chroma Key filter [11], shown on the room's projector. (We suggest this; not tested by the LL.)
2. Skip keying: white paper and a thick black marker with a luma key or a "multiply" layer, which needs no green and keys a drawing cleanly (after Marlon's 2022–23 note [8]; not tested in a classroom).

### Digitally

- OBS (free, Windows, macOS and Linux) keys any camera with its Chroma Key filter [11]; its output can be shared to a call.
- Video-call apps have their own virtual-background green screen settings; we could not confirm a vendor page for Zoom's while writing this, so none is cited.
