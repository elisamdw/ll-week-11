# The studio screen as a shared readout

## Definition

A game or activity puts its public information (a reveal, a score, a timer, the board) on one large screen that the whole room reads at once, while each person's private information, if any, stays on their own device or cards.

## Description

From the record:

- **Guess the category.** Marlon, Nov 16, 05:58 (voice agent's record): "A moment to bring back the guessing game from an earlier era of our LLM-driven games ... come up with a category and 15 terms in it, and display them one by one, slowly ... on the screen in the studio, and people have to try to guess the category. The fewer words it takes you to guess the category, the more you win" [1]. The card proposes a page with "huge type, one term at a time on a timer" and a host key to stop the reveal [2]. The earlier LL game itself has not been found [2].
- **The game show board.** EXPOS 40's *Are You Serious?!* runs a countdown, a buzzer and music on the screen from the booth; the fellows could "Play the board on the studio screen" [3].
- **The stage window.** TDM 155AI, Nov 11, 2026: the class app was built so that "every mechanic fires a cue or sets a parameter on a full-screen `/stage` window for the projector"; its use in class is not yet confirmed [4].
- **The week's game in hotseat.** `/game/play/<version>` is played "hotseat on one screen" [6].

## How to

1. **Full screen, type readable from the back** [2]; the accessibility research asks for "Large text at distance" and a visual for every meaningful sound [5].
2. **Host controls apart from the display** (a key to stop, a separate launcher) [2][4].
3. **A timer the host can change or turn off** [3][5].

## Why to

From the record: the reveal makes the whole room guess together and scores the speed of an insight [1]; Marlon frames the guessing game as one of "our LLM-driven games" [1]; the card adds that played on the week's own material it "is also review, which is the learning-game point" (a proposal) [2].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** the shared readout's lead use in teaching.

1. Review as a game (decks from the course's terms) [2].
2. A live tally of the room's answers, shown only after everyone has answered on their phones.
3. A stage for student presentations with cues they fire themselves [4].

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

The room's projector and the instructor's laptop show the same page [2][3].

### Digitally

Screen-share the page in a video call; buzzing in becomes a chat message or a reaction. (Our suggestion.)
