# Station rotation

## Definition

A session format: the room is divided into a few stations, each set up for one kind of work (often paper, equipment and software), and small groups move from station to station on a fixed clock until everyone has done each.

## Description

The Greatest Hits report (a model's synthesis of the LL's 2021–25 HackMD notes) calls the "three-station rotation format (15-20 minutes each)" the LL's most successful pattern, with three archetypes: "Conceptual/Low-Tech", "Equipment/Hands-On" and "Digital Tools" [1]. The LL's own overview describes multi-station and multi-zone workshops for AFVS 173F, SOC-STD 98MR, NEC 101 ("rotating teams") and language faculty, among others [8].

This term, from the record:

- **AI Open Studio re-run of TDM 155AI class 1, Sept 10:** "keep it at three: the table, the computers for the 'I Want' song, and the dark corner for the written self-portrait demo" [2].
- **TDM 155AI class 3, Sept 23:** "four stations (studio lights, cameras, camera obscura and projection, desk video kit)" [3].
- **Week 6 skills class, Oct 14:** "Video generation with AI / Block printing" among three stations [4].
- **ENGL 189VG session 1, Oct 26:** "Students rotate through three stations. Each station mixes the digital, the physical, and AI" [5].
- **TDM 155AI, Nov 11 (planned):** MediaPipe and button launching "kept apart in the rotation 'to isolate the different things students are learning to do'" [6].
- **Tomorrow, Nov 18:** Marlon, 05:12 (voice agent's record): the Stafford game "could be one of the stations" [7].

## How to

From the Greatest Hits report's replication guide [1]: an introduction (5 minutes), three rounds of 20, a closing reflection; one staff member per station plus a coordinator; "station instruction cards (brief bullet points)" at each; documentation from the overhead camera afterwards.

## Why to

The Greatest Hits report's reasons (a synthesis): it "Divides class into manageable groups (5-8 students per station)", "Creates movement and energy in the room", "Allows for differentiated instruction", and students "experience full pipeline: concept → creation → refinement" [1]. Week 10's: to "isolate the different things students are learning to do" [6].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** what to say about rotation vs. choice.

1. Fixed rotation: everyone does every station (the classic format).
2. Open stations: students choose and stay, as at an open house, with the host moving people on.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

Tables as stations, a printed card at each, a timer on the projector; the instructor as the one roaming staff member. (Our suggestion.)

### Digitally

Breakout rooms as stations, rotated on a clock, each with its instruction card pinned in the room's chat. (Our suggestion.)
