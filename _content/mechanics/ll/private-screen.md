# The secret on your phone

## Definition

Each player's phone works as a private screen: it shows information only that player may see (a hand of cards, a secret role, a team, starting coordinates), while what everyone knows is on the table or the shared screen.

## Description

Marlon, Nov 17, 06:01 (voice agent's record): "the dealing of a hand, or something that's metaphorically a hand — the starting coordinates of some kind of game, your identity in a playful guessing game, your teammates — a revealing of asymmetrical information. Clearly this could just be done with phones; people play those Jackbox-type games where they input codes and share that kind of information... but the idea of a card for each position around the table, people tapping their phones there and then seeing whatever the secret information is on their phone, is probably a good initial proof of concept" [1]. The day before: "Things involving asymmetrical information on the phone: good" [2]; and "Asymmetrical information, a key mechanic all week" [3].

Not yet run as a phone game; the proof of concept is for this week [1].

## How to

As proposed in the record: one tagged card per seat; each player taps the card at their seat and sees their secret [1]. Without tags: each player opens the game's link and joins with a code [1]. Phones on the same network as the laptop, or the game deployed [4].

What goes wrong: a player can show their phone to a neighbour, as with any hand of cards (our note); and a tag URL can be loaded without the card [4].

## Why to

From Marlon: "a revealing of asymmetrical information" [1].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** what the private screen does in teaching.

1. Roles in a role-play or simulation (the Stafford game's players each holding a different brief).
2. A private answer before a public reveal, so no one anchors on the first voice.
3. Different evidence for each student, so the room has to talk to assemble the whole.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

Paper does it: role cards dealt face down (our note). Phones with a join code do it without tags [1].

### Digitally

In a video call, each student opens their own link; the shared screen is the call's screen share. (Our suggestion.)
