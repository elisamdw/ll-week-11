# Cards

## Definition

A set of printed cards, each holding one thing (a term, an idea, a role, a record, a challenge), which people draw, sort, deal, rank, lay out and link on a table, so that a body of material can be handled one piece at a time and rearranged.

## Description

Marlon, Nov 17, 07:13 (voice agent's record), in his list of LL mechanics: "Just cards" [1]. A card is small enough to hold one idea and to move by hand, so a group can sort, sequence, group or deal a whole body of material on a table and see the arrangement at once. The same deck can be read, played or photographed.

Sessions where the LL used them, from the record:

- **SOC-STD 98to (William Stafford), Sept 28, 2026: "Database construction with index cards."** Marlon's account: "A way of thinking about the material of the course. Comes out of Manovich's text on databases versus narratives." Groups brainstormed databases of the course and went in different directions; some reached "a database of infrastructural elements, 'which is what we anticipated'", and the next session is to "do the database of infrastructure, because we're going to build a game based on it" [2]. That game is Monday's (Nov 23) Stafford workshop.
- **The TF AI-comfort card sort** (Beyond the Chatbox use case, cited Sept 24, 2026): "12 table photos → CSV → chart" [3].
- **ENGL 189VG, session 1, Oct 26, 2026.** The story station: "You'll design a short dialogue tree using colored cards" [4]; Marlon's plan for the stations: "story dev in cards and unity" [5]. How the session went is not yet recorded [5].
- **The mic taste test** (FRENCH 30 and 40, and others): students "use adjective cards to describe quality: 'warm,' 'crisp,' 'muddy,' 'clear'"; and in entrepreneurship sessions, "moving storyboards with colored cards" [6]. (Both from the Greatest Hits report, a model's synthesis of the LL's HackMD record.)
- **TDM 98, Nov 5, 2026.** Forty challenge cards and a game-mechanics page; at Marlon's request a drawn card pops up full screen on black to be keyed over video ("we'll run that through atem as luma key"; built, not yet tested on the ATEM) [7].
- **Week 11, Nov 13 to 17, 2026.** The week's ideas and cards in `_content/ideas/` and `_content/cards/`, printed in cut-out sheets [11]. On Nov 16 Marlon proposed the physical card as the game object whose properties live digitally, read with a phone tap (see [The tap](tap-to-move.md)) [8].

## How to

1. **One thing per card.** Write or print one term, idea, role or record on each. Marlon's print spec for this week's cards: "a nice chunky 900 pt headline/title with a 500 weight descript in Inter", and for the size, "5.5" wide (a column of 11x17 vertically, with varying heights depending on how much text" [9].
2. **Print and cut.** "He prints on 11x17 (and Letter), cuts the cards, and uses them on tables and walls" [10]. This repo's reader prints cut-out sheets on Letter at `/ideas/print` (`?per=4|6|8`) and the cards at `/cards/print` [11].
3. **Give a move.** A deck is only a deck until the activity says what to do with it: draw three, rank, group, build a tree, deal one each. The ENGL 189VG station gives the move and a question ("Mapping out the dialogue, can you get the player to where you want them to go?") [4].
4. **Capture the arrangement.** Photograph the table from above when the arrangement is done; the card sort became data that way [3] (see [The overhead view](overhead-view.md)).

What goes wrong, from the record: too many cards to print ("There may be too many to print, but that's ok--we're learning", Marlon, Nov 15 [10]); printed cards drift from their fuller digital text, so the week chose "abbreviated print cards that match fuller digital text" and no QR codes [10]; color cards under a keyed camera key out if they are green (see [Green paper](green-paper-key.md)).

## Why to

From the record:

- **To think about the material as data.** Stafford, Sept 28: the index cards were "A way of thinking about the material of the course", leading to "how things change when you start designing something that's more spatial, more schema-ish" (Marlon) [2].
- **To turn a room's opinions into a chart.** The card sort's photos became a CSV and a chart [3].
- **To give students words for what they hear.** The adjective cards in the taste test [6].
- **To make a game object.** Marlon, Nov 16: the card is "the game object", its properties live digitally, and every player "can see different things about these cards" [8].

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

Index cards and markers, or a printed sheet cut into cards, need nothing a classroom lacks. The LL's print views make Letter sheets of four, six or eight cards [11]. A phone photo from above keeps the result [3].

### Digitally

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. The deck on a shared board: this repo's `/ideas/board` and `/cards` views (whiteboard and node graph) [11], run by the instructor on the room's screen.
2. A shared online whiteboard where each student drags their own copy of the cards (any whiteboard app; none tested by the LL for this).
3. Physical cards with an NFC tag each, so a phone tap opens the card's digital side (the week's proof of concept; see [NFC tags](../technologies/nfc-tags.md)).
