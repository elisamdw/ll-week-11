# Paper prototype under the camera

## Definition

A first version of a video, a visual argument or an explainer made with paper (printed stills, cut-outs, drawings, text) and recorded or photographed under an overhead camera, before any editing software is opened.

## Description

The Greatest Hits report (a model's synthesis of the LL's 2021–25 HackMD notes) calls paper prototyping "standard first step" from 2022–23 on, in the three-station video essay workshop and elsewhere, with a "Standard Workflow": sketch on paper, "Arrange printed images, text, and drawings on overhead camera", photograph iterations, pick the strongest, then "Transition to digital tools" [1]. On the record: SCRB 111, Sept 16, 2025, "a paper prototype + overhead camera for an explainer video segment" [2]; HUM 7, Oct 7, 2025 (plan), "cut-up film stills under an overhead camera" for "a short mock video essay" [3]; SLAVIC/TDM 121, spring 2024, "annotated printed frame-by-frame stills ... using arts supplies to make claims about ballet the way they might in something like a video essay" [4].

## How to

From the report's replication guide: "Print 20-30 film stills from course-relevant films (color, 8.5×11)"; "markers, colored paper, scissors, tape, glue sticks"; "Set up overhead camera at Station 1"; afterwards "Upload overhead camera documentation to shared folder" [1].

## Why to

The report's reasons (a synthesis): it "Removes 'I don't know the software' barrier", gives "Rapid iteration (draw, photograph, adjust = 2 minutes)", and keeps the "Focus on ideas and composition, not technical execution" [1]. HUM 7's plan: it "rehearses the same analytical moves they'll use in their written papers" [3].

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:** whether the entry presents paper first as a rule for every LL media workshop, or as one station among three.

1. A rule: paper before software, always.
2. A station: one of the three, alongside equipment and software.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

Printed stills, scissors and a phone above the desk; the photos are the prototype. (Our suggestion, after the report's workflow [1].)

### Digitally

Students arrange the stills in any slide or drawing app and export each version. (Our suggestion.)
