# LLUF Prompt: Deep Dives on Mechanics and Technologies

### Background

Week 11 is games and interaction. This week the LL is building a reference of **mechanics**: the moves a game is made of (hidden information, turn order, trading), and the moves the LL itself makes again and again in the studio (the overhead camera, green paper as a green screen, cards, 11x17 paper as a map or a place setting, concept maps). Alongside them go the **technologies** that make those moves possible, like webcam hand tracking or an NFC card tapped with a phone. Each entry says what the thing is, how to do it, why you would do it in a class or a presentation, and how to do it outside the LL, in a regular classroom or on a laptop at home.

Agents can write the encyclopedia part. What they cannot write is what happened when you tried it. Marlon, November 17 (the voice agent's record of him): "LLUF-created deep dives on mechanics and technologies they've experimented with."

### The Pitch

You have already made things this term with the LL's kit: instruments you play with your hands in front of a webcam, games on paper and on phones, setups under the overhead camera. Pick one, and write the page that would let someone else do it tomorrow, here or anywhere else. Bring photos.

### The Full Prompt

1. **Pick one thing from the [pick list](pick-list.md)**, or propose your own: anything you have tried with your own hands. Pairs are fine. If someone is already writing an entry on it, read theirs first and write what yours adds.
2. **Copy the [template](template.md)** and answer its questions from what you did. Plain sentences, in your own words. A short answer from experience is worth more than a long one from the web. If you looked something up, say where.
3. **Take stills.** The setup from a few steps back, the thing in use, and one close-up of whatever makes it work. Photograph hands, tables and screens rather than faces, and ask before anyone appears in a picture.
4. **Try the home version if you can.** Even ten minutes on a phone or a laptop tells you more than a guess. If you did not try it, say so.
5. **Hand it in** (see Logistics). Your words are saved as you wrote them. Someone may come back with a question or two at the foot of the page.

See [the worked example](examples/hand-tracking.md) for the length and the tone. It was written by Claude, from the LL's records, so its "what happened" section is secondhand; yours should not be.
