# Turn order

## Definition

The rule for who acts when: players act one at a time, in a fixed or changing sequence, so each move can be seen and answered before the next.

On GM's card: "Players act one at a time in a set sequence." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Chess](../games/chess.md)**: White moves first and the players alternate, one move each. [2]
- **[Catan](../games/catan.md)**: In setup each player places a settlement in turn, then a second one in reverse order, so the player who placed first places last. [3]
- **[Civilization](../games/civilization.md)**: Every player and computer civilization moves its units during its turn, and the world changes only when the turn ends. [4]
- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: The AI's phase comes first in every round, then upkeep, then each faction acts in turn. [5]

**Shows it** (GM's card): Chess [1]

**With AI** (HI): Where the AI sits in the turn order decides whether humans react to it or anticipate it. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: every version (the AI's phase, then each faction in turn) [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (GM's card): Tell a story one word at a time around the table. [1]

**Build it** (GM's card): A cue list on `/launch` where Go passes the turn and lights the next player's name. [1]

## Why to

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Turn order is the easiest rule to change in a class remix: who goes first, and does the AI go before or after the humans?
2. Turn-based play gives time to talk between moves, which is what a discussion-based class needs.
