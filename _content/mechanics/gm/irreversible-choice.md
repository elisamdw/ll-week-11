# Irreversible choice

## Definition

A decision that cannot be taken back, so its consequences shape the rest of the game.

On GM's card: "A decision that cannot be undone shapes the rest of the game." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Mass Effect](../games/mass-effect.md)**: On Virmire the player must leave one of two squad members to die, and that choice carries into the sequels. [2]
- **[Pandemic Legacy: Season 1](../games/pandemic-legacy.md)**: Players put stickers on the board and tear up cards, so a choice in one game changes every game after it. [3]
- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: Once the turn card is drawn, the AI's proposals pass without a vote for the rest of the game. [4][5]

**Shows it** (GM's card): *The Oregon Trail* [1]

**With AI** (HI): Some AI decisions can't be reversed: once it stops asking, it never asks again. [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: the turn card in Data Center and The Committee cannot be undone [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (GM's card): Before the scene, each player tears up one of three cards. That option is gone for good. [1]

**Build it** (GM's card): One choice locks a branch for good; `/stage` shows the road not taken. [1]

## Why to

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. Make students commit in writing before they see the outcome, then discuss the commitment.
2. Use it to contrast games (where you can restart) with policy (where you cannot).
