# Timer and countdown

## Definition

A limit on time, kept by a clock or by a number of rounds or cards, after which play stops or something happens. It makes waiting cost something and forces decisions.

On GM's card: "A clock that forces a decision before it runs out." [1]

## Description

In games:

- **[Boggle](../games/boggle.md)**: A sand timer of about three minutes gives every player the same short window to find words. [2]
- **[Pandemic](../games/pandemic.md)**: The player deck works as a clock: if a player has to draw two cards and fewer than two are left, everyone loses. [3]
- **[Data Center](../games/data-center.md)**: Ten rounds (twelve in The Committee) set the length of play; the humans survive if the AI is still under half the map when they run out. [4][5]

**Shows it** (GM's card): *Boggle* (the sand timer) [1]

**With AI** (HI): A countdown to the AI's endgame that players can delay by paying (Bogost's Cowpocalypse clock). [1]

**In our game** (Nov 14 rulesets): yes: a fixed number of rounds in every version [1]

## How to

**Try it in two minutes** (GM's card): Describe your project in thirty seconds while someone counts down aloud. [1]

**Build it** (GM's card): A big countdown on `/stage`, started from a MIDI pad. [1]

## Why to

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A visible countdown makes a station rotation into a game: everyone sees how long is left and plans for it.
2. Compare a timer in seconds with a timer in rounds to show the difference between real-time and turn-based play.

## Home game version

### In a regular classroom

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A sand timer or a phone timer on the document camera.
2. A deck of cards as the clock: when the last card is drawn, the activity ends.

### Digitally

**Proposed, for Marlon to choose:**

1. A full-screen countdown on the projector.
2. A round counter in a shared doc that the facilitator advances.
