## What they share

**One game, two versions.** Both classes play the week's AI game: TDM as two theories of AI side by side ([Play two theories of AI](tdm155ai/play-two-theories-of-ai.md): *Data Center* and *Normal Technology*), the Stafford workshop as [Play Data Center](stafford/play-data-center.md) with one faction each. That makes Wednesday a dry run for Monday.

**The same verbs.** Twenty-one mechanics from `mechanics.csv` appear on cards for both classes. The ones that carry the most:

- `hidden-information`, `faction-card`, `house-rules`, `externality-card`, `resource-management`: what a player knows, who they play for, and what a move costs someone else.
- `treacherous-turn`, `harden-node`, `voting-and-the-collective-decision`: the AI game's own mechanics.
- `incremental-growth`, `automate-this`, `delegation`, `come-back-later`: *Universal Paperclips* and games that play themselves.
- `unplay-reskin-rewrite`, `any-map-test`, `not-modeled`: remixing, and asking what the game leaves out.

**The same readings.** Fourteen readings are cited on both sides, among them Bogost's *Persuasive Games*, Nguyen's *Games: Agency as Art*, Lantz's *Universal Paperclips*, Crawford's *Atlas of AI*, Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes", Flanagan's *Critical Play*, Manovich's "Database as Symbolic Form" and Suber's *Nomic*. Bogost against Nguyen ([Agency or argument](tdm155ai/agency-or-argument.md)) is Marlon's tension for the week, and the Stafford debrief ([What the mechanic argues](stafford/what-the-mechanic-argues.md)) asks the same question in William's terms.

## Where one activity can serve two audiences

Each chain below starts with a prototype the LLUFs could playtest early in the week, becomes a station or activity in Wednesday's class, and becomes a stop in William's workshop. The order lets each room improve the thing before the next one uses it.

1. **Prices that move.** PL's prototype `economy/prices-that-move`, and `hybrid/monopoly-with-live-rents` for a printed board. LLUFs playtest it ([Playtest one change](llufs/playtest-one-change.md)) and run it on paper ([A paper market with prices on a phone](llufs/tabletop-phone-market.md)) → TDM [Prices that move, at the table](tdm155ai/prices-that-move-at-the-table.md) (Session B) → Stafford [Every resource names a place](stafford/every-resource-names-a-place.md), where each price lands on a named place, and a choice in [Remix one mechanic](stafford/remix-one-mechanic.md). What changes between rooms: TDM asks what moving prices do to play; Stafford asks who pays them.
2. **Photo to game state.** PL's `hybrid/photo-to-game-state` (tested only on example pins). LLUFs compare what a photo shows with what a tap log records ([The board and the log](llufs/the-board-and-the-log.md)) and set up the overhead camera or a phone on a stand → TDM [Photo to game state, then read a save file](tdm155ai/photo-to-game-state.md) (Session A) → Stafford [Read the game state](stafford/read-the-game-state.md) and [Cards become the map](stafford/cards-become-the-map.md), which use the students' own Sept 28 index cards ([The map is their database](stafford/the-map-is-their-database.md)). The research card both rest on: [A game is data](tdm155ai/a-game-is-data.md).
3. **Universal Paperclips and games that play themselves.** PL's `economy/paperclip-loop`, sized so a student can change it in fifteen minutes. TDM [Games that play themselves](tdm155ai/games-that-play-themselves.md) → Stafford [Play Paperclips and log the easy moments](stafford/play-paperclips-and-log-the-easy-moments.md), the homework before the workshop, read with Winner and Rabinbach ([Reverse adaptation and the human motor](stafford/reverse-adaptation-and-the-human-motor.md)).
4. **Coupling and cascade.** PL's `economy/cascade` (from SW's request), with `economy/leaks` for power and water whose costs land elsewhere on the map. Stafford [Knock over the network](stafford/knock-over-the-network.md) is ten minutes of paper; the same deck could be one of TDM's [Change one card](tdm155ai/change-one-card.md) draws, and LLUFs could test whether the paper version and the screen version teach the same thing.
5. **The AI takes a seat.** PL's `players/committee` (an AI advisor right eight times in ten), `players/the-phone-plays-the-ai`, and `players/the-intent` (one player is secretly the AI). TDM [Claude takes a seat](tdm155ai/claude-takes-a-seat.md) (Session B) → Stafford, the advisor as a remix choice. *The committee* is short enough (about two minutes a run) to open either class.
6. **Remix one mechanic.** TDM [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](tdm155ai/remix-with-claude-code.md) and Stafford [Remix one mechanic](stafford/remix-one-mechanic.md) are one activity with two rules about AI: in TDM students make the change with Claude Code; in William's room Claude Code is on the screen as a thing to examine, or the change is a house rule on paper. The worked remixes TDM students make on Wednesday could be the examples William's students read on Monday, if Marlon wants student work shown (it would need their permission, and no names).
