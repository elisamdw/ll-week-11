**Marlon, Nov 11:** "Contingency thinking. It could be that we just create it all as an online async experience for the students." And on Nov 14: async play "yes ultimately."

**What students do:**

1. Eight students in four faction pairs. Each pair has a private thread or chat for planning.
2. Each morning whoever runs the board (William, a TF, or an LL fellow) posts a photo of the board and the AI's last move.
3. By midnight each faction posts its two actions as one short reply. The board-runner plays them in the morning, draws the AI's card, and posts the new photo.
4. Seven days, seven rounds. The AI's turn card is in the deck somewhere; nobody knows which day.
5. On workshop day (or in William's next class), the debrief card runs as written, with the week's photos as the record.

**What it teaches:** Long-turn play (Freeciv's LongTurn games run one turn a day) feels like living with infrastructure: you check in, it has changed without you. Fizek's playing at a distance, lived for a week.

**Prepare:** One copy of the game set up somewhere it can stay (or one laptop), a shared thread, and a board-runner. Nothing needs a server: the photo is the shared state. A screen version with shared state for several phones would need PL's shared-state helper and a deploy, which is Marlon's call.

**Try it:** Run three days with the LL's own fellows first and time the board-runner's job.

**Could go wrong:** Students miss a deadline; a faction that doesn't post takes no actions (that's a mechanic too). The board-runner job is real work; William should not end up doing it unless he wants to.
