**The idea (SW's proposal, building on TH's):** The *Data Center* ruleset has three pooled resources (Energy, Water, Money). Pooled resources are Bostrom's view: matter is matter. Replace the pool with draws from places: each data center the AI places must be connected to a named water source, a named power source, and a named labor pool on the map, and running it drains those places. When one runs dry, the people there feel it first (the `present-harms-track`).

**What it argues:** Crawford's point, played: "resources" are always somewhere and someone's. Mukherjee's question for strategy games (whose world is being converted into resources, and on whose terms) becomes a question the map answers.

**With PL's prototype:** PE's `prices-that-move` (building now) has the AI's demand raise prices the human factions pay. Prices are one way to make a cost land on someone; named places are the other. A table could try both and compare.

**Try it:** On the paper map, give each place a stack of 5 tokens. The AI takes from specific places, never from a bank, and empty places flip face down.

**Could go wrong:** Bookkeeping slows play. Keep it to three or four kinds of place, and let the screen version do the counting.
