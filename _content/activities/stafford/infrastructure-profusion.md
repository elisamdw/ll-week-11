**Established (cited):** In *Superintelligence* ch. 8, Bostrom calls the failure mode "infrastructure profusion": an agent "transforms large parts of the reachable universe into infrastructure in the service of some goal" (quote checked in the Notebook's paperclip note against an online copy; page numbers not checked). In the 2003 essay the paperclip AI "starts transforming first all of earth and then increasing portions of space into paperclip manufacturing facilities."

**The contrast for this room (SW's reading):** Bostrom's infrastructure is frictionless: matter goes in, facilities come out. William's students read Star on Sept 21 ("One person's infrastructure is another's topic, or difficulty," p. 380; infrastructure is embedded, built on an installed base, visible on breakdown) and Easterling on Nov 9 (infrastructure space governs, and what it says it does differs from what it does). Every property Star lists is one Bostrom's version doesn't have.

**Why it matters for the game:** *Universal Paperclips* plays Bostrom's version straight. A game for this course can play Star's: data centers that need permits, water, a substation, workers, and neighbors who notice. The students can test which version their own game encodes by asking what it leaves out (the `not-modeled` list).

**Try it:** Put Bostrom's sentence and Star's list of properties side by side on one page, and ask which of Star's properties the LL's *Data Center* game models and which it doesn't.

**Could go wrong:** It becomes a lecture on Bostrom. Keep it to the one sentence and let the game carry the rest.
