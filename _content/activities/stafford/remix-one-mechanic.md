**Marlon, Nov 14:** "The key thing is that the students need agency, and I'm not sure that playing an existing game we create is enough." And in the voice chat: "the design of the game is much, much more than the rules"; "The UI is Claude code itself."

**What students do:**

1. Each table picks one change from a printed menu (or invents one). The menu, each tied to a reading: prices that rise as the AI buys (PE's prototype); every resource drawn from a named place (Crawford); a workers' faction that can strike (Rabinbach); goals rewritten when the AI builds nearby (Winner); an AI advisor on the council that is right most of the time (PI's committee); the AI's next target shown to one player only (PI's readout); a cap on compute everyone must vote to raise; the AI as normal technology or superintelligence, by vote before play (the game's two rulesets); a rival lab racing the first, with a treaty as the only brake (PE's race).
2. **On screen:** the table tells Claude Code what they want, in plain words, and an LL facilitator runs it. The change goes into a new remix folder, so the original game stays intact. **On paper:** the table writes the change on a card as a house rule.
3. Play two rounds with the change.
4. One sentence: what does the game argue now that it didn't before?

**What it teaches:** Mechanics as procedural rhetoric (Bogost) and as the player's means of acting (Sicart, Schell's elements). Changing one and watching the outcome move is the clearest way to see a game as an argument.

**Prepare:** The game's `AGENTS.md` and worked remixes (GL's proposal, waiting on Marlon's go) so Claude knows where a change goes; test each menu item once. The paper route needs nothing but cards.

**Try it:** Without a laptop, the paper route runs from the menu alone.

**Could go wrong:** Claude takes minutes per change and the table waits; give the paper route to one table and the screen to the other, then swap. Under William's AI policy, Claude builds the change; the students' sentence about what it argues is theirs.
