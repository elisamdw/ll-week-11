**Established (William's syllabus, quoted):** "The core policy is that the use of generative or agentic AI in any form is prohibited for aid in analyzing, idea-generation, research, formatting, organizing, outlining, drafting, revising, or editing any work submitted in Social Studies courses." And, right after: "In this course, we will make varied uses of AI and work with AI artefacts and we will work on how to describe, explain, and justify their use in research."

**Marlon, Nov 14 (voice chat):** "The UI is Claude code itself." "We're definitely showing them under the hood."

**What follows for the workshop (SW's reading, for Marlon and William to check):**

- A game remixed with Claude Code in the room is an **AI artefact the class studies**, which the syllabus allows. Nothing Claude writes should go into a journal entry, the prospectus, or the final paper.
- Journal prompts after the workshop ask students to describe and justify what the AI did, in their own words; that is the use the syllabus names ("describe, explain, and justify").
- If a student takes the game home and wants to change it with Claude Code for their project, that is William's call under the Social Studies policy, not ours.

**Try it:** Put the two quoted sentences on the session card's first line so William sees the workshop was planned around his policy.

**Could go wrong:** A remix activity drifts into Claude writing students' analysis ("what does this mechanic argue?"). Keep Claude on building and the students on arguing.
