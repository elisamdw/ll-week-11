**Established (cited):** Crawford's *Atlas of AI* (2021) argues that AI is an extractive industry, following it back to mines, energy, water, and underpaid labor (ch. 1, "Earth," is starred in our Stafford reading set). Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes" (2015) reads the NSA's Utah Data Center through its water: the facility cools servers in one of the driest states, so the cloud has a watershed. These two are not on William's syllabus; they are in our suggested set.

**Already in his course:** Monserrate's "Thermotemporalities" (server heat, uptime and downtime), Mattern's deep time of media infrastructure, and Nyckel on Amazon's anticipatory shipping were all assigned on Oct 26; Pasquinelli and Joler's Nooscope (AI as knowledge extractivism) on Oct 19. Students already have the vocabulary; the game can lean on readings they've done instead of adding new ones.

**For the game (SW's proposal):** Every resource on the board names a place or a group. Not "Energy 6" but "the gas plant on the river" and "the night shift." When the AI builds, the cost lands somewhere on the map, often far from the data center (HI's `externality-card` mechanic). Server heat could be a track of its own (Monserrate).

**Try it:** Hand each table the *Data Center* map's 14 places and ask them to write, for each one, who would notice first if it ran dry.

**Could go wrong:** Numbers invented for the game get read as data. Label any figure on a card as the game's, not the world's, unless it comes from a cited source.
