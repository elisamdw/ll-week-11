**Marlon, Nov 3 (draft proposal):** "Then we'll design a game together on top of the database they made."

**What students do:**

1. Each table takes the index cards its group made on Sept 28 (or prints of them) and lays out the ones that name parts of an infrastructure. Blank cards fill gaps.
2. On each card, add three things in pencil: what it yields (power, water, data, labor, money), what it depends on (draw links: red for tight, blue for loose), and who would notice first if it failed.
3. Pick one card as the AI's first data center. Draw three cards from the AI's deck and play them on the new map: where does it spread, what runs dry, what falls?
4. Photograph the map. That photo is the template for the remix that follows.

**What it teaches:** The game's map is the students' own categories, so every outcome is something their database made possible. Star's call to study boring things becomes the work of labelling dependencies; the `any-map-test` asks whether the AI's rules still make sense on a world they weren't written for.

**Prepare:** Marlon's photos of the Sept 28 cards, printed at card size (the originals may be with William or the students). If the database Marlon planned to build from them exists by then, print it as cards instead.

**Try it:** With no Sept 28 cards, any 12 cards naming parts of Harvard's infrastructure (the steam tunnels, the dining halls' suppliers, the campus network) work.

**Could go wrong:** The Sept 28 cards were about the course's database, not all of them about infrastructure; some groups' cards won't map. Let those tables use blank cards, and say so.
