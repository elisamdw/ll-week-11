**The gap (SW's reading):** *Data Center* has four factions: the utility, the council, the company, and the neighbors. Nobody in it works; the ruleset's own `notModeled` list names "The people who work in the data center" first, and "Chips, mines, and the supply chain behind the hardware" third. William's course has read about operations and logistics (Mezzadra and Neilson on Nov 9, Nyckel on Amazon's anticipatory shipping on Oct 26), Fisch's train drivers and commuters, and on workshop day Rabinbach's human motor.

**The idea (SW's proposal):** A fifth faction: the workers who build, run, cool, and repair the data centers, and move what they need. They place workers on nodes (a node without workers can't run), they get tired (Rabinbach's fatigue: each shift drains them, rest refills them), they absorb a cascade for one round at a cost to themselves (Fisch's margin of indeterminacy), and they can `strike`, stopping a node. HI's mechanic card puts their goal as "wages, not winning."

**Why it matters for this room:** It turns the game from humans-versus-AI into a question the course asks: who keeps the infrastructure running, and what happens to them when it's automated (the AI's `automate-this` move can replace them).

**Try it:** Add a fifth player with 6 worker tokens to one table's paper game; nodes without a worker token produce nothing.

**Could go wrong:** With eight students, a fifth faction leaves one table short-handed. Two tables of four can swap one faction out (the council) for workers.
