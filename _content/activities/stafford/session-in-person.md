## Minute by minute (SW's proposal)

- **12:30, before the room fills.** Two tables of four, a laptop at each on the reader app with a fresh game loaded (Table A on *Data Center*, Table B on *Normal Technology*), the paper kit stacked beside each laptop. The journal prompt is already on the whiteboard, with the mechanics menu printed at each seat.
- **12:45 William.** His readings, his frame. The LL says one sentence: we brought a game, and William's questions will run it.
- **12:50 Coupling.** Each table gets 12 cards and two colours of tape and rolls within two minutes. A table that wants the screen plays `/proto/economy/cascade`. One question to end on: which link would you loosen first, and what would it cost?
- **13:00 The game.** One facilitator per table plays the first round with them, then steps back. Neither table is told that they are playing different AIs; that is revealed in the debrief. Stop at 13:28 wherever the game is.
- **13:30 The state.** Pairs look at their own game as data: the 14 places (template) beside who holds what now (instance). If the state panel isn't built, use a printed saved state.
- **13:40 Their cards.** Each table lays the Sept 28 cards out as places, tapes the dependencies, and replays one round of the AI's deck on that map. A card that doesn't map goes in a "left out" pile, which feeds the debrief.
- **14:00 Remix.** Each table picks one item from the menu. At one table an LL facilitator drives Claude Code in the game's folder; the other table remixes on paper. At 14:12 they swap if both are ready. Each table writes one sentence on what its change argues, and that sentence is theirs.
- **14:25 Debrief, William.** The [debrief card](what-the-mechanic-argues.md), from the board.
- **14:40 Close, William.** The journal prompt, and the kit handed to him.

## Roles (SW's proposal)

- **William:** opens, runs the debrief, closes. He decides whether a segment runs long.
- **Two LL facilitators,** one per table: they run the rules, play the first round, keep time, and at remix one of them drives Claude Code. Who they are is Marlon's call.
- **Timekeeper:** one of the facilitators, with a two-minute warning before each change.

## Fallbacks (SW's proposal)

- **A laptop or the reader app fails:** that table switches to the paper kit (the same rules) and the other table carries on. This needs the print-and-play kit, which is why it is on the checklist.
- **No Sept 28 cards or photos:** "Cards become the map" uses blank cards; each student writes three places from their own project. The rest is unchanged.
- **Claude Code is slow or stuck at remix:** both tables remix on paper; the facilitator shows one worked remix on screen as an example instead.
- **The room runs short on time:** the order to cut is above. If the game itself overruns, stop it at round 4 and carry on.

## What William takes with him

From [What William takes with him](what-william-takes-with-him.md): the print-and-play kit made from his students' cards, the game's folder with its ruleset and `AGENTS.md`, a playable link if Marlon deploys one, and one page of prompts. William opens and closes the session and leads the debrief, so the readings stay his; the LL brings the game and runs the middle. Every card's **Try it** line is sized so he could run that segment again with only the card and the kit.
