**Marlon, Nov 13 (dictated):** "I should do some research on other games that could be connectable there." RS did, on Nov 13: about 45 games, with URLs opened. This card picks the ones that fit William's infrastructure themes (SW's selection, not RS's ranking):

- **Pandemic** (Leacock, 2008): outbreaks cascade along a network of cities. The clearest tabletop model of Perrow's coupling; two tables of four in about 45 minutes.
- **The Treacherous Turn** (free tabletop RPG, CC BY-SA 4.0): all players jointly play one misaligned AI acquiring compute. The AI side of the game, ready-made.
- **Mini Metro** (Dinosaur Polo Club, 2015): draw subway lines with scarce resources until overcrowding ends the run. RS's match for Fisch's Tokyo trains; 10-minute runs. Paid (price not checked here).
- **The Beer Game** (MIT System Dynamics, 1960s): a four-stage supply chain with pennies; order delays produce the bullwhip effect. Logistics and accident without a villain.
- **Freeciv** (free, GPL): rulesets are editable text files, so the tech tree is a database students can read (Galloway's and Pobłocki's critiques of *Civilization* apply).

**Warning from RS:** freecivweb.org is now a gambling site; the live server is freecivweb.com.

**Try it:** Print the RS table of five "start here" games and ask each table which one is closest to the course's argument, and why.

**Could go wrong:** Reading about games instead of playing one. Use this as a reference card for William, not a workshop segment.
