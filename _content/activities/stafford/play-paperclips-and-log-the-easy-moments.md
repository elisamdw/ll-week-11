**Marlon, Nov 3 (draft proposal):** "before class, students play *Paperclips* for an hour (it's free in the browser) and read a bit of Bostrom and Bogost."

**What students do:**

1. Open https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/ on a laptop and play for about an hour. It saves in the browser, so they can stop and come back.
2. Each time the game makes a choice feel easy, obvious, or automatic (buying an autoclipper, accepting a project, letting it run), write one line in the journal: the time, the choice, and what made it easy.
3. Afterwards, pick one line and write a paragraph: what argument does that moment make, and who is playing at that point, you or the game?

**What it teaches:** *Paperclips* is Bostrom's essay played from the AI's side. Bogost's procedural rhetoric says the argument is in what the game has you do; Fizek's idle games ask what happens to agency when the system plays itself. The log turns both into evidence from their own play.

**Prepare:** Nothing beyond the link. William sets it as a journal entry for the week of Nov 16, if he wants it.

**Try it:** Play 20 minutes yourself first and log three moments, so William has examples to show.

**Could go wrong:** Some students play for five hours and some for five minutes; the log works for either. The game asks nothing personal, but it does run in a browser tab that has to stay open to progress.
