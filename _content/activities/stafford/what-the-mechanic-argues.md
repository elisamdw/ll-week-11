**Marlon, Nov 14 (voice chat):** he sees a tension between Nguyen's games as the art of agency and Bogost's procedural rhetoric, which implies the interactions send a message or, "in limit case," train the player (voice Claude's paraphrase around his words).

**What students do (William leads):**

1. **Two settings.** Reveal that the tables played different AIs (fast and slow). What did each table do differently, and which did they believe?
2. **First and last goals.** Each faction writes its goal again, now, and puts it beside the card it started with. Where did a goal bend toward what the infrastructure needed? (Winner's reverse adaptation, on the day his chapter is assigned.)
3. **What's left out.** Read the game's own `notModeled` list. Which omission would change the outcome most? Which did a remix put back in?
4. **Agency or training?** One round of hands: did the game give you choices, or teach you a lesson? (Nguyen against Bogost.)
5. **Journal prompt** to take away, in William's style: *Choose one mechanic from today. Describe what it made you do, and argue whether that is how the infrastructure in your project works.*

**What it teaches:** The day's readings, applied to something the room just did. Winner and Rabinbach become observations about play rather than summaries.

**Prepare:** The starting goal cards from each faction, kept after the game.

**Try it:** Runs from this card and a whiteboard; nothing else.

**Could go wrong:** It runs long and the journal prompt gets lost. Write the prompt on the board before step 1.
