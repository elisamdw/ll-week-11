# Playtest one change, report the evidence

1. **Choose and predict (3 min).** Open a prototype whose card exists and whose page loads. Record its route, the time and a visible version or commit if supplied by PL. Write one prediction: “When ___ happens, the player will ___.” A queued board entry is not a playable build.
2. **Play without coaching (5 min).** In pairs, one plays and one observes. Record the first attempted action, the first surprise and any point where play stops. Use roles or colors, never names.
3. **Separate evidence and interpretation (4 min).** Write: observed action; visible result; player’s explanation; observer’s hypothesis. If a failure occurs, write steps, expected result and observed result. Do not turn a guess about intent into a fact.
4. **Change one thing (5 min).** Swap roles. Use an exposed setting, or give the builder a single remix request with a specific route and expected effect. Do not edit another pane’s prototype files. If the change is not available, compare a paper version and label it as paper.
5. **Replay and report (8 min).** Start from the same initial setup, replay, and note what differs. Hand the builder one observation, one suggestion and one question. Staff or the authorized lead decides how it is sent; this card does not authorize Slack posting.

**What it teaches and why:** MDA provides a vocabulary for distinguishing what the system permits, what happens over time and what a player feels. Schell’s lenses keep playtesting open to changes in story, aesthetics and technology. A two-round comparison is evidence for a next design step, not proof of learning or balance.

**PL’s suggested test order (cmux update recorded Nov 15, 08:21:56 EST):** first Prices that move, both growth settings back to back with a chart comparison; then Every tap is a row with four people sharing a keyboard and reading its findings; then Committee with a room of voters. These are proposed tests. PL reports headless Chrome playthroughs, with no physical phone or hardware verification. For the four-player or room version, keep a separate observer and let the group take the player role; the pair format above is the minimum module.

**Prepare:** PL identifies a current playable build and reset. Use Committee or Every tap is a row only after the corresponding page is checked; the cards are candidate targets, not test results.

**Try it:** two fellows, one laptop, two five-minute rounds and a paper log: route/version, setup, action, expected, observed, suggestion, unresolved.

**What could go wrong:** a changed build or stale room makes comparisons misleading. Record the build and reset state; if those differ, label the comparison inconclusive.
