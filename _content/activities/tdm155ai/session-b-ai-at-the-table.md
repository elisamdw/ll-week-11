**The arc:** design first, then put the machine in the game. Students make their own paper games from the deck, then give Claude a seat in them (opponent, advisor or director), then move a board's prices onto a phone, the hybrid version Marlon said is "definitely for my class." The remix and the Final Project close are the same as Session A.

**Ideas to say, not lecture:** mechanics are the verbs; AI can be a player or a material; the phone holds what a board can't.

**Strengths:** students make more of their own games, and AI is in the room as a player, not only as a coding tool; it uses PL's prototypes most. **Risks:** less on game state and *Paperclips* (Session A's core, and Marlon's Nov 14 board-game-to-data direction); the prices activity depends on PE's prototype or a spreadsheet stand-in. **A mix (TD's suggestion):** Session A's first hour, then *Claude takes a seat* in place of *Play two theories of AI*.
