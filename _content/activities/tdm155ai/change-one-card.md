**What students do**

1. In pairs, play one round of tic-tac-toe (or rock-paper-scissors). One minute.
2. Draw one card from the mechanics deck. Read its one line and its "Try it in two minutes."
3. Change the game with that mechanic and play two rounds. Card 1 on its own turns tic-tac-toe inside out ("whoever makes three in a row loses"); Hidden information, Chance, Push your luck, Timer and Constraint all work on a three-by-three grid.
4. Each pair says in one sentence what changed: a new decision, or only a new rule to remember?

**What it teaches:** a mechanic is a part you can swap, and swapping one changes the play more than its size suggests. It opens the day on the deck students will use all afternoon, with their hands rather than the projector.

**Prepare:** the deck printed and cut, or the 11×17 posters on the walls (GM's build is in the class repo, [README](../../../../../tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-11/game-mechanics/README.md); hand cards at half-letter are proposed there, not built). Pull the cards that work on a grid (TD's pick: 01, 06, 12, 13, 14, 16, 40), or let pairs draw from the whole deck and redraw if stuck.

**Try it:** two students, one sheet of paper, card 16 (*Hidden rule*): one invents a secret rule for where marks may go; the other finds it by playing.

**What could go wrong:** a pair draws a card that needs a crowd (Voting, Spect-actor). Let them redraw; that is a finding too (some mechanics need more players).
