**The history, already written:** HI's [Strand 2, AI inside games](../../history/2-ai-inside-games.md). Four items show four uses:

- **The director** ([*Left 4 Dead*, 2008](../../history/items/left-4-dead-ai-director.md)): a system estimates each player's intensity and cycles build-up, peak, fade and relax, adjusting pacing "to maximize 'drama'" (Booth's slides, quoted by HI). AI as a stage manager.
- **The nemesis** ([*Shadow of Mordor*'s Nemesis system](../../history/items/nemesis-system.md)): enemies remember you. AI as memory.
- **The improvising character** ([*Façade*, 2005](../../history/items/facade.md)): a drama in which two characters answer what you type. AI as actor.
- **The dungeon master** ([*AI Dungeon*, 2019](../../history/items/ai-dungeon.md)): a language model narrates whatever you try. AI as game master, with no rules underneath.

GM's deck has the current version of crafting with a model: *Infinite Craft*, where a model decides what two things combine into ([card 33](../../../../../tdm155ai/tdm155ai-week-11/game-mechanics/cards/33-crafting-and-combining.md), in the class repo).

**Why it matters for this class (TD's proposal):** students' Final Projects are AI systems that meet an audience. The director, the improvising character and the game master are three patterns they could build into a project in a week: a system that watches, one that answers, one that narrates. It is also where the old week 11 plan (Slackbots as "improvisational AI co-stars") still fits, under a games frame.
