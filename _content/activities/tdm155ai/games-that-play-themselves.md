**Already written:** TH's [Fizek entry](../../theory/fizek-playing-at-a-distance.md) (*Playing at a Distance*, 2022, open access; *Paperclips* is on pp. 60–61): games that automate their own play, so the player's job becomes setting things running and checking back. GL's [*Paperclips* folder](../../paperclips/README.md) (the game, its licence, why we write our own); HI's [*Paperclips* as data](../../game-state/paperclips.md) (about 230 values in one `saveGame` snapshot, readable in the browser).

**The mechanics, from the inventory:** `incremental-growth` (numbers grow by themselves; the player tunes the rate), `automate-this` (each upgrade removes a click and a decision, until the player can only watch and set priorities), `come-back-later`, `delegation`.

**Why it matters for this class (TD's proposal):** week 9 and 10 had students building agent systems that run while they watch. *Paperclips* is the same situation turned into a game, and its theme (Bostrom's maximizer) is the fear about such systems. Fifteen minutes of play and one look at the save file make both points without a lecture.

**Caution:** *Paperclips* is long; a class can only see its first phase. Say so, and offer the rest as homework over Thanksgiving if Marlon wants it.
