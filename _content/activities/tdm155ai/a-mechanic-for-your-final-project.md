The syllabus for Nov 18: "Games, interactive systems, and multiplayer AI: audiences and participants in the loop; games as systems with many, many parts. … This is the week your project meets other people." Marlon, Nov 2: "Interaction can be part of both games and installations."

**What students do**

0. **A taste of the learning harness (8 min, whole class).** HB's [taste card](../../learning/class/pointer-at-the-closer.md): Marlon shows a harness folder in Claude Desktop's Code tab, then the same folder as a map at `/learn`, and hands out the take-home: over Thanksgiving, set up a folder ([setup](../../learning/class/setup.md)), learn one thing your Final Project needs, play it as a game, try one remix, and show the map on Dec 2. It is the day's last game, and its mechanic (an idea unlocks when you explain it: `tech-tree-and-unlocks`, `tutor-face`) is one more card students can pick in step 1.
1. **Pick a card (3 min).** From the deck, each student picks the one mechanic that would let someone into their Final Project: a visitor's vote, a zone they step into, a choice that branches, a spect-actor who steps in, a thing that runs while they're away.
2. **Sketch it (4 min).** On an index card: who meets the work, what they do (the verb), what the system does back, and what it remembers (the state).
3. **Try it on a neighbour (4 min).** Talk a neighbour through it as if they were the visitor; they play it out loud, and say where they got lost.
4. **Say it (6 min, in turns or in pairs at the camera).** Sixty seconds, unscripted: "In my project, a visitor can … and the system …"

**What it teaches:** it brings the day back to the Final Projects two weeks before the Dec 2 work-in-progress showing and its installation planning. A game's mechanics are a vocabulary for the question the term has been asking since week 9: how and where will someone encounter your system? The sixty seconds is the course's third skill, speaking about the work on the spot.

**Prepare:** the harness folder and `/learn` (HB's card lists what); the deck laid out by family; index cards; the camera set up as for reflection videos. No student names or projects go in this repo; the cards stay with the students.

**Try it:** Marlon models it first with an LL project, in sixty seconds.

**What could go wrong:** a student's project has no audience moment yet; then the card is the first one, and that's the point. Students whose projects are not interactive by design (a podcast series) can pick a mechanic for how listeners find or follow it (`come-back-later`, `feedback`).
