**What students do**

1. **Play (10 min).** Each table starts one board game from its neutral setup and plays until the timer. Before the first roll, the overhead camera (or a phone held high) takes a photo of the set-up board; keep it for the next activity.
2. **Take it apart (12 min).** On a large sheet, four columns:
   - **Rules:** what's allowed and how it ends. Who enforced each one at your table?
   - **Mechanics:** the verbs. What did players do? Match each to a card in the deck; write a new card for any the deck lacks.
   - **Game state:** what's true right now that the game needs to remember.
   - **Other game elements** (Schell's tetrad): the story, the look and feel of the board, the technology (dice, paper money, cardboard).
3. **Report (3 min).** Each table names the one mechanic it would remove first, and what that would do to the game.

**What it teaches:** that "rules" is the smallest of the four columns. Marlon, Nov 14: "the design of the game is much, much more than the rules," and rules may belong more to pre-digital games, where people perform the mechanics and police the turns. Here students are the computer: they enforce the rules and keep the state by hand, which makes the next activity (photographing that state) and the screen game after it legible. Readings behind it: Schell's tetrad, MDA, Sicart's mechanics as "methods invoked by agents" (all in TH's [handout](../../handouts/rules-and-mechanics.md)).

**Prepare:** the three boxes, opened and checked (HI's [Monopoly page](../../game-state/monopoly.md) notes editions differ in money and names); one sheet and markers per table; the deck; the overhead camera if it is up, otherwise phones.

**Try it:** Monopoly, four players, ten minutes; the table that gets furthest in the "rules" column usually finds that half of what it wrote down are house rules.

**What could go wrong:** ten minutes of Monopoly barely leaves the start. That is fine for this activity, but for the photo activity after it, ask one table to play faster (skip buying auctions) so its board changes. Risk takes long to set up; set it up before class.
