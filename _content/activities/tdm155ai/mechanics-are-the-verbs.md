Marlon, Nov 14 (voice chat): "The design of the game is much, much more than the rules." He works from Schell's *The Art of Game Design* and thinks in mechanics and game elements.

**The one idea for Wednesday:** rules bound the possibility space; mechanics are the verbs (roll, buy, spread, harden); the game state is what the verbs change. In a board game the players do all three with their hands. In a video game the computer enforces the rules and keeps the state, so the player meets only the verbs and how the world answers ("That's just the way the game works," Marlon).

**Where it is argued, already written:** TH's handout [Rules, mechanics, and the rest of the game](../../handouts/rules-and-mechanics.md) has the quotations (Sicart 2008, Juul, Costikyan, Manovich, Schell's tetrad) and the push-back (Bogost puts the argument in the rules; Adams and Dormans fold rules into mechanics; MDA pairs them). The [glossary](../../glossary/README.md) defines rules, mechanics, procedures, game elements, game state, template and instance.

**For the class (TD's proposal):** don't lecture it. Let students meet it in the order of the day: play a board game (they enforce the rules), take it apart into verbs, then play a screen game where the rules vanish into the code. Schell's question from his Lens of Rules, via TH, is a good one to write on the board: "Who enforces the rules?"
