# The Singularity Is Near / The Singularity Is Nearer

Two books by the inventor and futurist Ray Kurzweil: *The Singularity Is Near: When Humans Transcend Biology* (Viking, September 2005, 652 pages) and its sequel *The Singularity Is Nearer: When We Merge with AI* (released June 25, 2024), the canonical techno-utopian forecast of human-level AI by 2029 and a merger of humans and machines by 2045.

## The argument

Kurzweil's engine is the **law of accelerating returns**: information technologies improve exponentially, not linearly, because each generation of technology is used to build the next, so the 21st century will hold far more than a hundred years of change at today's rate. From price-performance curves of computation he projects that machines will match human intelligence by **2029** (a date he has held for decades and repeats in 2024) and that by **2045** humans will merge with the intelligence they have built, through brain–computer interfaces and nanobots, multiplying their own intelligence many times over; that merger is his **Singularity**. Unlike Vinge's 1993 version, where the human era ends, Kurzweil's Singularity is something humans join rather than suffer; he takes the risks seriously (he discusses genetics, nanotechnology, and robotics as "GNR") but treats them as manageable. The 2024 sequel, written into the generative-AI boom, keeps both dates and adds a chapter on how much human life has improved over the centuries. His curve is the shape that later forecasts like [AI 2027](ai-2027.md) inherit, and the phrase "Law of Accelerating Returns" appears by name in Andreessen's [Techno-Optimist Manifesto](accelerationism-and-ai-safety.md).

## Read this

- Free and short: Kurzweil's essay "The Law of Accelerating Returns" (March 7, 2001), the argument of the 2005 book in essay form; the opening summary and the section titled "The Singularity Is Near" take 20 minutes. Archived at the Wayback Machine (kurzweilai.net has moved).
- *The Singularity Is Near*, chapter 1 ("The Six Epochs") and chapter 2 (the law of accelerating returns): about two hours. Borrowable from the Internet Archive's lending library (identifier `singularityisnea00kurz`) or Harvard Library.
- *The Singularity Is Nearer* (2024), chapter 1 ("Where Are We in the Six Stages?") for the updated dates: about 40 minutes; library or purchase.

## Critiques and conversations

- Vernor Vinge's 1993 essay is the source of the term and of its darker reading ([Vinge](vinge-singularity.md)).
- Gebru and Torres put "singularitarianism" in the TESCREAL bundle and quote Kurzweil's 1999 phrase "a clear and future danger" as the hinge between utopia and apocalypse ([TESCREAL](tescreal.md)).
- Narayanan and Kapoor argue that diffusion, not capability, sets the pace, and that adoption of past general-purpose technologies like electricity took decades ([normal technology](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md)); Amodei rejects the instant-Singularity reading too, citing physical limits ([Machines of Loving Grace](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md)).
- Becca Rothfeld reviewed the sequel in the *Washington Post* (June 26, 2024) as "Ray Kurzweil is (still, somehow) excited about humans merging with machines" (title only, via Wikipedia).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (C) **Exponential clock:** the AI player's income doubles on a fixed schedule (the law of accelerating returns as a rule), so the human team's linear actions feel adequate early and hopeless late; that felt gap is the lesson.
- (B) **Merge as a third ending:** a "Merge" track that humans can advance by connecting their faction to the AI's nodes; reaching it ends the game in neither victory nor defeat, and the debrief asks whether that counts as winning.
- (A, B, C) **Dated milestone cards:** "2029: human-level AI" and "2045: Singularity" as timeline cards on the board, with later forecasts (AI 2027, AI 2040) as alternate decks; students choose which forecaster's deck to play.
