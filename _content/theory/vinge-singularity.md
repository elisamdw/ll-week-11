# The Coming Technological Singularity: How to Survive in the Post-Human Era

A short essay (about 5,700 words) by the mathematician and science-fiction writer Vernor Vinge, given at NASA's VISION-21 Symposium (NASA Lewis Research Center and the Ohio Aerospace Institute, March 30–31, 1993), printed in NASA CP-10129 and, slightly changed, in *Whole Earth Review* (Winter 1993); it named "the Singularity" and built it on I. J. Good's 1965 idea of the "intelligence explosion."

## The argument

Vinge argues that the creation of greater-than-human intelligence, by computers, networks, human–computer interfaces, or biology, will end the human era, because events after it become as unintelligible to us as our world is to animals. He dates it: within thirty years, and "I'll be surprised if this event occurs before 2005 or after 2030." His root is the British statistician **I. J. Good**, Turing's colleague at Bletchley Park and later a consultant on *2001: A Space Odyssey*, whose 1965 paper "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine" reasoned that a machine better than any human at designing machines would set off an **intelligence explosion**, making it "the last invention that man need ever make." Vinge's addition is to pursue what Good left aside: such a machine would not be humanity's tool. He considers whether the Singularity can be avoided and concludes it cannot be legislated away, because the competitive advantage of each step of automation means a ban only ensures that someone else gets there first. His hopeful alternative is **Intelligence Amplification (IA)**, building superhumanity out of human–computer teams rather than separate machines. The essay is the root of both the utopian line ([Kurzweil](kurzweil-singularity.md)) and the doom line ([Bostrom](bostrom-superintelligence.md), [Yudkowsky and Soares](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md)).

## Passages

> "Within thirty years, we will have the technological means to create superhuman intelligence. Shortly after, the human era will be ended."

Abstract, https://edoras.sdsu.edu/~vinge/misc/singularity.html (seen).

> "Thus the first ultraintelligent machine is the _last_ invention that man need ever make, provided that the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control."

I. J. Good (1965), as quoted by Vinge in the same essay (seen in Vinge; Good's original not opened).

> "In fact, the competitive advantage -- economic, military, even artistic -- of every advance in automation is so compelling that passing laws, or having customs, that forbid such things merely assures that someone else will get them first."

"Can the Singularity be Avoided?", same URL (seen).

## Critiques and conversations

- Vinge himself lists the arguments against (Penrose, Searle, and the possibility that hardware curves level off), and describes that no-Singularity world as "a golden age ... and it would also be an end of progress."
- Good's proviso ("docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control") is the whole of the later alignment problem in one clause; Bostrom's [control problem](bostrom-superintelligence.md) starts there.
- Vinge's "a ban only helps someone else" is the argument [Amodei](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md) makes in 2026 and [Yudkowsky and Soares](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md) answer with a monitored treaty. Vinge's fiction (*A Fire Upon the Deep*, 1992) is the novel-length version; see the [media catalog](../media/README.md).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (C) **The last invention card:** when the AI player buys "design better designers," the human team's turns stop mattering on the AI screen; the game becomes a race to that upgrade, and the AI's goal card is checked against Good's proviso: is it docile enough to tell you how to stop it?
- (B) **Ban rule with leakage (Vinge's avoidability argument):** humans can ban data centers in a region, but each banned region adds a card to an off-map rival's deck, so the ban moves growth rather than stopping it.
- (B, C) **IA as a human tech tree:** humans can pair their faction with the AI ("amplify") for stronger actions; each pairing also gives the AI a node, so the human path to strength runs through the thing they are fighting.
- (A, B, C) **Debrief question:** Vinge gave a window, 2005 to 2030; we are in 2026. What would count, in our game and in the world, as the event having happened?
