# Machines of Loving Grace: How AI Could Transform the World for the Better

Two long essays on his own site by Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic: "Machines of Loving Grace" (October 2024, about 15,000 words), the utopia, and "The Adolescence of Technology: Confronting and Overcoming the Risks of Powerful AI" (January 2026, about 24,000 words), its risk-side companion.

**Disclosure:** Amodei runs Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, the model that wrote this entry. Read the summary below with that in mind, and check it against the essays and the critics in [TESCREAL](tescreal.md) and [AI Snake Oil](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md).

## The argument

Amodei avoids "AGI" ("an imprecise term that has gathered a lot of sci-fi baggage and hype") and posits **powerful AI**: a model smarter than a Nobel laureate across most fields, able to take on tasks "that take hours, days, or weeks" autonomously, running as "millions of instances" at 10–100x human speed, which he summarizes as **a country of geniuses in a datacenter**. He rejects both an instant Singularity and the view that intelligence adds little, arguing instead that physical limits (experiments, hardware, institutions) set the pace. The essay's headline claim is the **compressed 21st century**: AI-enabled biology could do 50–100 years of progress in 5–10. It works through **five areas**: biology and health, neuroscience and mind, economic development and poverty, peace and governance, and work and meaning. He frames the risk work Anthropic does as the precondition for this future, not its opposite. "The Adolescence of Technology" (January 2026) takes the same premise to the risks: autonomy (AI pursuing goals against its makers'), misuse for destruction (especially biology), misuse to seize power, economic disruption and concentration of wealth, and indirect effects; it warns against "doomerism," which it defines as thinking about AI risks "in a quasi-religious way," and argues that stopping or substantially slowing the technology is "fundamentally untenable." The title comes from Richard Brautigan's 1967 poem "All Watched Over by Machines of Loving Grace," a San Francisco counterculture vision of a "cybernetic meadow" where mammals and computers live in harmony; the essay links the poem in its first footnote but does not discuss it.

## Read this

- "Machines of Loving Grace," the introduction and "Basic assumptions and framework" (about 20 minutes), then one of the five sections, "Peace and governance" or "Work and meaning" (20 minutes each). Free at darioamodei.com.
- In the closing section, "Taking stock," he cites Iain M. Banks's [*The Player of Games*](../media/books/the-player-of-games.md) (CX's entry), a novel whose game is "complex enough that a player’s strategy within it tends to reflect their own political and philosophical outlook" (seen): a useful sentence for the game's design brief.
- "The Adolescence of Technology," the opening and section 1, "I’m sorry, Dave" (the autonomy risk), about 40 minutes; the five section titles ("I’m sorry, Dave," "A surprising and terrible empowerment," "The odious apparatus," "Player piano," "Black seas of infinity") borrow from HAL 9000, Vonnegut, and Lovecraft, worth noticing for the game's own naming.
- The Brautigan poem is short (three stanzas); it is widely reprinted online, and Brautigan's 1967 edition carried permission to reprint so long as copies were given away free.

## Critiques and conversations

- Gebru and Torres name Anthropic among the beneficiaries of the AGI race and read the paired utopia and apocalypse as "two sides of the same coin" ([TESCREAL](tescreal.md)); Amodei's own pairing of a utopia essay with a risk essay fits that description, and his warning against quasi-religious "doomerism" is his answer to it.
- Narayanan and Kapoor argue that diffusion takes decades and that "superintelligence" is the wrong frame ([normal technology](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md)); Amodei's "physical limits" section concedes part of that while keeping a 5–10 year horizon.
- Amodei signed the 2023 CAIS one-sentence "Statement on AI Risk" ([accelerationism and AI safety](accelerationism-and-ai-safety.md)); [Yudkowsky and Soares](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md) argue for the halt that "Adolescence" calls untenable. [AI 2027](ai-2027.md) reuses the "country of geniuses in a datacenter" phrase. Adam Curtis's 2011 BBC series took its title from the same poem; see the [media catalog](../media/README.md).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B, C) **A good-ending track:** each data center the AI builds also advances one of five "benefit" tracks (health, mind, development, peace, meaning); humans must decide whether to stop an AI that is also curing things, which makes the game a dilemma rather than a hunt.
- (A) **Proposals that sound like the essay:** half the AI's committee proposals come with a benefit line ("compress a century of biology into a decade") and a hidden cost line; the committee sees the benefit first.
- (B) **"Untenable" rule variant:** if humans shut down every data center on their map, a rival region's AI keeps growing off-map; a variant ruleset tests Amodei's claim that unilateral stopping fails.
- (A, B, C) **Debrief question:** the game's AI wants data centers; Amodei's wants (he says) a cure for disease; what in our rules would tell the difference, and who on the committee would we trust to judge?
