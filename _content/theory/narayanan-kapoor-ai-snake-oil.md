# AI Snake Oil / AI as Normal Technology

Two works by the Princeton computer scientists Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor: the book *AI Snake Oil: What Artificial Intelligence Can Do, What It Can't, and How to Tell the Difference* (Princeton University Press, September 24, 2024, about 350 pages) and the essay "AI as Normal Technology" (Knight First Amendment Institute at Columbia, April 15, 2025, about 22,000 words), the main skeptical alternative to both doom and utopia.

## The argument

*AI Snake Oil* separates kinds of AI that the word lumps together. **Predictive AI**, used to forecast who will commit a crime, fail a course, or default, is where most of the snake oil is: in the authors' words, it "not only does not work today, but will likely never work, because of the inherent difficulties in predicting human behavior." **Generative AI** is useful and overhyped; content moderation by AI cannot fix social media. Chapter 5, "Is Advanced AI an Existential Threat?", rejects the superintelligence framing. "AI as Normal Technology" generalizes this into a worldview: AI is a transformative general-purpose technology like **electricity** or the internet, and like them its effects arrive through **diffusion**, which takes decades because organizations, institutions, and safety-critical fields adapt slowly. They separate AI **methods**, **applications**, and **adoption**, each moving at a different speed, and they reject "intelligence" and "superintelligence" as concepts that cloud reasoning: humans with AI tools are not on a single scale below a machine. On risk, they call catastrophic misalignment "(by far) the most speculative" risk and put the defenses **downstream**, in hardening the systems an AI might attack (cybersecurity, critical infrastructure), not in aligning a hypothetical superintelligence. Both utopian and dystopian visions, they argue, share the error of treating AI "akin to a separate species."

## Passages

> "To view AI as normal is not to understate its impact—even transformative, general-purpose technologies such as electricity and the internet are “normal” in our conception. But it is in contrast to both utopian and dystopian visions of the future of AI which have a common tendency to treat it akin to a separate species, a highly autonomous, potentially superintelligent entity."

"AI as Normal Technology," introduction, https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology (seen).

> "In our view, the primary defense against misalignment, again, lies downstream. The defenses needed against misuse that we discussed earlier—from hardening critical infrastructure to improving cybersecurity—will also serve as protection against potential misalignment risks."

Same essay, Part III, "Catastrophic misalignment is a speculative risk" (seen).

## Critiques and conversations

- The AI 2027 authors and Yudkowsky are the targets ([AI 2027](ai-2027.md), [If Anyone Builds It](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md)); LessWrong replies include "AI is Not Normal Technology" (greaterwrong.com/posts/F2mwKxFRhjqizA89p, not opened); the common objection is that gradual impact does not make AI continuous with past technologies.
- [Amodei](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md) concedes physical and institutional limits but keeps a horizon of years, not decades.
- Allies with a different emphasis: Bender and Hanna's *The AI Con* and the [TESCREAL](tescreal.md) critique, which share the rejection of the superintelligence frame and add an argument about power and ideology. On the "games" section, compare Bogost and Sicart in `NB/_context/projects/50-in-focus/ll-weeks/fall-2026/11-ending-20261120/research/learning-games-classics.md`.

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B) **Normal-technology ruleset:** a variant in which the AI's deck can only place a data center where a human faction has adopted it (sold power, approved a permit); spread is set by adoption, not by the AI's will, and the game lasts many rounds with no turn card.
- (B) **Downstream defense actions:** the regulator and utility can "harden" a node (backup power, segmentation), which stops failures cascading along tightly coupled links (Perrow) whatever the AI intends; a direct test of the essay's claim.
- (A, B, C) **Debrief question from their games section:** they argue games give "misleading intuitions" about superintelligence; what intuition did our game just give, and where did its rules make the AI more capable than any system could be?
- (A) **Snake-oil proposals:** some of the AI's committee proposals are predictive claims ("I can tell which districts will riot"); approving one costs trust later when it fails, teaching the predictive-versus-generative distinction.
