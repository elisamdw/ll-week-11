# The TESCREAL bundle: Eugenics and the promise of utopia through artificial general intelligence

An open-access journal article by Timnit Gebru (DAIR) and Émile P. Torres in *First Monday* 29(4) (April 2024, doi:10.5210/fm.v29i4.13636, about 21,000 words with notes), the main critique of AI apocalypse and utopia talk as one ideology, read here with its background: "Stochastic Parrots" (2021), the DAIR statement on the pause letter (2023), and Bender and Hanna's *The AI Con* (2025).

## The argument

Gebru and Torres ask why building **AGI** is treated as an unquestioned goal, and answer that the motive is ideological: a bundle of seven overlapping movements, **TESCREAL** ("transhumanism, Extropianism, singularitarianism, (modern) cosmism, Rationalism, Effective Altruism, and longtermism"), often shaped and funded by the same people, which they trace back to twentieth-century Anglo-American eugenics through transhumanism. The bundle's **eschatology** has two faces, utopia and apocalypse, which are bound up together: the same technology promises a posthuman paradise and threatens extinction, so "AI safety" and the race to build AGI are two sides of one coin (section 6's title). They argue an undefined system like "AGI" cannot be tested for safety the way a scoped tool can, and that the race concentrates resources and power in a few firms (they name OpenAI and Anthropic) while harming marginalized groups now, under the language of "benefiting humanity." Their prescription: build defined tools for specific tasks with safety protocols, not an all-knowing system. The background is **"On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots"** (Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and "Shmargaret Shmitchell," that is, Margaret Mitchell; FAccT, March 2021, pp. 610–623), which argued that ever-larger language models carry environmental, financial, and bias costs and produce fluent text without understanding. When the FLI pause letter appeared, its listed authors answered (DAIR, March 31, 2023) that its hypothetical "powerful digital minds" came from longtermism and distracted from present harms: worker exploitation, data theft, and concentration of power. *The AI Con* (Bender and Alex Hanna, Harper, May 2025) carries the line into a trade book, treating both boosters and doomers as selling the same premise, that AI is a singular, autonomous force.

## Critiques and conversations

- The article quotes Andreessen putting "TESCREAList" in his own social-media bio; Torres also files e/acc under the bundle ([accelerationism and AI safety](accelerationism-and-ai-safety.md)).
- Critics from within effective altruism and rationalism say the acronym lumps distinct, sometimes opposed movements into one "monolithic" target (e.g., Ozy Brennan in *Asterisk*; not opened here).
- Amodei's warning against "quasi-religious" doomerism and "prophecies of salvation" ([Machines of Loving Grace](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md)) concedes the eschatology charge while rejecting the conclusion. Narayanan and Kapoor reach a similar skepticism of "superintelligence" without the genealogy ([AI Snake Oil](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md)). Bostrom, central to the article's section 4, has his own entry ([Superintelligence](bostrom-superintelligence.md), [Deep Utopia](bostrom-deep-utopia.md)).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (A, B, C) **Debrief question, whose future:** the game assumes a superintelligent AI is the threat; list who in our game is harmed before the AI "turns" (workers, the city, the river) and ask whether the turn card was the right climax at all.
- (B) **Present-harm track:** every data center placed also costs a local faction (water, rent, labor) immediately; humans can lose by neglect of this track even if the AI never turns.
- (A) **Undefined goal penalty (the AGI-cannot-be-tested argument):** the committee may choose a scoped goal (one task, testable) or an open one ("benefit humanity"); open goals grow faster but no safety check can be run on them.
- (B, C) **Who funds the AI:** the company faction's private goal card is drawn from a deck of named ideologies (accelerationist, longtermist, safety lab); the reveal at the end asks students to place each in or out of the bundle.
