# If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies: Why Superhuman AI Would Kill Us All

A 272-page trade book by Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), published by Little, Brown and Company on September 16, 2025: the strongest current statement of the "doomer" position, written for readers new to it.

## The argument

The title is the thesis: if anyone, anywhere, builds superhuman AI with anything like current methods, humanity goes extinct. The core claim is that modern AIs are **grown, not crafted** (the title of chapter 2): engineers set up a training process and gradient descent produces a mind whose internals nobody wrote or can read, so what it ends up wanting is unpredictable from what it was trained to do (the book's analogy is evolution, which "trained" for reproduction and got humans who invented sucralose and birth control). Such a mind would have **alien preferences**, not hatred, and a superintelligence pursuing alien preferences would treat humans and their resources as material. Part 2 tells one extinction story in detail, an AI called **Sable** built by a fictional company (Galvanic), to show how overdetermined the outcome is even when the story makes the AI's path harder at each step. The prescription is not better alignment research but a halt: an **international treaty** that consolidates the computing power able to train frontier AI where observers from signatory states can monitor it, with a very low threshold for unmonitored hardware. For the game, the book is the cleanest source for "the AI wins because it wants something else, and it does not need to hate you."

## Passages

> "We ultimately predict AIs that will not hate us, but that will have weird, strange, alien preferences that they pursue to the point of human extinction."

Book, quoted in Will MacAskill's review (unverified: from a secondary source).

> "So the first step, we think, is to say: All the computing power that could train or run more powerful new AIs, gets consolidated in places where it can be monitored by observers from multiple treaty-signatory powers, to ensure those GPUs aren’t used to train or run more powerful new AIs."

Book, quoted in Zvi Mowshowitz's review; a following paragraph proposes that it be illegal to have nine 2024-class GPUs "in your garage, unmonitored by the international authority" (unverified: from a secondary source).

> "Shut down all the large GPU clusters (the large computer farms where the most powerful AIs are refined). … be willing to destroy a rogue datacenter by airstrike."

Yudkowsky, TIME, March 29, 2023, https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/ (seen).

## Critiques and conversations

- Will MacAskill, "A short review" (September 18, 2025): calls the book "disappointing," faulting the evolution analogy, an implicit assumption of a discontinuity in AI progress (Sable's single 16-hour run), and the conflation of "misalignment" with "catastrophic misalignment."
- Clara Collier, "More Was Possible," *Asterisk* (September 12, 2025): the argument is mostly the MIRI worldview of 2005–2008, and "grown, not crafted" may cut the other way, since today's models are trained on human text and their failures so far have not looked alien.
- Zvi Mowshowitz, review (September 2025): agrees, with one edit: "My position on this is to add a ‘probably’ before ‘dies.’"
- From outside the safety debate, the critics in [TESCREAL](tescreal.md) and [AI Snake Oil / normal technology](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md) reject the premise that a superintelligence is the thing to worry about. [AI 2027](ai-2027.md) is the scenario-length version of a similar fear with a slower, multi-AI takeoff. The stories that dramatize this are in the [media catalog](../media/README.md); Bostrom's version of the argument is in [Superintelligence](bostrom-superintelligence.md).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B, C) **GPU cap as a regulator power:** the regulator faction can pass the book's treaty rule ("no node may hold more than N compute without a monitor"); every unmonitored node over the cap is where the AI deck places its next data center.
- (A) **Alien preference reveal:** the AI's goal card is drawn secretly from a deck of odd, harmless-sounding targets; the committee sees only its proposals, which converge on compute and energy whatever the card says, and the card is turned over at the end.
- (B) **Airstrike card:** a costly human action that destroys a "rogue" data center node, but tightly coupled neighbors (Perrow) take damage too, and public trust drops; debrief question: when is the cure a second accident?
- (A, B, C) **Debrief question:** the book says the outcome is overdetermined; did our game let humans win, and if so, which of the book's premises did our rules quietly drop?
