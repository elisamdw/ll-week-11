# Play Anything: The Pleasure of Limits, the Uses of Boredom, and the Secret of Games

Ian Bogost's trade book on play as attention to limits (Basic Books, September 2016, 288 pages), written for general readers rather than game scholars.

## The argument

Bogost argues that play is not escape from boring reality but close work inside a limited system: "Play is a way of operating a constrained system in a gratifying way" (as quoted by LaLone). **Fun** is not a feeling we bring; it comes after, as a by-product of taking a thing's limits seriously, "the exhaust produced when an operator can treat something with dignity" (as quoted by LaLone). He replaces Huizinga's magic circle with the **playground**, any bounded place whose materials and rules we accept, from a mall floor his daughter turns into a sidewalk-crack game to his own lawn, which he came to tend obsessively by learning how grass works. His diagnosis of the present is **ironoia**, "the mistrust of things" (a chapter title): an ironic distance that keeps the world at arm's length so it cannot disappoint us. The book is therefore also a quiet rebuttal of gamification: you do not add sugar to a chore, you find the play already in its constraints, which follows directly from [Bogost, Cow Clicker](bogost-cow-clicker.md).

## Critiques and conversations

- LaLone (2017) reads it as part self-help, part design book, part humanities monograph, and says no one category fits.
- A 2021 lecture report keeps the thesis alive: "Anything can be a ground for play, once the materials are taken seriously" (Bogost as quoted by Jeannette Cooperman, "Play Is a Form of Respect," *Common Reader*, Washington University, Dec. 1, 2021, seen).
- Set against [Sicart, Play Matters](sicart-play-matters.md), which also locates play in the player's activity rather than the game object, but stresses play as appropriation and disruption where Bogost stresses respect for the material's limits.
- His earlier, darker account of what a game made only of timers does to players: [Bogost, Cow Clicker](bogost-cow-clicker.md).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (A, B) Co-design by subtraction: students improve the game by removing an action, a resource, or a rule, and test whether each cut makes it more playable.
- (B) Materials taken seriously: node properties come from the students' index-card database (capacity, water draw, who depends on it), not invented flavor, so the play comes from how the infrastructure works.
- (C) Resistance for the AI player: the human side and the map should push back on the incremental screen (shortages, refusals), since "we have the most fun when they resist us."
- (A, B, C) An ironoia debrief question: AI doom invites ironic distance (memes, shrugging); did the game let you stay ironic, or did it make you commit to a side?
