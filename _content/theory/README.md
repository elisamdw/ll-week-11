## Start here (TH's picks, about five hours in all)

1. **Bostrom, "The Superintelligent Will" (2012).** Free, 15 pages, about 45 minutes. Orthogonality and instrumental convergence in Bostrom's exact words: the premise of *Paperclips* and of the game. → [Superintelligence](bostrom-superintelligence.md)
2. **Bogost, "The Rhetoric of Video Games" (2008).** Free, 24 pages, about 45 minutes. His own short version of *Persuasive Games*: procedural rhetoric, *Animal Crossing*'s mortgage, *The McDonald's Videogame*. The test for whether the game argues through what it makes players do. → [Persuasive Games](bogost-persuasive-games.md)
3. **Fizek, *Playing at a Distance*, Prelude and ch. 4 "Automated Play" (2022).** Open access, about 50 minutes; *Paperclips* is on pp. 60–61. Games that play themselves, which is what an AI that runs on its own looks like from the player's chair. → [Fizek](fizek-playing-at-a-distance.md)
4. **Nguyen, "Value Capture" (2024), section 2 and pp. 492–94.** Open access, about 30 minutes. A person or group that takes a simplified metric as its goal: the paperclip maximizer, met in people and institutions (that link is TH's reading, not Nguyen's). → [Nguyen](nguyen-games-agency-as-art.md)
5. ***AI 2027*, through "October 2027" and the race ending (2025).** Free, about two hours. A scenario that already turns on a committee vote, 6–4, and ends with the Earth "reshaped into" data centers. → [AI 2027](ai-2027.md)
6. **Narayanan and Kapoor, "AI as Normal Technology" (2025), intro, Part I, and "Games provide misleading intuitions about the possibility of superintelligence."** Free, about 45 minutes. The counterweight, and a section aimed at a project like ours. → [AI Snake Oil](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md)

## Suggested order

TH's proposal: from the premise, to how games argue, to who is playing, to who designs, then the futures debate and its critics.

### 1. The premise: an AI with an arbitrary goal

- [Nick Bostrom, *Superintelligence* (2014)](bostrom-superintelligence.md): orthogonality, instrumental convergence, the treacherous turn, the singleton, the control problem (ch. 9 reads like a list of game cards). The 2003 paperclip essay and the ch. 8 quotes are in The Notebook at `_context/projects/50-in-focus/william-stafford/workshop-2/bostrom-paperclip-maximizer.md`.
- [Critiques of the *Superintelligence* picture](bostrom-critiques.md): Cegłowski, Mitchell, Pinker, Zador and LeCun, Thorstad.
- [Bostrom, *Deep Utopia* (2024)](bostrom-deep-utopia.md): the other ending. If control works, what is left for people to do? Bostrom's answer runs through games and "artificial purpose."

### 2. How games argue

- [Ian Bogost, *Persuasive Games* (2007)](bogost-persuasive-games.md): procedural rhetoric.
- [Bogost, *Unit Operations* (2006)](bogost-unit-operations.md): a game as a set of small units, and the "simulation gap" players fill. (unverified: book not opened)
- [Alexander R. Galloway, *Gaming* (2006)](galloway-gaming.md): the four moments of gamic action, including the machine's own acts; "to play the game means to play the code of the game" on *Civilization*.
- [Jesper Juul, *Half-Real* (2005)](juul-half-real.md): rules are real, fiction is imagined; emergence vs progression; the paradox of failure (in a game the humans may lose).

### 3. Who is playing: idle games and machines that play

- [Sonia Fizek, *Playing at a Distance* (2022)](fizek-playing-at-a-distance.md): idle games, delegated play, *Paperclips*.
- [Frank Lantz, *The Beauty of Games* (2023) and his writing on *Paperclips* and AI](lantz-games-thinking-about-thinking.md): games as thought made visible to itself (his heading; the phrase "thinking about thinking" was not found in his words, though the 2017 [depth paper](articles/lantz-et-al-2017-depth-in-strategic-games.md) comes close). The game itself is in GL's [Universal Paperclips](../paperclips/README.md).
- [McKenzie Wark, *Gamer Theory* (2007)](wark-gamer-theory.md): gamespace, and chapters on *The Sims*, *Civilization III*, *SimEarth*.
- [Julian Togelius, *Playing Smart* (2018)](togelius-playing-smart.md): games as where AI is tested and grown. The history is HI's, in [history](../history/README.md).

### 4. Who designs: agency, play, and critique

These three bear most on Marlon's 11:00 line, "the students need agency, and I'm not sure that playing an existing game we create is enough."

- [C. Thi Nguyen, *Games: Agency as Art* (2020)](nguyen-games-agency-as-art.md): agency is the medium of games; value capture.
- [Miguel Sicart, *Play Matters* (2014)](sicart-play-matters.md): play is appropriative; players take games over.
- [Mary Flanagan, *Critical Play* (2009)](flanagan-critical-play.md): unplaying, reskinning, rewriting; Values at Play and Grow-a-Game.
- [Bogost, *Play Anything* (2016)](bogost-play-anything.md): fun from working within limits. (unverified: book not opened)

### 5. Games of empire and the gamification critique

- [Nick Dyer-Witheford and Greig de Peuter, *Games of Empire* (2009)](dyer-witheford-de-peuter-games-of-empire.md): games, global capital, and labor; whose world the strategy game converts.
- [Bogost, *Cow Clicker* (2010) and the gamification critique](bogost-cow-clicker.md): satire made by building the thing; "exploitationware."

### 6. The futures debate: singularity, doom, utopia

- [I. J. Good (1965) and Vernor Vinge, "The Coming Technological Singularity" (1993)](vinge-singularity.md): the intelligence explosion and the end of the human era.
- [Ray Kurzweil, *The Singularity Is Near* (2005) and *Nearer* (2024)](kurzweil-singularity.md): accelerating returns, 2029 and 2045. (unverified: book pages not opened)
- [Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares, *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies* (2025)](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md): AIs are grown, not crafted; the call for a treaty. (unverified: quotes via reviews)
- [*AI 2027* (2025)](ai-2027.md): the race and slowdown endings, and the authors' later revisions.
- [Dario Amodei, "Machines of Loving Grace" (2024) and "The Adolescence of Technology" (2026)](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md): "a country of geniuses in a datacenter." Disclosure in the entry: Amodei runs Anthropic, which makes the model that wrote these entries.
- [Accelerationism and AI safety](accelerationism-and-ai-safety.md): e/acc and Andreessen; the pause letter, the extinction statement, *Situational Awareness*.

### 7. The critics of the frame

- [Timnit Gebru and Émile P. Torres, the TESCREAL bundle (2024)](tescreal.md), with "Stochastic Parrots" and *The AI Con*.
- [Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, *AI Snake Oil* (2024) and "AI as Normal Technology" (2025)](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md).

## Threads across the entries (TH's reading)

- **The arbitrary goal.** Bostrom's orthogonality, Nguyen's value capture, Lantz's game goals that need no why, and Juul's "valorization of outcomes" are four views of one thing the game puts in the AI's hands: a number it pursues for its own sake.
- **The machine plays.** Galloway's machine acts, Fizek's delegated play, Wark's *SimEarth*, and Togelius's AI players describe a game that goes on without the humans, which is also what async play is.
- **Rules as argument.** Bogost, Galloway, Juul, and Flanagan give the editor its structure: rules and fiction kept apart, each rule carrying the claim it makes, and rewriting as the way students take part.
- **Present harms against the turn.** Cegłowski, Gebru and Torres, Narayanan and Kapoor, and Dyer-Witheford and de Peuter all ask whether the climax should be a treacherous turn or the water, rent, labor, and mines spent along the way. Stafford's course on infrastructure (Perrow, Crawford) sits on this side.
