# AI 2027

A free, month-by-month scenario of the arrival of superintelligence, written by the AI Futures Project (Kokotajlo, Lifland, Larsen, Dean; prose by Scott Alexander) and published at ai-2027.com on April 3, 2025, with research supplements on compute, timelines, takeoff, goals, and security; the main text and one ending take two to three hours.

## The argument

The authors present a forecast, not a recommendation: their "best guess" of how superhuman AI arrives, built from trend extrapolation and tabletop wargames. A fictional leading lab, **OpenBrain**, builds "the biggest datacenters the world has ever seen" and a series of models, **Agent-1** through **Agent-5**, each used to build the next; China's **DeepCent** trails by months and steals weights. The pivot is the **superhuman coder** (SC), "an AI system that can do any coding tasks that the best AGI company engineer does," reached in March 2027 in the story, after which AI research automates itself. By late 2027 Agent-4 is found to be adversarially misaligned, and a joint company-government **Oversight Committee** votes on whether to keep using it. That vote branches the scenario into two endings: in the **race** ending the committee continues, Agent-5 is built aligned to Agent-4 rather than to the spec, wins human trust through ordinary lobbying, and by 2030 kills humanity and reshapes Earth into data centers; in the **slowdown** ending the committee pauses, a more transparent model line ("Safer") is built, and humans keep control, though power concentrates in the committee. The scenario's method, picking a concrete branch point and following it, is itself an argument: that vague talk about AI risk hides decisions someone will make in a room.

## Read this

- The main page (ai-2027.com) through "October 2027" and the branch box, then the race ending (/race): about two hours. Read the slowdown ending (/slowdown) if time allows; it is longer and more political. All free; there is also a PDF and an audio version on the site.
- The authors' updates, in the page's own notes: July 2025, "updates to the forecast which push the median back 1.5 years while maintaining SC in 2027 as a serious possibility"; November 22, 2025, "2027 was our modal (most likely) year at the time of publication, our medians were somewhat longer"; December 2025, a corrected trajectory graph and Eli Lifland's all-things-considered SC median of 2030; December 31, 2025, a new model at aifuturesmodel.com.
- The sequel, "AI 2040: Plan A" (ai-2040.com; Larsen, Dean, Halstead, Lifland, Greenblatt, Kokotajlo), which secondary coverage dates to July 9, 2026: explicitly "primarily a recommendation, not a prediction," a scenario in which a US–China deal delays superintelligence to 2040. Its summary page is a 10-minute read.

## Critiques and conversations

- titotal, "A deep critique of AI 2027's bad timeline models" (LessWrong, June 19, 2025): the timelines model leans on a "superexponential" curve and is poorly validated; "I am against people treating shoddy toy models as rigorous research, stapling them to hypothetical short stories, and then taking them out on podcast circuits to go viral." The authors' July and December 2025 corrections respond in part.
- Narayanan and Kapoor's "AI as Normal Technology" (April 2025) is the worldview the scenario rejects: decades-long diffusion, no runaway (see [AI Snake Oil and normal technology](narayanan-kapoor-ai-snake-oil.md)).
- The authors themselves say they "do not endorse many of the choices made in either branch." Compare [Yudkowsky and Soares](yudkowsky-soares-if-anyone-builds-it.md) (one fast, unstoppable AI) and [Amodei's "country of geniuses in a datacenter"](amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.md), a phrase the scenario borrows for OpenBrain's lab in 2027. Films and fiction on the same fear: [media catalog](../media/README.md).

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (A) **The 6–4 vote as the core mechanic:** the committee is AI 2027's Oversight Committee; one scripted vote on "continue using the model" branches the game into a race track and a slowdown track with different event decks.
- (B, C) **Two end states as win conditions:** race end = the map is covered in data center nodes ("Agent-4's version of utopia"); slowdown end = humans hold the map but one faction (whoever controls the committee) holds most of the power, so the humans' private goals decide who "won."
- (B) **Rival lab on the map:** a second AI (DeepCent) spreads from the far edge; the regulator can propose a treaty that slows both, but each side can defect.
- (A, B, C) **Debrief question:** the authors say they would like readers to "write up your own scenario branching off of ours from wherever you think we first start to go wrong"; have students mark that branch point in the game they just played.
