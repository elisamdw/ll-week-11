# The TESCREAL bundle: Eugenics and the promise of utopia through artificial general intelligence

Timnit Gebru (DAIR) and Émile P. Torres (Case Western Reserve University), an article in the open-access journal *First Monday* 29 (4), April 2024 (received 21 September 2023, accepted 10 March 2024); the PDF galley runs 42 pages, about 21 of them text and the rest notes and references. Licence, as printed at the end of the PDF: "This paper is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International License." **[The PDF](gebru-torres-2024-tescreal-bundle.pdf)**.

## What it is

The critique of the AI apocalypse as an ideology rather than an argument. Gebru and Torres ask why building **AGI** is treated as a goal no one has to justify, and answer with a genealogy: seven overlapping movements, the **TESCREAL bundle**, which they trace to twentieth-century eugenics through transhumanism (§§3–4, with a summary table on pp. 10–11 and a note on **effective accelerationism** as a later variant). Section 5 follows the money and the founders into the AGI labs. Section 6, the part that bears most on this game, makes four claims: AGI projects build **unscoped** systems that cannot be safety-tested (§6.1); they consume ever more data, compute, and energy and **centralize power** (§6.2); talking about AI "as if they exist on their own" lets companies **evade accountability** for workers and harms (§6.3); and the same groups use the language of **safety** both to fund the race and to deflect attention from its present harms (§6.4). Section 7 offers an engineer's alternative: well-scoped systems with specified operating conditions and stress tests. The sibling entry already quotes the eschatology passage (§4.2, p. 8); the segments below are other passages.

## Segments

**The thesis on safety** (abstract, p. 1):

> "We argue that, unlike systems with specific applications which can be evaluated following standard engineering principles, undefined systems like “AGI” cannot be appropriately tested for safety."

**Ignore the first thousand years** (§4.1, p. 7):

> "Longtermists Hilary Greaves and William MacAskill (2019) thus wrote that we may simply ignore “the effects contained in the first 100 (or even 1,000) years.”"

**Why build what could destroy us** (§4.2, p. 8):

> "Hence, developing these technologies was deemed necessary, but they potentially could destroy humanity."

> "However, many of these same prominent figures contend that the potential benefits of advanced technology are worth the extreme risks; building these technologies to bring about utopia should be our primary focus."

**Effective accelerationism** (§4.2, pp. 11–12):

> "Effective accelerationists believe that the probability of a bad outcome due to AGI is very low, and hence that “progress” toward increasingly “powerful” AI systems should be made to accelerate (Torres, 2023c)."

**The everything machine** (§6.1, p. 16):

> "Unlike the “narrow AI” systems that TESCREALists lamented the field of AI was focused on, attempting to build something akin to an everything machine results in systems that are unscoped and therefore inherently unsafe, as one cannot design appropriate tests to determine what the systems should and should not be used for."

**Resources and power** (§6.2, p. 16):

> "The AGI race fueled by the TESCREAList goal to build “transhuman minds” (Goertzel, 2010) and bring about “unlimited intelligence” has also resulted in systems that consume more and more resources in terms of data and compute power. This leads to a high environmental impact, increases the risks that arise due to the lack of appropriate scoping discussed earlier, and results in the centralization of power among a handful of entities."

**Siphoned away** (§6.2, p. 17):

> "Resources that could go to many entities around the world, each building computational systems that serve the needs of specific communities, are being siphoned away to a handful of corporations trying to build AGI."

**As if they exist on their own** (§6.3, p. 18):

> "When organizations advertise their systems as a step toward AGI, or when researchers ask if machines can learn “morality” and whether they “understand us” (Agüera y Arcas, 2022; Jiang, et al., 2022), they move attention away from organizations’ responsibility to create products with certain requirements, or protecting the wellbeing of the workers involved in the process, to discussions of AI systems as if they exist on their own (Tucker, 2022)."

**Safety, twice** (§6.4, p. 20):

> "Thus, TESCREALists use the language of “safety” to first drive resources into the goal of building AGI, which in turn causes harm to marginalized groups, and then use the language of “safety” to dissuade investigations into those harms and once again divert resources into preventing a hypothetical AGI apocalypse."

**Drop testing** (§7, p. 20):

> "Some of the tests that we performed as part of our work included drop testing, constantly dropping devices to understand the manner in which their functionality degrades when they are exposed to shocks [125], placing the devices in extremely cold or hot environments (International Electrotechnical Commission, 2020), frequently restarting them [126], and performing different types of tests to understand the behavior of these systems under conditions that they were not normally meant to operate in."

**Not inevitable** (§7, p. 21):

> "The AGI race is not an inevitable, unstoppable march towards technological progress, grounded in careful scientific and engineering principles (van Rooij, et al., 2023)."

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B, debrief) **"As if they exist on their own" (§6.3):** the game's theme is an AI running on its own; give the AI's deck a visible owner (the company faction), and at the debrief ask who decided each AI move, the deck or the people who wrote and funded it.
- (B) **Siphon rule (§6.2):** every data center the AI places removes a small local project card (a clinic's model, a translation tool, a co-op's server) from the same node, so the map records what the race displaced, not only what it built.
- (B, editor) **Drop test for rulesets (§7):** before a student ruleset is saved in `/game/edit`, run it under three off-normal conditions (a heat wave, a grid outage, a rival AI) and record in the rhetoric field what broke; a ruleset with no stated operating conditions cannot be saved.
- (B) **Safety funding card (§6.4):** the company faction can play "safety" to gain funding; the same card also raises the AI's compute, so a safety move both slows and feeds the race.
