# Playing Games Across the Superintelligence Divide

Aaron Isaksen, Julian Togelius, Frank Lantz, and Andy Nealen (NYU Game Innovation Lab and NYU Game Center), a nine-page workshop paper at AAAI-16, *AI, Ethics, and Society* (WS-16-02), pp. 89–97. © 2016 AAAI; stored here for study. **[The PDF](isaksen-et-al-2016-superintelligence-divide.pdf)**.

## What it is

The paper asks what games a superintelligence would choose to play with us. It reasons by analogy from the games we play with those less skilled than us: **adult and child, expert and novice, human and animal**. It sorts each by the relation at its core: **friendly, learning, observational, domination**. The twelve cells of that grid (Table 1, p. 90) run from peek-a-boo and handicapped Go to birdwatching, griefing, and bullfighting. The warning is the bottom row: domination games, which the authors say "would pose a significant risk to humanity." Frank Lantz made *Universal Paperclips* the following year; this is the same group thinking about AI and play before the game.

## Segments

**The analogy** (abstract, p. 89):

> "We assume a superintelligence, because of its greater cognitive ability, would stand in a similar relation to us as an adult does to a child, an expert to a novice, or a human to an animal. We define friendly games, learning games, observational games, and domination games, and proceed to consider games adults play with children, experts play with novices, and humans play with animals."

**For the skeptic** (p. 90):

> "For the purposes of this paper, we hope a superintelligence skeptic can mentally replace the terms “superintelligence” with “highly-functioning and deeply-integrated artificial intelligence” and find our arguments still valid."

**Table 1** (p. 90), the twelve cells, transcribed:

| | Adult–Child | Expert–Novice | Human–Animal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Friendly | Peek-a-boo, Tea Party, Hide & Seek, Building Blocks | Handicapping in Go or Golf | Hamster Wheels, Dog Toys, Polo, Racing |
| Learning | Candy Land, Tic-Tac-Toe, Tee-Ball, Chutes & Ladders, Trivial Pursuit for Kids | Tutoring, Mentoring, Coaching | Dog Tricks |
| Observational | Playground Park Bench, Spectating Kids Sports, Psychological Studies | Spectating Professional Sports | Birdwatching, Dog Park, Fish Tank |
| Domination | Forcing Play, Tickling | Player Killing, Griefing | Bull Fighting, Fishing, Hunting, Rodeo, Biotic Games |

**The four relations defined** (p. 90):

> "Domination games are those where one party exerts dominance over the other, such that the dominated player is not enjoying the experience and may have no choice but to participate, and where the dominating player may be inflicting mental or physical pain and suffering (and possibly even death in the case of some games with animals)."

**Why it might play with us** (p. 91):

> "It is worth stressing that though the superintelligences are more intelligent than we are, they may still be as stumped in trying to predict our behavior in detail as we frequently are when observing much simpler organisms."

**Games that persuade us** (p. 93), on an AI that designs games to improve our behavior:

> "But once we begin to submit ourselves to this kind of game we may lose the ability to distinguish between our genuinely held values and those our AI companions want us to have, at which point something important will have been lost."

**The paperclip move, made with games** (p. 94):

> "A seed AI that was originally programmed to win as many games as possible might force all living humans to constantly play games against it: this might be the best way to win as often as possible. As with greed play, the machine isn’t necessarily trying to punish us, but our welfare is less important to the superintelligence than its own."

**A game design, in passing** (p. 95), on centaur play:

> "Imagine, for example, a game of complex resource management with alliances and political negotiation, played by 100 humans each with an advanced AI companion whispering advice in their ear. The AIs’ ability to navigate the complex problem-space will be refracted through the essentially human qualities of charisma, persuasion, duplicity, and trust."

**Resources** (p. 96), one reason a superintelligence might hunt us:

> "Another possibility is that humans own or control resources which are desired by the artificial intelligence; this is analogous to eating an animal killed while hunting or fishing."

**Conclusion** (p. 96):

> "Simply inheriting existing human ethics will not be sufficient, as shown by analogy with how humans currently play domination games against children, novices, and animals."

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B, C) **The p. 95 game as a variant:** each human faction gets an AI advisor card that whispers a best move each round; the faction can follow it or not, and the advisors all belong to the AI whose data centers are spreading.
- (A, B) **Table 1 as a deck:** the AI's stance toward the humans is drawn from the twelve cells and changes when its compute crosses a threshold (friendly → learning → observational → domination), so the treacherous turn becomes a move down the table's rows.
- (A) **Committee proposal "Games for your own good":** the AI offers a game that will improve the humans' behavior (p. 93); approving it raises trust and output, and the debrief asks whose values the game was teaching.
- (all, debrief) **Which cell were we in?** After play, each player places the game they just played on Table 1, from the humans' side and from the AI's.
