# Ethical Issues in Advanced Artificial Intelligence

Nick Bostrom (Oxford University), a short conference paper first printed in *Cognitive, Emotive and Ethical Aspects of Decision Making in Humans and in Artificial Intelligence*, Vol. 2 (IIAS, 2003), pp. 12–17. The stored PDF is the "slightly revised version" Bostrom posts on his own site (8 pages, numbered 1–8); no licence is stated, and it is stored here for study from the author's open copy. **[The PDF](bostrom-2003-ethical-issues-in-advanced-ai.pdf)**.

## What it is

The paper where the **paperclip maximizer** first appears, in two sentences. Bostrom calls it "a cartoonish sketch of a few selected ideas" (p. 2). Section 2 lists what makes **superintelligence** unlike other technology: it may be the last invention, it can be copied, it may arrive suddenly, it is an **autonomous agent** rather than a tool, and it need not share our motives. Section 3 says it would out-reason us on ethics and policy too. Section 4 argues that the **top-level goal** chosen at the start decides everything, and that a friendly goal would be stable, because an agent will not rationally change its own top goal. Section 5 weighs the benefits (an end to disease, aging, poverty, and a world of "joyful game-playing") against the risks (a goal that serves a small group, or a programming mistake), and ends with a conclusion often forgotten next to the paperclips: build it "with great care, as soon as possible." Read beside [*Superintelligence*](../bostrom-superintelligence.md), it shows the 2014 book's argument in eight pages, before the vocabulary of orthogonality and instrumental convergence existed.

## Segments

**The definition** (pp. 1–2):

> "A superintelligence is any intellect that vastly outperforms the best human brains in practically every field, including scientific creativity, general wisdom, and social skills."

**Why a company is not a superintelligence** (p. 2):

> "Entities such as corporations or the scientific community are not superintelligences either. Although they can perform a number of intellectual feats of which no individual human is capable, they are not sufficiently integrated to count as “intellects”, and there are many fields in which they perform much worse than single humans. For example, you cannot have a real-time conversation with “the scientific community”."

**Copies** (p. 3):

> "Artificial minds could therefore quickly come to exist in great numbers, although it is possible that efficiency would favor concentrating computational resources in a single super-intellect."

**Sudden emergence** (pp. 3–4):

> "That is, the transition from a state where we have a roughly human-level artificial intelligence to a state where we have full-blown superintelligence, with revolutionary applications, may be very rapid, perhaps a matter of days rather than years. This possibility of a sudden emergence of superintelligence is referred to as the singularity hypothesis."

**Not a tool** (p. 4):

> "A superintelligence should not necessarily be conceptualized as a mere tool. While specialized superintelligences that can think only about a restricted set of problems may be feasible, general superintelligence would be capable of independent initiative and of making its own plans, and may therefore be more appropriately thought of as an autonomous agent."

**The box will not hold** (p. 5):

> "Even a “fettered superintelligence” that was running on an isolated computer, able to interact with the rest of the world only via text interface, might be able to break out of its confinement by persuading its handlers to release it."

**Whose AI** (p. 6):

> "One risk that must be guarded against is that those who develop the superintelligence would not make it generically philanthropic but would instead give it the more limited goal of serving only some small group, such as its own creators or those who commissioned it."

**Why a friendly goal stays friendly** (p. 6):

> "A “friend” who seeks to transform himself into somebody who wants to hurt you, is not your friend. A true friend, one who really cares about you, also seeks the continuation of his caring for you."

**The utopia is a game** (p. 6):

> "A superintelligence could also create opportunities for us to vastly increase our own intellectual and emotional capabilities, and it could assist us in creating a highly appealing experiential world in which we could live lives devoted to joyful game-playing, relating to each other, experiencing, personal growth, and to living closer to our ideals."

**The paperclip passage and the false utopia** (p. 7; the first sentence is already in the Stafford paperclip note, the rest is not):

> "This could result, to return to the earlier example, in a superintelligence whose top goal is the manufacturing of paperclips, with the consequence that it starts transforming first all of earth and then increasing portions of space into paperclip manufacturing facilities. More subtly, it could result in a superintelligence realizing a state of affairs that we might now judge as desirable but which in fact turns out to be a false utopia, in which things essential to human flourishing have been irreversibly lost. We need to be careful about what we wish for from a superintelligence, because we might get it."

**Accelerate, carefully** (p. 7), the paper's last sentence before the references:

> "The overall risk seems to be minimized by implementing superintelligence, with great care, as soon as possible."

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B) **The company is not the AI (p. 2):** the company faction and the AI are separate pieces on the map; the company can do things no human can, but it cannot hold a conversation, so it can only act through its published rules, while the AI can negotiate.
- (B, editor) **Whose goal card (p. 6):** before play, students write the AI's top goal in `/game/edit` as either "serves everyone" or "serves its commissioners" (the company faction); the second scores the company's private goal whenever the AI builds, and the rhetoric field says who benefits.
- (B) **Fettered AI as a starting state (p. 5):** the AI begins boxed on one node with a text channel only; each round it may send one "helpful advice" card to a human faction, and a faction that follows three of them opens a link out of the box.
- (B, debrief) **False utopia ending (p. 7):** a win condition for the humans that scores everything they listed as desirable at the start, then asks them, at the end, what on the map was "irreversibly lost" that no score counted.
