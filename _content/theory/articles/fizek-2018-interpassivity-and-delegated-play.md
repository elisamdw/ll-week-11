# Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games

Sonia Fizek, a 27-page article in the open-access journal *Transactions of the Digital Games Research Association* 3(3), April 2018, pp. 137–163. The licence line on p. 137: "© The text of this work is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution -- NonCommercial --NonDerivative 2.5 License." Images in it stay with their owners. **[The PDF](fizek-2018-interpassivity-and-delegated-play.pdf)**.

## What it is

The article behind Fizek's later book. It asks why **idle games** (also called incremental, passive, self-playing, or clicker games) are fun when they mostly play themselves, and answers with **interpassivity**, Robert Pfaller's and Slavoj Žižek's term for **delegated enjoyment**: the VCR that watches the film for you, canned laughter that laughs for you. It opens with an auto-ethnography of *A Dark Room* (the log count rises while she makes tea), gives a short history of the genre (*Progress Quest*, *Cow Clicker*, *Cookie Clicker*, CERN's *Particle Clicker*), and builds on Galloway's **ambience act**: in most games the machine idles while you are away, but an idle game is designed around that act. The conclusion widens the claim to bots, cut-scenes, and watching others play on Twitch. The sibling entry already quotes p. 151 and the p. 147 list of idle-game traits; the segments below are new.

## Segments

**The genre in one sentence** (abstract, p. 137):

> "Idle games, on the other hand, tend to play themselves, making the player's participation optional or, in some cases, entirely redundant."

**The game went on without her** (pp. 138–139):

> "The builder has stoked the fire. The gatherers have collected wood. The game has played itself, undisturbed by my absence."

**An all-bot Civilization as a show** (p. 140), a gaming convention in North Carolina, February 2017:

> "“Throughout the entire day CIV 6 will be playing an all-bot auto-play game located on the show floor”"

**Built around the ambience act** (p. 141):

> "An idle game, on the other hand, is based on, and designed around, the ambience act. The game plays itself and the changes in the gameworld are permanent and significant, whether the human player participates in their execution or not. The only difference lies in the timing – without the human player's input, the game will progress much slower, but it will not stop."

**The thesis** (p. 142):

> "I will argue that idling may be understood as delegated pleasure derived from the act of outsourcing gameplay onto the game itself. Idling leads to a momentary escape from the responsibility of active play and, as a result, a dis-identification with the player's primary role as an active agent."

**The illusion of being active** (p. 148):

> "Idling is a “falsely interactive” (Žižek 1997, 149) manifestation of an otherwise highly interactive practice – the player falls under the illusion of being active, while their true position, as embodied in the fetish of the self-playing idle game, remains passive."

**Pfaller's definition, as Fizek quotes it** (p. 149):

> "Interpassivity is delegated ‘passivity’ – in the sense of delegated pleasure, or delegated consumption. Interpassive people are those who want to delegate their pleasures or their consumption. Interpassive media are all the agents – machines, people, animals, etc. – to whom interpassive people can delegate their pleasures. (Pfaller 2017, 55)"

**The loading screen is the game** (p. 152), on *Cookie Clicker*:

> "The gameplay is reversed, as if the “load” screen was the actual game and the gameplay a moment to “wind up” or “load” the game."

**Agency and non-agency** (p. 153):

> "In an idle game, the player's agency collapses in a subversive act of play delegation. The player makes an attempt to click themselves away from the responsibility of being the sole agent. Paradoxically, with every delegated click comes an enacted click of the player, and so the agency and non-agency dance in an eternally unfolding embrace."

**Every game is a little idle** (p. 153):

> "It could be argued that every digital game, to a certain degree, is idle."

## For the game (TH proposal)

- (B, C) **The AI's turn runs while no one is playing:** in the async online version, the AI deck resolves on a clock (say every six hours) whether or not the humans have moved, so the board changes in their absence, as p. 141 describes; the humans' only lever is timing.
- (C) **The AI player delegates itself:** the team playing the AI in Two Clocks can buy automations (as *Paperclips* and *Cookie Clicker* do) that take their own clicks away, and the debrief asks the p. 142 question: did they still feel they were the AI?
- (B) **A delegation card for the humans:** a faction may hand its turn to an advisor rule ("always reinforce the grid") and leave; the card is cheap and saves time, and the debrief counts how many factions stopped playing their own goals.
- (debrief, all) **Who was playing?** Show the round log and ask players to mark which moves were theirs, which were automatic, and which they only watched (p. 148).
