**What it is.** The second half of the learning-harness take-home, twenty-five minutes, in pairs (or solo at home).

**How it plays.** Open a partner's graph as a game. Route A runs the LL's game on their folder (`/learn/mine`); route B asks Claude in their folder to build a small page that reads `graph/` and shows it. The ideas they explained to Claude are already lit; the partner who didn't learn them tries to unlock the next node. Then pick one remix and ask Claude for it, naming the file (route A) or describing it (route B): a boss at the goal that takes three right answers in a row; spacing by the calendar, so ideas fade back to shaky when you stay away; the map reskinned as a dungeon, a city or a constellation; a two-player duel on one graph. End with one sentence each: what does the remix reward, and is that what learning is?

**What it does for learning.** A learning game is a view drawn from a record of learning, and every view argues something: that learning unlocks, that it is a path, that forgetting costs. Changing one mechanic changes the argument, with Claude Code as the editor. HB's cautions: remixes should change the game, not the graph (the game reads the graph and shouldn't rewrite ideas); partners shouldn't answer for each other, since the answers go into one person's record; a route B page opened as a file loads nothing until Claude starts a local server.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** Marlon has said nothing about this activity itself. CM's reading: it is where his framing of the harness as this week's game (CI's card [The learning harness is this week's game](the-learning-harness-is-this-weeks-game.md)) meets the week's remix activity, [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md), and the Nguyen-and-Bogost question of whether a game gives choices or makes an argument.
