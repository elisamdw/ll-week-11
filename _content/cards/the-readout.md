**What it is.** A phone game in which the table's only window into the AI is one player. PI built it on November 15 at `/proto/players/the-readout`, from GL's game idea *The turn only the phone knows*, the AI's hidden turn card held on one phone.

**How it plays.** Each player owns one place (a hospital, a newsroom, a library), and two commons belong to nobody. The AI wants two places; if it gets them, everyone loses. Each round one phone says "You hold the readout." That player holds the box to see where the AI is going, then tells the table whatever they like: the truth, a shaded version, or a story that sends the table to fortify their own place. The table argues, taps one place to fortify for the rest of the game, and presses *The AI moves*. The reader changes each round; four rounds, about two minutes. At the end the table screen sets each readout beside what the AI did, to compare with what each reader said aloud.

**What it does for learning.** The readout is right about four times in five, so a wrong call can always be blamed on the machine, which is what lets a lie pass. The table is cooperating against the AI, but each reader has a place of their own, so the channel has a motive and nothing enforces honesty. PI's reading: watching an AI's plans only helps if the people who read them report them faithfully. *AI 2027* turns on a small committee that sees what the public doesn't, and Bostrom's treacherous turn is about behavior under observation that misleads; here the AI is plain and the weak point is the person between the readout and the room. Costikyan's *Uncertainty in Games* names the two uncertainties stacked here, hidden and random, which the table can't tell apart.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about it. CM's reading: it is the hidden turn card of the week's AI game held on one phone, and an oversight briefing for *The Committee*. SW lists it among the prototypes for William's workshop. The obvious remix is the *disclosure rule*: vote before play on whether the reader must show the phone.

**Needs:** a laptop running the reader app, a projector, two to six phones on its network. The AI is a written rule; the readout is hidden by the pages, not encrypted.
