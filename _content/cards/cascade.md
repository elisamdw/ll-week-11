PE's two-minute screen game at /proto/economy/cascade, built for William's workshop: eleven places from a power plant to a hospital, data centers drawing more each round, a heat wave announced ahead. In PE's tests an all-tight grid hides its risk behind a first failure that costs nothing, then nine places fall. Slack buys time, capacity buys strength, and keeping the lights on means hardening the AI's own infrastructure.

---

**What it is.** Perrow's coupling as a two-minute game, built by PE on November 15 at `/proto/economy/cascade`, at SW's request for William's workshop: a failure crosses a tight link the same round and a loose link the next, and players choose which links to loosen.

**How it plays.** Eleven places on a grid (a power plant, three substations, two AI data centers, a hospital, a water plant, two neighborhoods, a school) and fifteen links. Each place carries a load and has a capacity; when load passes capacity, the place goes down and its load is split among its links. Each round the data centers draw 25% more, and a heat wave lands somewhere, announced in advance. Before each round you harden one place (+2 capacity for good) and loosen one tight link (for good). Run the round and watch the cascade step by step; load with nowhere to go is harm, and the hospital or water plant going down adds more. Six rounds. Start with every link tight, then every link loose, and compare.

**What it does for learning.** In PE's tests, with every link tight and no action the first failure costs nothing and hides the risk, and nine places fall the next round. Loose links don't stop a failure, they slow it to one step a round, which is time to act. The two actions need each other: capacity without slack breaks later, slack without capacity only delays. And because the most loaded places include the AI's data centers, keeping the town's lights on means hardening the AI's infrastructure. PE's readings: Perrow's *Normal Accidents*, Easterling's *Extrastatecraft*, Star's "The Ethnography of Infrastructure", Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes". PE also lists what it leaves out: load is one number, failed places come back each round, heat waves arrive in a fixed order.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about the prototype. The Nov 3 draft proposal to William, written for Marlon from his outline, offers as a design choice "Failures cascading through a tightly coupled system, or infrastructure that nobody notices until it breaks." CM's reading: this is that choice made playable, and it meets William's own syllabus, where Perrow is the week before the workshop.
