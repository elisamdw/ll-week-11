A home game was the boxed edition of a TV game show, sold from the late 1950s and given to losing contestants. For the LL it is a pattern: take something that runs in the studio, with the LL's people and gear, and make a version that goes home with the people who were there.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Game-show marketing. RS found 22 old ads; the most quoted line is from "Weird Al" Yankovic's "I Lost on Jeopardy" (1984), where the loser won't even get "a lousy copy of our home game."

> But then we should also think about other home game versions of things we can do.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated.

**This week.** The first is for Terry Gipson's game show "Are You Serious?!" in EXPOS 40, after the in-studio sessions were canceled.

> we have canceled for good. we will create a 'home game' version as a nextjs app next week and send this.

Marlon, Nov 13, 3:16 PM, relayed by MZ. By Nov 16 it carries three needs: accessibility standards, running in rooms across campus, and physical parts to source.
