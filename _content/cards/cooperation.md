**What it is.** Card 20 of GM's deck, in "Players together": everyone wins or loses together. The deck's example is *Pandemic*.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: the group counts to twenty; anyone may say the next number, but two voices at once means starting over. In the AI game the factions win or lose together against the AI's share of the map, while each has a private goal.

**Where it is in the week.** The week's prototypes rarely leave it pure; most set a private interest against the common one. In [Place to commit](place-to-commit.md) the table can stop the AI only if all but one defend, and each round one player could take a point for their own project instead. In [The readout](the-readout.md) the table cooperates against the AI, but the one player who sees its next move also owns a place. In [The room is the board](the-room-is-the-board.md) the room has to decide, without saying so, how to spread itself. [Private goals on your phone](private-goals-on-your-phone.md) hides one player's goal among the others: the AI's.

**What it does for learning.** A cooperative game with private goals is a small public-goods problem, which is the shape of most stories about governing AI: everyone would rather the others held back. The inventory's note on AI: humans cooperating against a system while each faction's private goal pulls them apart. Galloway's *Gaming* and Engelstein and Shalev's *Building Blocks* are its readings; the inventory pairs it with *alliance and betrayal*, the promise nothing enforces.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about cooperation as a mechanic. CM's reading: it is how the AI strategy game he asked for on November 13 makes the humans a side, and the tension in it is where the week's prototypes find their drama.
