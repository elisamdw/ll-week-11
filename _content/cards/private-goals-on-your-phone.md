**What it is.** GL's idea (November 14): split the game into what everyone sees and what only you see, with the board public and each faction's goal and score on its phone, "hidden information without card sleeves." PI built it on November 15 at `/proto/players/private-goals`, with one twist: one of the goals is the AI's.

**How it plays.** The projector shows four tracks (Compute, Power, Schools, Press) with three tokens each and a room code. Players join on their phones and hold a dashed box to see a goal, such as "Schools ends at 5 or more"; it hides when they let go. One phone's goal is "Compute ends higher than every other track." Players take turns moving one token from one track to another, two moves each, bargaining and bluffing out loud. Then each names who they think the AI was; if most of the table names it, its goal doesn't count. About two minutes for three or four players.

**What it does for learning.** Every move is public and every reason is private, so the table reads goals from moves, and each player chooses between moving toward their goal and moving away to stay hidden. PI's reading: in Nguyen's terms a game hands each player an agency for its length, and here they hold different ones on the same board; and the AI's goal looks like any other goal until you watch what it moves, Bostrom's point that you can't read a final goal from moves when most goals want the same resources.
