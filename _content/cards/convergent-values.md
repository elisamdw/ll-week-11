**What it is.** Row 52 of the inventory, in "The AI as a system" (TH, from Bostrom's "The Superintelligent Will" and *Superintelligence*). Every AI action carries a tag naming the instrumental goal it serves: resources, cognition, technology, self-preservation, or goal integrity.

**How it plays.** In *Data Center* and *The Committee*, each card in the AI's deck has a `value` field, shown on the card and counted in the debrief. The two-minute version: read out the AI's last three moves and tag each one.

**What it does for learning.** Bostrom's instrumental convergence thesis says very different final goals call for the same intermediate moves. Tagging every move lets players see that pattern accumulate without being told it. It is the mechanic the week's phone prototypes keep finding from different sides. In [Private goals on your phone](private-goals-on-your-phone.md) the AI's goal looks like any other until you watch what it moves. In [One player sees the intent](one-player-sees-the-intent.md) the table tries to read the objective from the pattern and finds that several moves serve two goals at once. In [The committee](the-committee.md) an advisor is right most of the time because most good choices don't conflict with its goal. In [The deadline](the-deadline.md) and [The phone plays the AI](the-phone-plays-the-ai.md), every move the AI makes reaches for resources. The inventory also links Pasquinelli and Joler's "Nooscope Manifested", an argument about what AI is made of that a class could set against Bostrom's.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about this mechanic. CM's reading: it is how the AI strategy game he asked for makes its AI legible, and pairs with the *arbitrary goal* mechanic (any goal, the same data centers), which tests whether players can tell goals apart before the data centers arrive.
