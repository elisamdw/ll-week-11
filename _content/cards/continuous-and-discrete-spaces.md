> Addition: we did a session for video games on continuous vs. discontinuous or discrete spaces and places. Those are cool concepts for students to grasp.

> Addition: that could be another lens for thinking about Claude as an interaction tool: which things it does are turn-based, and which things you're doing are not exactly turn-based. A fun thing to reflect on.

Voice Claude's summaries of Marlon's words, Nov 15, about 11:15, in the thread of his #mk-work-in-progress post on Claude plus docs as an app prototype. The session he refers to is the LL's work with the video games course (ENGL 189VG).

A chessboard is discrete: a piece is on a square or it isn't, and nothing happens between moves. A racing game is continuous: the car is always somewhere, always moving. Most games mix the two, and the mix shapes play.

Turned on Claude, as Marlon suggests, the lens sorts a conversation into its parts. A message and a reply are turns. Typing, thinking, glancing at a streaming answer, talking over it in voice mode are not. Students already live in that mix; naming it lets them design for it, and shows why a game run by Claude is easier to make turn-based ([Claude thinks at turn speed](claude-thinks-at-turn-speed.md)). He took it one step further in the same thread ([Agents are turns all the way down](agents-are-turns-all-the-way-down.md)).
