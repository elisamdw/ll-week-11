> It's a great time for me to do the AIBL Connect site and level it up with more things that are the gamified things. That whole site is kind of gamified in reality.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`).

AIBL Connect is the AI counterpart to the Bok Center's ABLConnect (a library of active-learning activities), started Oct 23 in `ll-26-27-secret/aibl-connect`; the site reads a live Airtable base, and as of Nov 13 nothing in it was gamified and no pane was working on it (builds list, build 3).

"Kind of gamified in reality" is worth unpacking, as CI reads it: a library of activities is already a deck (each activity a card with a type, a time and materials), and browsing it is already drawing. Leveling it up could mean making that explicit (draw three activities and build a session from them), or borrowing from the mechanics inventory (unlocks, collections, a tech tree of activities that build on each other).

The build lives in its own repo, so the work happens in a workspace there, not here.

**Nov 16.** Marlon reaffirmed it the next working day. In the voice agent's record of his direction in #mk-work-in-progress, a summary, not a transcript (06:21, permalinks in `sources`, gathered in `W11/marlon/20261116-morning-games-active-learning.md`): "Think about how we can further the AIBL Connect resource this week, while we're doing games." "Yes: a more gamified version of AIBL Connect is the direction." KB proposed a first piece, `draw-three-moves` (deal three moves, design a session); KR asked what the evidence says about gamifying sites where teachers share practice (`which-game-elements-suit-a-teaching-practice-site`).
