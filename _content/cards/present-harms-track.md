**What it is.** Row 56 of the inventory, in "Goals and the loop" (TH, from Maciej Cegłowski's talk "Superintelligence: The Idea That Eats Smart People"; also Narayanan and Kapoor). A second score beside the AI's progress: a track of today's harms, such as water bills, layoffs and outages, that the players can lose on even while they hold the AI back.

**How it plays.** The two-minute version: keep two scores on the board, how far the AI got and what it cost the town on the way. In the AI game it is the `harm` track in every version: shortages and the AI's upkeep raise it, and it is a second way to lose.

**Where it is in the week.** In [Prices that move](prices-that-move.md), what the hospital, the lab and the co-op can't buy counts as harm, and the game ends when harm reaches 8. In [Cascade](cascade.md), load with nowhere to go is harm, and the hospital or water plant going down adds 3 more. SW's [Every resource names a place](every-resource-names-a-place.md) makes the people at a drained place feel it first, on this track.

**What it does for learning.** It splits the AI game's argument in two, which is where much of the public argument about AI is split. Cegłowski's point, as PE carries it into *Prices that move*: you can lose to today's shortages while watching for tomorrow's superintelligence. A table that wins on the AI's share and loses on harm has played that argument. The inventory pairs it with *profitable harm*, the AI's buying seen from the seller's side, where every unit it takes is a sale.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about the track. CM's reading: it holds the doomer and present-harms sides of his week 12 theme of autonomy and apocalypses, doomerism and accelerationism (CI's card [Autonomy as the Stafford game's theme](autonomy-as-the-stafford-games-theme.md)), inside one game.
