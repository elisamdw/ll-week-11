Agency is the player's power to act and to see the act matter. Designers shape it: they decide what kind of agent a player gets to be. One of the week's claims is that a game heightens agency, asking for more decisions, about more crucial things, over longer chains of cause and effect than ordinary life does.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Janet Murray: "the satisfying power to take meaningful action and see the results of our decisions and choices" (*Hamlet on the Holodeck*, 1997, as quoted in Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009). C. Thi Nguyen, *Games: Agency as Art* (2020), treats agency as the medium games are made in.

**This week.** Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction on Nov 15 gives agency measures: decisions per second, the stakes of a decision, how much a player holds in mind, how long the causal chains are. Each could be a dial a designer turns. In the AI game, choosing whether to harden or protest is agency; the AI's spread, which the players don't choose, is not theirs.
