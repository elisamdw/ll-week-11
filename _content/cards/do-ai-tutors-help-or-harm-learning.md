Marlon's question on Nov 15 was what AI disrupts, and "clearly one of the main things is the teacher." Randomized trials of chat tutors since 2024 have found large gains in some designs and harm in others. What did they find, and what separated the two?

---

**Why the week needs it.** The learning harnesses are this week's game ([The learning harness is this week's game](the-learning-harness-is-this-weeks-game.md)), and [Who needs a teacher?](who-needs-a-teacher.md) is open. `_content/learning/` catalogs the tools and their history but not the trials. A faculty audience will ask for the evidence.

**What would answer it.** The main randomized studies with their settings and effect sizes: the Harvard physics AI-tutor trial (Kestin et al.), the Turkish high-school math study where unguarded GPT access lowered later scores (Bastani et al.), the World Bank's Nigeria pilot, any 2025–26 replication; what the helpful tutors had in common (guardrails, hints instead of answers, a course-specific design).

**Where to look.** *Scientific Reports*, *PNAS*, the World Bank blog and working papers, SSRN and arXiv, Google Scholar citations of each.
