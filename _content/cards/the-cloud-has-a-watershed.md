**The readings.** Kate Crawford's *Atlas of AI* (2021) argues that AI is an extractive industry and follows it back to mines, energy, water and underpaid labor; chapter 1, "Earth", is starred in the LL's Stafford reading set. Mél Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes" (2015) reads the NSA's Utah Data Center through its water: the facility cools servers in one of the driest states, so the cloud has a watershed. (TH's summaries, from the bibliography.) Neither is on William's syllabus; they are in the LL's suggested set. His course has already read Monserrate on server heat, Mattern on the deep time of media infrastructure, Nyckel on Amazon's anticipatory shipping, and Pasquinelli and Joler's Nooscope, so students have the vocabulary.

**What they give the week's games.** SW's proposal: every resource on the board names a place or a group. Not "Energy 6" but "the gas plant on the river" and "the night shift." When the AI builds, the cost lands somewhere on the map, often far from the data center. Server heat could be a track of its own, after Monserrate. SW's caution: numbers invented for a game get read as data, so any figure on a card should be labeled as the game's.

**Where it is played.** [Every resource names a place](every-resource-names-a-place.md) is the proposal as a mechanic. PE's [Leaks](leaks.md) cites both readings: the company's dashboard shows compute while the costs land on an aquifer and farms it can't see. [Prices that move](prices-that-move.md) puts Crawford's materials on a price curve with the AI first in line.
