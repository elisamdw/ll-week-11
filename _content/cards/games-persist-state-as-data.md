On Nov 14, in the voice chat, Marlon asked whether any of the readings treat a database as the back end of a game, and then made it concrete. In voice Claude's summary (handoff back, section 4): building a video game means persisting data; NPCs and mobs are instances of classes; in *Breath of the Wild* a horse has standard properties, and as you customize it those properties are stored. He tied it to

> that kind of Lev Manovich theory of database.

and named *Civilization* and *Universal Paperclips* as the examples to look at.

The idea turns a game inside out for students who have only played games. Behind the horse there is a record; behind the world, a table of records; behind a turn, a change to some of them. Manovich's "Database as Symbolic Form" (1999) argues that the database is a cultural form of its own, set against narrative; a game is where the two meet, a story told by changing a database.

Voice Claude's additions, which CI passes on as notes, not Marlon's: the standard horse is a *template* and your horse with its name and saddle is an *instance*; William's students' index cards could be the template layer, with play creating instances; and *Breath of the Wild* is offline, so the horse lives in a save file rather than on a server. Template and instance went into [the glossary](a-little-glossary.md).

The Nov 14 workspace wrote game-state pages for seven games (`_content/game-state/`); CM's [Photo to game state, then read a save file](photo-to-game-state-then-read-a-save-file.md) is the class activity built on this.
