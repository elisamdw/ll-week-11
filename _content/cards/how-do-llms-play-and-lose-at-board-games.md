Wednesday's class seats Claude at a table game as opponent, advisor and rule-following faction, and asks whether anyone tried to trick it. The history the week has (Deep Blue, AlphaGo, the Go blind spot) is about game engines, not language models. What do recent studies say about how language models play, and how they fail?

---

**Why the week needs it.** [Claude takes a seat](claude-takes-a-seat.md) is the agreed swap in Session A, and [AI as a player](ai-as-a-player.md) gives the engine history but nothing on chat models. Students will see Claude make illegal moves or lose track of the board; the teacher should know which of those failures are documented and common.

**What would answer it.** Three or four benchmarks or matches with their numbers (illegal-move rates, win rates); the failure modes the sources name (lost board state, invented rules, being talked into a bad move, betrayal in negotiation games); anything on people manipulating a model player; anything on a model as advisor to a human team.

**Where to look.** arXiv game benchmarks for language models (GTBench, BALROG, TextArena), the Kaggle Game Arena chess matches, Every's AI Diplomacy, Meta's Cicero paper in *Science*.
