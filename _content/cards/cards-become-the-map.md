**What it is.** Twenty minutes at tables in William's workshop. The game's map stops being the LL's and becomes the students' own: the index cards they wrote in the September 28 database workshop.

**How it plays.** Each table lays out the cards its group made on September 28 (or prints of them) that name parts of an infrastructure; blank cards fill gaps. On each card, in pencil: what it yields (power, water, data, labor, money), what it depends on (links drawn red for tight, blue for loose), and who would notice first if it failed. Pick one card as the AI's first data center. Draw three cards from the AI's deck and play them on the new map: where does it spread, what runs dry, what falls? A card that doesn't map goes in a "left out" pile for the debrief. Photograph the map; the photo is the template for the remix that follows.

**What it does for learning.** The map is the students' own categories, so every outcome is something their database made possible. SW reads it through Star's call to study boring things, which here is the work of labelling dependencies, and the *any-map test*: do the AI's rules still make sense on a world they weren't written for? It is also where the September workshop and this one become one project.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** The November 3 draft proposal to William, written for Marlon from his outline, says "Then we'll design a game together on top of the database they made." In the November 14 voice chat he came back to it:

> all of the cards that they wrote or their database

Marlon, Nov 14, in a voice chat with Claude, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 4), meaning William's students' index cards from September 28. CI's card [Design a game on the class's own database](design-a-game-on-the-classs-own-database.md) holds the idea; this is the activity that runs it.

Without them, any twelve cards naming parts of Harvard's infrastructure (the steam tunnels, the dining halls' suppliers, the campus network) work, or each student writes three places from their own project.
