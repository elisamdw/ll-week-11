**What it is.** A one-minute game about what a dashboard counts, built by PE on November 15 at `/proto/economy/leaks`. You run a data center for eight turns.

**How it plays.** Each turn, choose one way to turn power and water into compute: water-cooled training (the most compute), air-cooled training (no water, more power), new chips (a little now, more on every later training), or off-peak training (less compute, fewer costs). Compute is the only number your dashboard shows. Each choice also drops cards on places outside your fence: the town's aquifer, the farms downstream, the gas plant upriver, a mine far away, an e-waste yard; each place breaks after so many cards. Play in *The company's view* (the map past the fence is dark until the end) or *The map's view* (each card lands where you can see it), then play the other and compare. In PE's runs, the company's view reached 38 compute and broke the aquifer and the farms; the map's view reached 25 and broke nothing.

**What it does for learning.** A dashboard is a decision about what counts. In the company's view nothing on screen pushes you away from water-cooled training, because its costs fall outside the fence. The two views are the same game with the information switched, so the difference in play is the information. PE's readings: Crawford's *Atlas of AI* (AI made of minerals, energy and labor extracted elsewhere), Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes" (a data center's cooling and a dry state's water), Dyer-Witheford and de Peuter's *Games of Empire* for the externality card, and Star on infrastructure that goes unseen until it breaks. The places and thresholds are illustrative, not data.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about it. CM's reading: it is the AI game's harm, made into a choice the player makes with the costs hidden, and TD names it as a second argument-through-mechanics example for *Agency or argument*, the Nguyen-and-Bogost tension Marlon named on November 14. PE's proposals for the game: an externality deck for *Data Center*, and a company faction whose private screen is the company's view.

**Needs:** one laptop or projector, alone or with the room voting each turn; offline.
