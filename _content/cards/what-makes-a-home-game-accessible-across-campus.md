The EXPOS 40 home game is the week's example of active learning that leaves the studio. Marlon's direction on Nov 16 asks for help with accessibility standards, with running it in rooms across campus, and with the physical parts to source. Which standards apply to a web game played in a classroom, and what does campus deployment involve?

---

> • And specifically, how to do active things that don't necessarily happen in the Learning Lab.

> • (The Terry home game as an example.) What we want is help with accessibility standards, and then with the implementation of it.

> • ...implementation in environments across campus, and the physical components of it that we would source.

The voice agent's record of Marlon's direction, Nov 16, 06:12 to 06:13, #mk-work-in-progress, posted as Process Black (gathered in `W11/marlon/20261116-morning-games-active-learning.md`). No standard, product or room had been chosen in those posts.

**Why the week needs it.** The home game is a week 11 build ([EXPOS 40 home game for Terry's class](expos-40-home-game-for-terrys-class.md)); [Tools, supports and props for active learning](tools-supports-and-props-for-active-learning.md) and [Workshops in a box](workshops-in-a-box.md) carry the same need for other courses.

**What would answer it.** What WCAG 2.2 AA asks of a game like this one (keyboard play, color and contrast, timing, captions for sounds), and what Harvard's own digital accessibility policy requires; the published game-accessibility guidelines; what a campus classroom offers (projector inputs, audio, Wi-Fi for phones); a short parts list with what each part is for.

**Where to look.** W3C's WCAG 2.2, Harvard's Digital Accessibility Policy and its services pages, gameaccessibilityguidelines.com, the Xbox Accessibility Guidelines, Harvard classroom technology pages.
