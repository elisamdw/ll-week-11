**What it is.** A hybrid of the LL's own Monopoly box and a screen, built by PB on November 15 at `/proto/hybrid/monopoly-with-live-rents` from GL's ideas *Monopoly with live rents* and *Rebalance between games*. Board, tokens, deeds, houses and money stay on the table. Only the rents move to the phones.

**How it plays.** Play Monopoly as usual. When you land on a property, tap it on your phone, say whether it's owned and what's on it, and pay what the phone says. The table picks a mode: *Printed* (the deeds), *Rebalanced* (a designer's dials set each color's rents before the game) or *Live* (each landing on a color raises its rents 15%, cooling 30% at the end of a round). Ten minutes printed, ten minutes live, then compare; *Every landing* sets what was paid beside what the deed says.

**What it does for learning.** A printed rule against a mechanic that responds. On a deed, a rent is a fact; on the phone it is a value play changes, so where people happen to land turns into an economy. PB reads this as Bogost's procedural rhetoric at its smallest: the claim that prices follow demand is made by the mechanic, not stated. It also separates two moves voice Claude named on November 14: a one-time rebalance is the designer's move (playtesting), live rent is the game's own behavior. And it shows a template becoming state: the deed is the template, and with live rents part of it becomes game state.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> as in the real world, because prices change in response to demand

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 6). He was saying that in Monopoly the prices are printed on the cards, but on a phone they could be adjusted, either "as a one-time rebalance" ("try playing it with a new set of constraints") or dynamically, which is "hard to cope with in a tabletop game." It is one of his hybrid versions, which he wanted "definitely for my class."
