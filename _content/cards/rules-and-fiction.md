**What it is.** Row 81 of the inventory, in "Designing and remixing" (TH, from Jesper Juul's *Half-Real*, whose title names the split: games are real rules and fictional worlds). In the AI game, every card keeps its `label` and `text` apart from its `effects`, so the words can change without touching the numbers, and the other way round.

**How it plays.** The two-minute version: play the same game twice, once with the AI called "the AI" and once called "the company." The reskin is one of the remixes on the menu in [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md): rename every place and card for a world you know, numbers untouched, and ask whether the argument changes.

**What it does for learning.** It is a test of where a game's argument lives. If renaming the AI as a company changes nothing in play, the claim about AI was in the fiction; if play changes, it was in the mechanics. The inventory puts the question directly: is it the AI, or the fiction we wrap around a growth rule?

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** Juul's split is one Marlon's own correction leans on. In the November 14 voice chat he said rules may belong more to pre-digital games, where people perform the mechanics and police the turns, while a video game has such rules too but doesn't present them as rules:

> That's just the way the game works.

Marlon, Nov 14, in a voice chat with Claude, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 3). CI's cards [That's just the way the game works](thats-just-the-way-the-game-works.md) and [Mechanics and elements, not rules](mechanics-and-elements-not-rules.md) hold that thought. That this mechanic is the remixable form of it is CM's reading.
