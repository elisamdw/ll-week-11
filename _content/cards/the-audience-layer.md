**What it is.** Broadcast poker gives viewers more than the players have: the hole cards. GL's idea (November 14) was to do the same with AR over a tabletop game. PB's prototype (November 15, `/proto/hybrid/the-audience-layer`) does it with phones and a tiny AI game.

**How it plays.** On the projected stage, two or three players protect a 4 × 3 board from an AI that spreads one cell at a time from the middle. Each 20-second round they shield up to two cells. They can't see where the AI is going; everyone in the audience can, on their phones. Each watcher has three nudges for the whole game, spent in the open: a *hint* lights the row the AI aims at (three hints light the cell), a *feed* pushes the AI on (two feeds give it an extra cell). The AI takes one cell a round, two from round 4, three from round 7. After eight rounds the table shows what the AI went for, what was shielded, and what the audience did.

**What it does for learning.** The spect-actor from the mechanics inventory, with a cost: watchers can change the play, but only three times, in public. The information runs backwards, with the audience knowing more than the players, so the players learn to read the tallies, and many feeds mean the audience isn't on their side. PB's question for a class: who in the room is on the AI's side, and why? T. L. Taylor's *Watch Me Play* is the reading for an audience that is part of the game.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about this prototype. CM's reading: it serves the syllabus line for November 18, "audiences and participants in the loop," and the Final Project question of how someone meets the work, which is why it is linked from [A mechanic for your Final Project](a-mechanic-for-your-final-project.md).

**Could go further (PB's proposals):** an audience for a tabletop *Data Center*, whose phones show the AI's hidden intent; feeds as investment, so the audience profits when the AI grows; half the class plays and half watches, then they swap. Needs a projected laptop and phones on the same Wi-Fi; tested headless, not yet with a room of phones.
