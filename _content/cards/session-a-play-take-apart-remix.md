The plan Marlon chose on Nov 15 for his TDM 155AI class, Wednesday 12:00 to 14:45, built on his Nov 14 wish to go from a board game to its game state. Six activities, read as a chain, move the rules out of the players' hands: enforced at the board, seen as data in a photo, hidden in code, changed with Claude Code, then brought to the students' own projects.

---

**What it is.** The plan for Marlon's TDM 155AI class on Wednesday, November 18, 12:00 to 14:45: six activities in one line. Marlon chose it at 08:10 on November 15 from two options TD drafted; AL's board records the choice as "Session A, with one B swap." The run of show, times and fallbacks are TD's proposals inside that choice.

**How it runs.** This card starts a `follows` chain; read the next cards in order.

- 12:00, [Change one card](change-one-card.md), 10 min
- 12:10, [Take apart a board game](take-apart-a-board-game.md), 25 min
- 12:35, [Photo to game state, then read a save file](photo-to-game-state-then-read-a-save-file.md), 30 min
- 13:05, break, with two prototypes open on a side screen
- 13:15, [Play two theories of AI](play-two-theories-of-ai.md), 25 min
- 13:40, [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md), 45 min
- 14:25, [A mechanic for your Final Project](a-mechanic-for-your-final-project.md), 20 min

The swap, agreed: if the LL's Monopoly, Risk and Life or the overhead camera aren't in the room by Wednesday, [Claude takes a seat](claude-takes-a-seat.md) fills 12:10 to 13:05, and the save-file step runs on its own.

**What it does for learning.** The arc moves the rules out of the players' hands step by step. At the board, students enforce the rules and keep the state themselves. In the photo, they see that state as data. On the screen, the rules have disappeared into code. With Claude Code, they change that code. Each step makes the next legible, and the day ends on their own projects two weeks before the December 2 work-in-progress showing. TD names three ideas for Marlon to say rather than lecture: mechanics are the verbs; a game is data; the argument is made through the play.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** It is built on his November 14 voice chat:

> In my class, at least, and maybe in Stafford's class, we should actually just go from a board game to how you would represent the data of that board game as a game state

> I don't need to build a UI for that designing or remixing. The UI is Claude code itself.

Marlon, Nov 14, in a voice chat with Claude, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (sections 4 and 2). In the same chat he said "the design of the game is much, much more than the rules," which is why the day is framed around mechanics.
