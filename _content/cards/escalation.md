**What it is.** Card 11 of GM's deck, in "Time": each round asks a little more than the last. The deck's examples are *Tetris*, where the speed rises, and *Jenga*.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: each line of a scene must be one word shorter than the last. The build: each cue fires faster than the last until someone hits stop. In the AI game it is the AI's acceleration (1.25 a round in *Data Center*, 1.05 in *Normal Technology*) and its "grow" cards.

**Where it is in the week.** Nearly every prototype with an AI in it escalates. In [The race](the-race.md), capability is worth 10% more and carries 15% more risk every round. In [Cascade](cascade.md), the data centers draw 25% more each round. In [The audience layer](the-audience-layer.md), the AI takes one cell a round, then two from round 4, three from round 7. In [Monopoly with live rents](monopoly-with-live-rents.md), every landing raises a color's rent. The two speeds of the AI game, side by side, are the comparison in [Play two theories of AI](play-two-theories-of-ai.md).

**What it does for learning.** The inventory's note on AI: each round the AI asks a little more, and the question is whether the humans notice the slope. Escalation is how a game makes a trend into an experience, and the rate is a setting a class can argue about, which is what separates the AI game's two theories. It differs from [incremental growth](incremental-growth.md): growth is a number that rises on its own; escalation is pressure on the players that rises each round.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about escalation itself. CM's reading: it is the Paperclips half of the "cross between Universal Paperclips and Civilization" he asked for on November 13, made into pressure a table can feel; CI's card [Universal Paperclips meets Civilization](universal-paperclips-meets-civilization.md) is its anchor.
