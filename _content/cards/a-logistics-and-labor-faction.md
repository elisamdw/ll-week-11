**What it is.** A fifth faction for the AI game, proposed by SW for William's course. *Data Center* has four factions, the utility, the council, the company and the neighbors, and nobody in it works. The ruleset's own `notModeled` list names "The people who work in the data center" first.

**How it plays.** The workers build, run, cool and repair the data centers, and move what they need. They place worker tokens on nodes, and a node without workers can't run. Each shift tires them and rest restores them. They can absorb a cascade for one round at a cost to themselves, and they can strike, stopping a node. Their goal, in HI's mechanic card, is "wages, not winning." To try it: a fifth player with six worker tokens at one table's paper game, where nodes without a token produce nothing. With eight students at two tables of four, one table can swap the council out for the workers.

**What it does for learning.** It turns humans-versus-AI into a question William's course asks: who keeps the infrastructure running, and what happens to them when it is automated? The AI's *automate this* move can replace them. SW draws on the course's readings: Mezzadra and Neilson on operations and logistics, Nyckel on Amazon's anticipatory shipping, Fisch's train drivers and their margin of indeterminacy (absorbing a cascade), and Rabinbach's human motor on the workshop day itself (fatigue as a resource).

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about it. CM's reading: it is a faction a student could write in [Remix one mechanic](remix-one-mechanic.md), and it puts back something the game admits it leaves out, which is the debrief's third question.
