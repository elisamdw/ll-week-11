**What it is.** The price board of the AI game on its own, built by PE on November 15 at `/proto/economy/prices-that-move`. Three markets, GPUs, power and land, each with a supply and a base price; every unit sold pushes its price up a curve.

**How it plays.** Pick how fast the AI's demand grows: *Data Center* (25% a round) or *Normal Technology* (5%), the two numbers from the game's rulesets. Each round the AI buys first, at any price, for more data-center sites. Then the hospital, the university lab and the housing co-op, one player each, buy what they need from what's left. Unmet needs become harm. Eight rounds, about two minutes; then the other setting, and compare the charts. In PE's test, *Data Center* holds until round 5 and breaks in round 8 with land sold out to the AI; under *Normal Technology* no need goes unmet and prices fall.

**What it does for learning.** Nobody in the game decides that the hospital goes without power: the price curve does, and the AI is first in line. That is the argument through process that Bogost describes (PE's proposal), with the materials from Crawford's *Atlas of AI* and Hogan's "Data Flows and Water Woes", and Narayanan and Kapoor's normal-technology view as the counter-setting. PE lists what the model leaves out (one price per market, no new power plants, an AI with unlimited money), which is what a class can argue with.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> For our AI game, these could be things like GPU prices or land prices or whatever.

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 6). Just before it he described prices changing "as in the real world, because prices change in response to demand," which is "hard to cope with in a tabletop game," and said of the hybrid versions: "definitely for my class."

**In the week:** the first of AL's chains across audiences. LLUFs playtest it, Wednesday's class could play it at a table with a printed board, and in William's workshop each price lands on a named place, where the question shifts from what moving prices do to play to who pays them. It is also one of the remixes on the menu in [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md). Runs offline on one laptop.
