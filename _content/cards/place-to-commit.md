**What it is.** *Diplomacy*'s order phase, small and with an AI. GL's NFC idea (November 14): each faction puts its order token on its own reader under the table, and the phone reveals everyone's choice together; placing is committing. PI built the phone version on November 15 at `/proto/players/place-to-commit`; the NFC version waits on the kit.

**How it plays.** Each round the table screen announces the AI's two moves and their strength ("The press, strength 1; Regional grid, strength 1"), set so the table can stop it only if all but one player defend. Players talk and make promises. Then each commits one token on their phone, in secret: defend a threatened place, or your own project for a point. No take-back. The table screen shows who has committed, never where; when the last phone commits, every order flips at once, and the AI takes any place it outnumbers. If it takes three, nobody's points count. Four rounds, two to six players, about two minutes.

**What it does for learning.** A public-goods problem with an AI as the shared threat. Promises are made in the open and kept or broken in private, and nothing enforces them; the own-project token is the temptation each round, and the room has to agree who takes it, or someone takes it anyway. Committing in secret and revealing at once changes the talk: you can't wait to see what others did. PI's reading ties it to *AI 2027*, where every actor would rather the others held back, and to Nguyen's "disposable ends" for the debrief: who played as a citizen, who as a free rider?

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> doing some game mechanics with NFC is great, and we should try that this week

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 5). "Place to commit" was one of the NFC mechanics voice Claude listed in the same chat. The phone version runs now; the reader-under-the-table version is the one his line points at.

**Needs:** a laptop running the reader app, a projector, two to six phones on its network. The AI is a written rule. Orders are hidden by the pages, not encrypted.
