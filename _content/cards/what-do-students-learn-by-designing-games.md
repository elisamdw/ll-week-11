Both of the week's sessions end with students changing a game, and the Stafford workshop has them design one on their own index-card database. There is a research line on learning by designing games, from Papert's students to Kafai to Scratch. What did it find, and what does it say about designing games from course material?

---

**Why the week needs it.** [Several ways to involve the students](several-ways-to-involve-the-students.md) is open, and [Design a game on the class's own database](design-a-game-on-the-classs-own-database.md) is the Nov 23 plan. The classics file covers playing games to learn; it doesn't cover making them.

**What would answer it.** What Kafai's *Minds in Play* (1995) and later game-design studies found students learned, and under what conditions; anything on co-designing games in a humanities or social-science course; anything on remixing an existing game versus starting blank.

**Where to look.** Kafai's work (UPenn), the MIT Media Lab's Scratch and game-making studies, Games for Change and Gamestar Mechanic studies, Google Scholar on "learning by design" and "game design as learning".
