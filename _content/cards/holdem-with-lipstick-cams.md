> let's just go with lipstick cams.

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat, on card games, as transcribed by voice Claude (handoff back, section 5). In voice Claude's summary of the lines before it: he'd like to set up a Texas Hold'em situation with small cameras; the LL has endoscopic USB cameras and other small USB cameras.

Voice Claude's history, flagged in the handoff as from memory: televised poker first showed hole cards through glass-topped tables with cameras underneath (*Late Night Poker*, around 1999), then through small "lipstick" cameras at the rail (the World Poker Tour, around 2003), and now through RFID cards read at each seat. Marlon chose the middle one, and ruled out RFID decks ([NFC for simple tabletop games](nfc-for-simple-tabletop-games.md)).

GL turned it into a game idea on Nov 14: the cameras send the hands to a screen the players can't see, so hidden information becomes spectator information, and the question is who the game is for, the players at the table or the people watching. It pairs with the AV Lab: a card table, a few cameras, and a switcher are a small broadcast.

What it would take, as GL sized it: one table, two cameras, one round, an audience screen in another room. Voice Claude's advice: search for cameras by type, not "poker camera," since that search turns up cheating gear. The camera task went on Marlon's Airtable list on Nov 14.
