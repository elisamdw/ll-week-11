Marlon wants to "level up" AIBL Connect "with more things that are the gamified things," and says the site "is kind of gamified in reality." The classics file has the meta-analyses on gamifying learning, but faculty sharing teaching practice are not students taking a course. What does the evidence say about gamifying contribution sites like this?

---

**Why the week needs it.** Build 3 on `W11/builds.md`, [Level up AIBL Connect with gamified things](level-up-aibl-connect-with-gamified-things.md), has no pane and no design yet. RS's proposal was to use Malone's intrinsic test and Bogost's critique as the check; a design needs evidence closer to the case.

**What would answer it.** Studies of badges and reputation on contribution sites (Stack Overflow, Wikipedia, MOOCs forums) and what they did to behavior; anything on gamified professional-development or teacher communities; which elements (progress, collections, challenges, recognition) the evidence supports, and which crowded out the reason people came.

**Where to look.** Anderson et al., "Steering User Behavior with Badges" (WWW 2013), Hamari's work on badges, teacher-community studies in *Computers & Education*, Sailer and Homner 2020 on which elements helped.
