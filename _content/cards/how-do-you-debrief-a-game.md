RS's reading of the meta-analyses (Nov 13) is that how a game is wrapped, with a debrief and group play, matters as much as the game. Both of the week's game sessions end on play; neither names how the debrief runs. What does the field say a debrief is, what is the evidence, and what format fits fifteen minutes?

---

**Why the week needs it.** RS's last proposal in `W11/research/learning-games-classics.md` is "Build every game activity with a debrief and group play." Wednesday's [Session A](session-a-play-take-apart-remix.md) and [the Stafford workshop](stafford-workshop-in-the-room.md) both end with play or remixing; [What the mechanic argues](what-the-mechanic-argues.md) is the nearest thing to a debrief question.

**What would answer it.** The classic debriefing models (Lederman, Thiagarajan, Crookall, Peters and Vissers) in a line each; the evidence, which is largest in healthcare simulation; two or three formats a teacher can run in ten to twenty minutes (What? So what? Now what?; the After Action Review; PEARLS); anything on debriefing a game about systems by asking what its rules argued and left out.

**Where to look.** *Simulation & Gaming* (Crookall 2010), healthcare-simulation meta-analyses on PubMed, the US Army's After Action Review guide.
