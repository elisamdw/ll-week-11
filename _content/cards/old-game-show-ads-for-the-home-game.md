> And I should just find video clips that are about the advertising of the home game in various old game shows, just so people understand that as a meme.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`).

RS found 22 clips the same day (`W11/research/home-game-ads.md`; no one has played them yet). Three to start: Vanna White and Alex Trebek holding the Tyco boxes (1992), a Milton Bradley *Concentration* spot (1967), and "Weird Al" Yankovic's "I Lost on Jeopardy" (1984), where Don Pardo tells the loser he won't even get "a lousy copy of our home game," which RS calls the most citable statement of the meme. The background: game shows sold boxed home versions from the late 1950s, often in numbered editions, and losing contestants went home with one.

What the clips do, as CI reads it: they give the team, and Terry, a shared picture for "home game" in a few seconds. The consolation-prize joke is part of it: the home game is what you get when you don't win the real thing, a small cheerful copy of something that happened in a studio.
