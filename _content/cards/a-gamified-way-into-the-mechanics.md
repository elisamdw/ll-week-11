> and there could be a gamified way of interacting with them

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated, right after asking for "great up-to-date inventories of game mechanics that are printable and displayable throughout the space" (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`).

A list of mechanics is a reference; a deck of mechanics is a game. The step between them is small, as CI reads it: print each mechanic on a card, and the inventory can be drawn from, dealt, traded, combined. "Draw three mechanics and make a game that uses all of them" is a design exercise and a game at once.

The week's activity workspace has drafted versions: TD's [Draw three](../activities/tdm155ai/draw-three.md) for the class, and CM's [Change one card](change-one-card.md). The open question is whether the gamified version is a printed deck, a screen, or both; the cards' print view would make the printed deck straightforward.
