**What it is.** Card 19 of GM's deck, in "Players together": the group chooses, and everyone lives with the result. The deck's example is *Quiplash*, where the audience votes. It came into the deck from TDM 98's small-rooms mechanics and its "mechanics of togetherness" (November 5).

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: show three possible endings, ten seconds each; the room picks one, and you play it. The build: phones vote, and the tally fires the winning cue on the stage screen. In the AI game, the AI's proposals wait for an approve-or-block vote in *Data Center*, *The Committee* and *Normal Technology*, until the turn, after which it stops asking.

**Where it is in the week.** [The committee](the-committee.md) is a vote by show of hands on ten proposals, with an advisor that is right eight times in ten. [Place to commit](place-to-commit.md) is a vote in secret, revealed at once. PB's proposal for [The room is the board](the-room-is-the-board.md) is a committee that votes with its feet. AL lists voting among the AI game's own mechanics shared by both classes.

**What it does for learning.** A vote is the simplest form of governance a game can hold, and the AI game sets it against a machine: the treacherous turn is the moment the vote stops mattering. That makes oversight playable as something a room does and can lose. The inventory links *AI 2027*, where an oversight committee's vote branches the story, and Engelstein and Shalev's *Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design* for the mechanics of voting.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about voting this week. CM's reading: it is how the AI strategy game he asked for makes the humans a collective, and its phone build is one of his tabletop-and-phone hybrids.
