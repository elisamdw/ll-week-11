A loop is the short cycle of actions a player repeats all game; the core loop is the main one. In Universal Paperclips it is make, sell, invest. Name the three things you do over and over in a game and you have its loop: the smallest piece of a game a student can design, test and change.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Designers' working vocabulary, made into theory by Michael Sellers in *Advanced Game Design* (2018): a game is parts, the loops among them, and the whole they make, with reinforcing loops that grow and balancing loops that hold back. Daniel Cook, quoted there: "An arc is a broken loop that you exit immediately" (p. 176). The core loop is card 2 of GM's mechanics deck.

**This week.** Every version of the AI game runs the same loop: the AI draws, upkeep is paid, the factions act. Two loops run at once, the AI's and the players', and the drama is where they meet. The paperclip loop keeps its tuning values at the top of one file, so a pair can swap a step in fifteen minutes.
