**What it is.** SW's version of Marlon's async contingency for William's course: the AI game played one round a day for the week before the workshop. Marlon chose the in-person workshop at 11:53 on November 15, so this stays a proposal, to run alongside it or another time.

**How it plays.** Eight students in four faction pairs, each with a private thread for planning. Each morning the board-runner (William, a TF or an LL fellow) posts a photo of the board and the AI's last move. By midnight each faction posts its two actions in one short reply. Next morning the board-runner plays them, draws the AI's card, and posts the new photo. Seven days, seven rounds; the AI's turn card is somewhere in the deck, and nobody knows which day. The photo is the shared state, so nothing needs a server. A faction that doesn't post takes no actions, which is a mechanic too.

**What it does for learning.** Long-turn play (Freeciv's LongTurn games run one turn a day) feels like living with infrastructure: you check in, and it has changed without you. SW reads it as Fizek's playing at a distance, lived for a week. The week's photos become the record the debrief works from.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> It could be that we just create it all as an online async experience for the students.

Marlon, Nov 11, recorded in `W11/builds.md` as his contingency for the Stafford workshop. On November 14 at 12:05, asked whether the game should play async, he answered "yes ultimately" (GL's board, `_context/orchestration/20261114/board.md`). CI's card [The Stafford game as an async online experience](the-stafford-game-as-an-async-online-experience.md) holds the idea; this is one way to run it.

**What it costs:** the board-runner's job is daily work, and SW says William should not end up doing it unless he wants to. SW proposes trying three days with the LL's own fellows first and timing the job. A screen version with shared state on several phones would need a deploy, which is Marlon's call.
