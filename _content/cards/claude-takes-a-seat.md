**What it is.** Twenty-five minutes at tables, from Session B, kept as Session A's agreed swap: if the board games or the overhead camera aren't in the room on Wednesday, this replaces the board-game segment. Marlon chose the swap at 08:10 on November 15 (AL's board).

**How it plays.** Each table gives Claude one role:

- **Opponent.** Claude is a player. The table types or photographs the state each turn; Claude says its move and one sentence of why.
- **Advisor.** One player has Claude on a phone, whispering a move each turn. The others don't know what it says.
- **Director.** Claude doesn't play. After each round it reads the state and chooses the next event (a price change, a disaster, a gift) to keep the game tense, like the Director in *Left 4 Dead*.

Before play the table writes Claude's instructions on an index card, three or four lines: who it is, what it wants, what it may and may not see. They play at least three rounds of a game short enough to type (a trading game with index cards, liar's dice), then compare: did the game feel different with a machine in the seat? Did anyone try to trick it, as Lee Sedol's Move 78 did?

**What it does for learning.** AI as a player and AI as a material, side by side. The opponent is the proving-ground tradition from chess to Go (HI's history); the advisor is hidden information; the director is AI as stage manager. The instruction card is a small version of what students write for their Final Project agents: a role, a goal, what it must not know. Claude playing too politely or explaining too much is the problem the card is there to fix.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** Marlon has said nothing about this activity beyond choosing it as the swap. CM's reading: it asks the AI game's question, who the AI is at the table, with a real model instead of a written rule.

**Without a model:** two prototypes play the roles from a script. [The committee](the-committee.md) is the advisor, right eight times in ten; [The phone plays the AI](the-phone-plays-the-ai.md) is the opponent, by a rule you can read. A written rule against Claude's judgment is a useful contrast.
