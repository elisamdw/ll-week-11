**What it is.** The ten-minute opener of Session A, in pairs, on paper. It puts the mechanics deck in students' hands before anything goes on the projector.

**How it plays.** A pair plays one round of tic-tac-toe or rock-paper-scissors. They draw a card from the deck, read its one line and its "try it in two minutes," change the game with it, and play two more rounds. Card 1, *Goal and win condition*, turns tic-tac-toe inside out: whoever makes three in a row loses. With *Hidden rule*, one player invents a secret rule for where marks may go and the other finds it by playing. TD's pick of cards that work on a three-by-three grid: 01, 06, 12, 13, 14, 16, 40. A pair that draws a card needing a crowd (*Voting*, *The spect-actor*) redraws, and that is a finding: some mechanics need more players.

The pair ends with one sentence: did the card add a new decision, or only a new rule to remember?

**What it does for learning.** A mechanic is a part you can swap, and swapping one changes the play more than its size suggests. The closing question is the token test in miniature (does a change add a decision?), which the class comes back to when it judges remixes at 14:00.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** The deck is the first build he named for the week:

> my repo should have this inventory of game mechanics in it. So it's another moment for us to go back and make sure that we have great up-to-date inventories of game mechanics that are printable and displayable throughout the space, and there could be a gamified way of interacting with them

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated to MX (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`). Change one card is the smallest gamified way in: the deck becomes a set of moves you can make on a game.

**Needs:** GM's deck printed and cut, or the 11 x 17 posters on the walls; paper and markers. Half-letter hand cards are proposed in the class repo, not built.
