**The readings.** Sonia Fizek's *Playing at a Distance* (2022, open access; *Universal Paperclips* is on pages 60 and 61) is about games that automate their own play, so the player's job becomes setting things running and checking back; her earlier essay on interpassivity and delegated play asks who is playing when the game plays for you. Frank Lantz's *Universal Paperclips* (2017) is the game itself, and his *The Beauty of Games* (2023) his account of why games matter. Bostrom's paperclip maximizer is the thought experiment underneath. (TH's entries.)

**What they give the week's games.** The inventory's mechanics for this: *incremental growth* (numbers grow by themselves while the player tunes the rate), *automate this* (each upgrade removes a click and a decision), *come back later*, *delegation*. And HI's page on *Paperclips* as data: about 230 values in one `saveGame` snapshot, readable in the browser, which is how Wednesday's class meets a video game's state.

**Where it is played.** [The paperclip loop](paperclip-loop.md) is PE's one-minute version, written from scratch because Lantz's game has no licence to reuse. The Stafford homework, [Play Paperclips and log the easy moments](play-paperclips-and-log-the-easy-moments.md), is Fizek's question asked of the students' own play. In Session A the save file comes up in [Photo to game state, then read a save file](photo-to-game-state-then-read-a-save-file.md).
