**The readings.** On November 16 William assigns Charles Perrow's "Complexity, Coupling, and Catastrophe" (from *Normal Accidents*, 1984) and Michael Fisch's "Ninety Seconds" (from *An Anthropology of the Machine*, 2018). Perrow's argument, as the bibliography summarizes it: in systems that are interactively complex and tightly coupled, accidents are normal, built in, and better safety devices can add new paths to failure. Fisch's: Tokyo's commuter network runs beyond capacity because people and control systems absorb the strain together, and his "margin of indeterminacy" is the slack that lets them. The chapter is about the 2005 Amagasaki derailment. (TH's summaries; SW notes the Fisch chapter was summarized from a review, not opened.)

**What they give the week's games.** SW's reading: both describe a system in terms a game already has, links, timing, slack and a cascade. Pandemic's outbreaks, the Beer Game's bullwhip and Mini Metro's overload all play it (RS's research). The proposal for the AI game: mark each link tight or loose; a failure crosses a tight link in the same round, before any human acts, and a loose one the round after; loosening a link costs something, so choosing which links to loosen is the argument. Fisch adds a second dial: how much strain the people at a node can absorb before they fail too.

**Where it is played.** [Knock over the network](knock-over-the-network.md) is the paper version that opens William's workshop, ten minutes with index cards and red and blue tape. [Cascade](cascade.md) is PE's screen version, built at SW's request. In PE's tests the tight grid fails all at once in round 2, after a first failure that cost nothing, which is Perrow's point played out.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about Perrow or Fisch. The Nov 3 draft proposal to William, written for Marlon from his outline, names "Failures cascading through a tightly coupled system" as a game students could design. CM's reading: starting from William's own readings is how the workshop keeps from feeling like the LL teaching in his place, Marlon's concern of November 11 ([The Stafford workshop in the room](stafford-workshop-in-the-room.md) quotes it). SW's caution: students have just read these with William, so the workshop should use them, not re-teach them.
