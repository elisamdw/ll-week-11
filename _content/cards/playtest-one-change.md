**What it is.** Twenty-five minutes, in pairs, the first module of the LLUF studio block. Its product is evidence a prototype's builder can use.

**How it runs.** Choose a prototype whose page loads, note its route and version, and write one prediction: "When ___ happens, the player will ___." One fellow plays without coaching while the other observes: the first action tried, the first surprise, any point where play stops. Use roles or colors, never names. Then separate four things on paper: what the player did, what the screen showed, the player's explanation, the observer's hypothesis. Swap roles and change one thing, an exposed setting or one remix request with a route and an expected effect. Replay from the same setup and note what differs. The builder gets one observation, one suggestion and one question.

PL's suggested order (recorded by LU, 08:21 on November 15): [Prices that move](prices-that-move.md) with both growth settings back to back; [Every tap is a row](every-tap-is-a-row.md) with four people on one keyboard; [The committee](the-committee.md) with a room of voters.

**What it does for learning.** LU uses MDA's distinction between what the system permits, what happens over time and what a player feels, and Schell's lenses to keep playtesting open to story, aesthetics and technology. LU is careful about its limits: two rounds are evidence for a next design step, not proof of learning or balance, and a changed build or a stale room makes a comparison inconclusive.
