**What it is.** William Stafford's SOC-STD 98to, *Technologies, Operations, and Infrastructures*, comes to the Bok Center on Monday, November 23, 12:45 to 14:45, for the AI and gamification workshop. Marlon chose the format at 11:53 on November 15, from SW's options (relayed by AL): in person, two hours. The async week stays a proposal. Everything inside the choice is SW's proposal, and nothing is agreed with William yet.

**How it runs (SW's proposal).** William opens, runs the debrief and closes; the LL brings the game and runs the middle. This card starts a `follows` chain:

- the week before: [Play Paperclips and log the easy moments](play-paperclips-and-log-the-easy-moments.md), an hour at home
- 12:50, [Knock over the network](knock-over-the-network.md), 10 min
- 13:00, [Play Data Center](play-data-center.md), 30 min, one table on each version
- 13:30, [Read the game state](read-the-game-state.md), 10 min
- 13:40, [Cards become the map](cards-become-the-map.md), 20 min
- 14:00, [Remix one mechanic](remix-one-mechanic.md), 25 min
- 14:25, [What the mechanic argues](what-the-mechanic-argues.md), 15 min, William leading

If it runs long, *Read the game state* goes first; the debrief and William's close are never cut. Every segment has a paper route if a laptop fails.

**What it does for learning.** The course is about infrastructure, operations and logistics, and the workshop makes those ideas playable: coupling, the cloud's watershed, what a database decides. The students' own September 28 index cards become the map, so the game is built on their work rather than ours. William's syllabus allows AI artefacts in class but not on submitted work, so Claude Code at the remix table is an object to examine, not a tool for their writing.
