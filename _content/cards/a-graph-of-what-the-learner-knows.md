> The better versions keep a *knowledge graph* of what and how the learner is learning, sometimes even the learner's internal mental model. Learning isn't a list of facts but a structure, a schema you internalize, and you have to work with learners' existing mental models, not just tell them everything they think they know is wrong.

> Producing accurate node graphs of a learner's existing knowledge, and of its development across a class, seems incredibly difficult, "but again, it's very, very exciting... if it really were doable."

> *Something MK wants to work on and research across this week.*

Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction, Nov 15, 10:07–10:19 voice session, posted as Process Black to #mk-work-in-progress. The words in inner quotes are his.

Two graphs, as CI reads it: the graph of the subject (the ideas and how they connect), and the graph of one person's grasp of it, including what they believe that isn't so. LearnHarness keeps the first and deliberately leaves the learner's standing off it (HL's close read, `_content/learning/learnharness/graph.md`). Marlon is asking for the second, and for it over time, across a class.

He marks it as a research question for the week, not a build: "if it really were doable."
