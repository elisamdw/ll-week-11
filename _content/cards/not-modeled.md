**What it is.** Row 59 of the inventory, in "Designing and remixing" (TH from Bogost; RS from Paul Starr's "Seductions of Sim"). Every ruleset of the AI game has a `notModeled` array: a list, in plain words, of what the game leaves out. *Data Center*'s begins with "The people who work in the data center."

**How it plays.** The list is shown at the end of play and read in the debrief. The two-minute version: after playing, name the omission that would change the game's argument most.

**What it does for learning.** A simulation argues as much by what it leaves out as by what it includes; Starr's point about the seductions of simulation is that the model's simplifications become invisible as you play. Printing the omissions makes them part of the game. In William's workshop the list runs through three activities: [Read the game state](read-the-game-state.md) asks pairs which omission their table would add; [What the mechanic argues](what-the-mechanic-argues.md) asks which would change the outcome most and which a remix put back; and SW's [A logistics and labor faction](a-logistics-and-labor-faction.md) is a remix built from the list's first line. The inventory's note on AI: labor, heat, chips and local politics are where real AI infrastructure differs from the model.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about the list itself. CM's reading: it serves the tension he named on November 14 between Nguyen's agency and Bogost's procedural rhetoric (CI's card [Procedural rhetoric lives in the play](procedural-rhetoric-lives-in-the-play.md)): if a game argues through what it has you do, the list is where it admits what it doesn't let you do. Unlike the per-rule `rhetoric` field, which Marlon set aside on November 14 along with the rule editor, the list sits outside the rules and is read after play.
