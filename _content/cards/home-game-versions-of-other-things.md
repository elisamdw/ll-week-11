> But then we should also think about other home game versions of things we can do.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated, right after the EXPOS 40 home game (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`).

"Home game" here is a pattern, not a product, as CI reads it: take something that runs in the studio with the LL's people and gear, and make a version that goes home with the people who were there. A game show becomes a boxed game; a studio workshop becomes an online companion; a game the LL ran in William's class becomes a game he can take with him.

Seen that way, two other ideas from the same weekend are home games: [Workshops in a box](workshops-in-a-box.md) (Nov 14) and [A game William can take with him](a-game-william-can-take-with-him.md) (Nov 11). Candidates CI would add for Marlon to consider, none of them his: the capture station, the tap station, a week's playable prototypes.

**Nov 16.** The home game became Marlon's example of active learning outside the studio. In the voice agent's record of his direction in #mk-work-in-progress, a summary, not a transcript (06:12 and 06:13, permalinks in `sources`, gathered in `W11/marlon/20261116-morning-games-active-learning.md`): "Create a Terry home game thing this week (the EXPOS 40 home game for Terry Gipson)." "(The Terry home game as an example.) What we want is help with accessibility standards, and then with the implementation of it." "...implementation in environments across campus, and the physical components of it that we would source." So any home game here would carry the same three needs: accessibility, running in campus rooms, and parts to source. KR's question card is `what-makes-a-home-game-accessible-across-campus`.
