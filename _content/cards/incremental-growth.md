**What it is.** Card 10 of GM's deck, in "Time": numbers grow by themselves, and the player tunes the rate. The deck's examples are *Cookie Clicker* and *Universal Paperclips*.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: every minute each player's pile of paper clips doubles, unless they spend some to change the rule. The build: Claude Code makes a one-page clicker on any theme, three resources each feeding the next. In the AI game, the AI's goal number grows each round in every version.

**Where it is in the week.** [The paperclip loop](paperclip-loop.md) is this mechanic stripped to its parts, with the count rescaling until it stops meaning clips. The homework before William's workshop, [Play Paperclips and log the easy moments](play-paperclips-and-log-the-easy-moments.md), asks students to notice it from the inside. It sits beside [Automate this](automate-this.md), which removes the player's clicks one by one, and [Escalation](escalation.md), which turns rising numbers into rising pressure.

**What it does for learning.** Compounding is hard to feel from a number, and an idle game makes it felt. The inventory's readings span the argument: Lantz's *Paperclips* and Fizek's *Playing at a Distance* on games that play themselves, Kurzweil's doubling, Bostrom's runaway. The inventory's note on AI: the AI's growth compounds, and the players tune its rate, which is the one dial the two versions of the AI game turn differently.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about the mechanic itself.
