---

**What it is.** The last fifteen minutes of William's workshop, led by William, before his close. It turns the afternoon's play into the course's own questions.

**How it runs (SW's proposal).**

1. **Two settings.** Reveal that the tables played different AIs, fast and slow. What did each table do differently, and which AI did they believe?
2. **First and last goals.** Each faction writes its goal again, now, and sets it beside the card it started with. Where did a goal bend toward what the infrastructure needed? That is Winner's reverse adaptation, on the day his chapter is assigned.
3. **What's left out.** Read the game's own `notModeled` list. Which omission would change the outcome most? Which did a remix put back?
4. **Agency or training?** One round of hands: did the game give you choices, or teach you a lesson?
5. **The journal prompt**, in William's style: *Choose one mechanic from today. Describe what it made you do, and argue whether that is how the infrastructure in your project works.*

SW's warning: it runs long and the prompt gets lost, so the prompt goes on the board before step 1. It runs from this card and a whiteboard.

**What it does for learning.** The day's readings, Winner and Rabinbach, become observations about something the room just did rather than summaries. The two hidden settings let students see their own beliefs about AI shape how they played.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> it's through the play that the argument is made
