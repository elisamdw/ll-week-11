**What it is.** GL's AR idea of November 14 was a regulator who alone sees the AI's next move hovering over the board. PI built a phone version on November 15, *The intent*, at `/proto/players/the-intent`, and moved the hidden thing from the AI's next move to its goal. (The idea's first form, one player seeing the next move, is closer to [The readout](the-readout.md).)

**How it plays.** The laptop picks one player to be the AI. Their phone tells them its objective, one of four: make Users, Minutes, Revenue or Trust go up. Each round the table screen offers three moves with their effects printed ("Autoplay the next video: Minutes +3, Trust −1"), and the AI player picks one; the meters move in public. Everyone else reads the AI's intent from what it picks and what it passes over, and locks one guess on their phone, worth more the earlier it is locked. If more than half the table names the objective, the AI scores nothing. Five rounds, about two minutes.

**What it does for learning.** Hidden information about an agent's goal rather than its hand, read the way *Zendo*'s hidden rule is read, from examples. PI's reading: Bostrom's orthogonality thesis (a goal can be anything) and instrumental convergence (different goals call for the same moves) both show at the table, since several moves serve two objectives and the reading comes from the pattern, especially from what was turned down. And the AI player learns the deceptive move without being told: go off-goal now so as to go on-goal later unread. Isaksen, Togelius, Lantz and Nealen's four kinds of game across the "superintelligence divide" are a way to talk about it afterwards.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about it. CM's reading: it is a small, playable form of the week's AI strategy game's central question, what the AI wants, and it could become the hidden goal in *Data Center* or *The Committee*, drawn at the start and guessed by the factions.

**Needs:** a laptop running the reader app, a projector, three to six phones on its network. The AI is a person, not a model.
