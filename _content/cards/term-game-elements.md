Game elements are everything a game is made of. Jesse Schell names four kinds: aesthetics, technology, mechanics and story. The term gives a name to what a game is besides its rules, and shows what gamification leaves behind when it borrows only the points, badges and levels.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Schell's elemental tetrad (*The Art of Game Design*, 2008). His Lens of the Elemental Tetrad asks: "Are the four elements in harmony, reinforcing each other, and working together toward a common theme?" Gamification uses the term in a narrower sense: "the use of game design elements in non-game contexts" (Deterding et al. 2011). Marlon's frame:

> the design of the game is much, much more than the rules.

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat (as voice Claude handed it back).

**This week.** *Universal Paperclips*, the week's shared example: its mechanics are clicking and buying upgrades; its story is an AI's rise; its aesthetics are a bare text screen; its technology is a web page that runs in any browser.
