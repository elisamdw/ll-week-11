Jesse Schell, *The Art of Game Design* (2008), treats mechanics as one of four elements of a game, beside aesthetics, technology and story, and keeps turning the designer from the game to the player's experience. On gamification he says to take one thread from games, not to make everything a game. The book was not opened; these are his lens cards and a 2010 interview.

---

> To use this lens, take stock of what your game is truly made of. Consider each element separately, and then all of them together as a whole. Ask yourself these questions:
>
> - Is my game design using elements of all four types (Aesthetics, Technology, Mechanics and Story)?
> - Could my design be improved by enhancing elements in one or more of the categories?
> - Are the four elements in harmony, reinforcing each other, and working together toward a common theme?

Jesse Schell, "The Lens of The Elemental Tetrad," the whole card, in his Deck of Lenses web app (card #9 there; Lens #7 in the 2008 edition), deck.artofgamedesign.com, opened Nov 16.

> To use this lens, stop thinking about your game, and start thinking about the experience of the player. Ask yourself these questions:
>
> - What experience do I want the player to have?
> - What is essential to the experience?
> - How can my game capture that essence?

The same deck, "The Lens of Essential Experience."

> To use this lens, you must simultaneously see your game structure and the player experience. You may shift your focus from one to the other, but it is far better to view your game and experience holographically.

The same deck, "The Lens of Holographic Design" (its three questions left off).

> However, I think where the real win is when people start to analyze what is it about games that people like, and then to take those elements and weed those into what the experiences they're making in a natural way.
>
> It may be that you've taken some thread that works out of games and woven it into your thing. Your thing has not become a game, maybe, but maybe you've given it an enhanced sense of progress, or an enhanced sense of feedback, or an enhanced sense of camaraderie, things that games do really well, and you're going to, you know, take one thread. I think that's where the real success is going to come from in this space.

Jesse Schell, spoken, in Christian Nutt, "Peering At The Future: Jesse Schell Speaks," Gamasutra, Dec 3, 2010, as published ("weed" is printed so; he may have said "weave").

**What this gives the week, as KR reads it.** It is the frame behind the week's line that a game is more than its rules: when the LLUFs remix a game, they can ask which of the four elements a change touches. "Take one thread" is the measure for the teaching sites: borrow progress, feedback or camaraderie, not points for their own sake.
