> Tracking one image at a time isn't a huge limitation. Mechanics: point the phone at one card to see its hidden (or distorted) properties, e.g. a secret feature of Marvin Gardens; or the whole board as one image, with a secret square only you know you need to reach.

Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction, Nov 14, voice session part 2 (about 14:12–16:51), posted as Process Black to #mk-work-in-progress.

He was answering a limit. Voice Claude had reported that browser image tracking (MindAR) works on iPhone but holds only two or three images at once. His reply turns the limit into two mechanics. One card at a time: the phone is a lens that shows what the printed card doesn't, a hidden property of Marvin Gardens, or a distorted one, so that two players looking at the same card see different things. The whole board as one image: the phone shows you, and only you, the square you're racing for.

Both are hidden information carried by the phone instead of a face-down card, which is the thread of the week's phone prototypes ([Private goals on your phone](private-goals-on-your-phone.md), [One player sees the intent](one-player-sees-the-intent.md)). "Distorted" is the new part, as CI reads it: not hidden but misreported, a phone that can lie about a card. In the AI game that could be the AI's view of a place, shown to whoever has adopted it.

What it would take: a web page with MindAR, a Monopoly card compiled as a target, and one hidden property shown over it; a second test with the whole board as the target.
