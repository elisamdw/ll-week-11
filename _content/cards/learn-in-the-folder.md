**What it is.** The first half of the learning-harness take-home, thirty minutes, solo, in a folder opened in Claude Desktop's Code tab (HD's starter, `starters/learning-harness/`).

**How it runs.** Pick a topic small enough for half an hour and one you want: something from your Final Project research, a rule from this week's game, a concept from a reading. HB's examples: why a save file can bring back a whole game; what a mechanic is, as against a rule. Type `/learn <topic>`: Claude asks short questions to find where your understanding stops and agrees a goal with you, in your words. Then `/teach`, one step at a time: guess first, read one short step, explain it back, and say how sure you are (1 to 4) and why. Aim to hold three or four ideas. Last, ask Claude for `/graph`, open `graph.md` and `graph/attempts.json`, and find one answer in your own words and one idea and what it requires.

**What it does for learning.** An AI tutor's memory can be plain files the learner owns and can read, and "learned" can mean something specific: you explained it back and it felt obviously true, not that the chat happened. HB's watch list is the practical part: if Claude lectures several ideas at once, say "one step at a time, and check me after each"; if answers aren't in the files, say "write what we've done to the graph"; if the topic is too big, narrow the goal, not the pace.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** Marlon has said nothing about this activity itself. CM's reading: it is the first half of what he asked for on November 15, students using "Claude Desktop (code) as the harness," and it follows LearnHarness's loop of predicting, explaining and checking, which HL read closely that morning.
