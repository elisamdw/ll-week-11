On sites where people contribute, recognition from other people did the most: a barnstar raised top Wikipedia editors' output 60%, and a symbolic award kept 20% more newcomers active. Progress badges worked, then faded once earned. Awards for crossing post-count thresholds lowered contribution. No controlled study of a faculty practice-sharing site turned up.

---

**The answer to** [Which game elements suit a teaching-practice site?](which-game-elements-suit-a-teaching-practice-site.md), from a research pass on Nov 16; the full note is `W11/research/learner-models-and-badges.md` (part B).

**Recognition.** Restivo and van de Rijt (*PLoS ONE* 2012): a barnstar given at random to top-1% Wikipedia editors raised their productivity 60%. Gallus (*Management Science* 2017): a purely symbolic award raised the share of newcomers still active a month later by 20%, for four quarters.

**Progress, briefly.** Anderson et al. (WWW 2013), Stack Overflow: activity rises before a badge "and then almost immediately returns to near-baseline levels"; they advise spacing badges for one action and keeping them about equal in value. In a MOOC forum (Anderson et al. 2014), a badge ladder showing earned and locked badges and the next step had the clearest effect; its designers gave badges for reading and voting, not posting, and let posts earn recognition only through peer upvotes.

**What backfired.** Staub et al., "Heavy Medal" (ICIS 2022), a natural experiment with a control community: awards for crossing contribution thresholds reduced contribution overall; experienced members posted less and newcomers posted lower-quality content.

**Teachers.** Thin and self-reported: a Finnish badge-driven programme for 329 vocational teachers, and a Zhejiang survey where presentation and feedback mattered most to teachers' satisfaction. Sailer and Homner (2020) on gamified learning generally: small positive effects, with game fiction and social interaction as moderators.

**For AIBL Connect, as KR reads it**, and as the researcher infers: reward reading, trying a practice and peers endorsing it, not counts of posts. That fits the site's activity cards ([The activity is the unit](the-activity-is-the-unit.md)) and RS's Nov 13 proposal to use Malone's "intrinsic" test and Bogost's critique as the design check (`W11/research/learning-games-classics.md`).
