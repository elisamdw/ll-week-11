Game shows used to sell boxed home versions of themselves, and losing contestants went home with one. Marlon borrows the idea: a version of a studio experience that people can run without the LL's room, gear or staff. Terry's game show is the first. Two ideas from the same weekend are the same move: a game William can take with him, and workshops in a box.

---

> But then we should also think about other home game versions of things we can do. And I should just find video clips that are about the advertising of the home game in various old game shows, just so people understand that as a meme.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated.

**The meme.** RS found 22 clips. In one, "Weird Al" Yankovic's "I Lost on Jeopardy" (1984), the loser won't even get "a lousy copy of our home game."

**Why it matters.** On Nov 16 Marlon made the home game his example of active learning outside the studio. He added "implementation in environments across campus, and the physical components of it that we would source", in the voice agent's record (a summary, not a transcript).

**Candidates (agents' proposals, not his).** Instruments, a mic workshop, interview practice, the capture station, a week's playable prototypes.

**What the fellows could do this week.**

- Watch three of the ads.
- Pick a station they tested and say what its home version would need in the box.
