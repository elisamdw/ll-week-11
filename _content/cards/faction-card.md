**What it is.** Row 83 of the inventory, in "Designing and remixing" (TH, from C. Thi Nguyen's *Games: Agency as Art*; also Anthropy's *Rise of the Videogame Zinesters* and Fullerton's *Game Design Workshop*). A faction is a goal, two actions and one forbidden act, written for someone else to play.

**How it plays.** On an index card: who you are, what you want, two things you can do, one thing you won't. In the AI game, factions already exist (the utility, the council, the company, the neighbors); writing a new one is a remix, which students describe to Claude Code and Claude adds to a ruleset.

**Where it is in the week.** "Write a faction" is one of the worked remixes in [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md). SW's [A logistics and labor faction](a-logistics-and-labor-faction.md) is a full example: workers whose goal is wages and rest, who can strike. The four existing factions are what students play in [Play Data Center](play-data-center.md), one each, and in [A round a day](a-round-a-day.md), in pairs.

**What it does for learning.** Nguyen's claim is that games are the art form whose medium is agency: a designer hands players goals and abilities to take on for a while. Writing a faction makes students the designer of an agency for someone else, which is the assignment in miniature. The forbidden act matters as much as the actions: it says what this faction won't do, and that is often what the game argues.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about this mechanic. CM's reading: it is the Nguyen side of the tension he named on November 14, and CI's card [Games heighten agency](games-heighten-agency.md) is its anchor.
