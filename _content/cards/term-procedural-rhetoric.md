Procedural rhetoric is arguing through what a system makes happen and what it lets you do, not through words or pictures. Ian Bogost named it. A game can make a case about debt, empire or AI by what it rewards and what it makes hard. Marlon's reading this week: the argument is made in the play.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Bogost: "Procedural rhetoric is a general name for the practice of authoring arguments through processes" (2008, p. 125; the book is *Persuasive Games*, 2007). His wording leans on rules. Marlon reads it the other way:

> must be talking about more the sort of mechanics and actions people can take rather than the rules … it's through the play that the argument is made.

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat (as voice Claude handed it back). Sicart's "Against Procedurality" (2011) argues on his side.

**This week.** The Stafford game: if games argue through their procedures, a 4X game trains its players in a colonialist and capitalist mindset, because growth by conquest is built into play. Bogost's own example: in *Animal Crossing*, paying off your house loan earns a bigger house and a bigger loan, an argument about debt without a word said.
