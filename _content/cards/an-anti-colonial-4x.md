> Introduce 4X as a common type of strategy game, acknowledge we may not want to be exploitative, and make the *anti-colonialist version of a 4X game*, like Anti-Monopoly: a game that critiques the original game's logic. Highlighting how the corporations expand, exploit and exterminate is critical of both the genre and the companies.

Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction, Nov 15, 10:07–10:19 voice session, posted as Process Black to #mk-work-in-progress.

The model is a game that plays against its original. *Anti-Monopoly* (Ralph Anspach, 1973) kept Monopoly's board and turned its goal around: break up monopolies rather than build one. Here the 4X frame stays, and the game makes its four verbs visible as what they are when firms do them.

Voice Claude added a caution in the same post, which CI passes on: Monopoly itself began as a critique (Lizzie Magie's *The Landlord's Game*, 1904), and the genre absorbed it. A critical game can be played straight. That is a good discussion question for the students, not a reason to drop the idea.

Readings already in the bibliography that bear on it, per the same post: Pobłocki on *Civilization*'s tech tree, and Mukherjee's "Playing Subaltern." Flanagan's *Critical Play* and Dyer-Witheford and de Peuter's *Games of Empire* are in `_content/theory/`.

Marlon filed deep-research tasks from this session on the history of Monopoly, playing from the colonized side, and the history of the 4X genre (per the post's "Tasks sent" line).
