The possibility space is every move and situation a game's rules allow. Rules draw its edges; play explores it. Chess's is vast; tic-tac-toe's is small enough to solve. Change one card or one rule and the space is redrawn, which is why the week's activities ask players to change one thing and play again.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Bogost: "Procedures (or processes) are sets of constraints that create possibility spaces, which can be explored through play" (2008, p. 122). Sicart: "rules provide the possibility space where that interaction is possible" (2008).

**This week.** The activities that change one card or one rule and then play again are experiments on the possibility space: what new moves open, which close, and whether the game still argues what it did.
