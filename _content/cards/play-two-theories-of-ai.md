**What it is.** Twenty-five minutes with the week's AI game, which GL built on November 14 at `/game`. Two versions run on one engine. In *Data Center* the AI draws from a deck and one hidden card is the treacherous turn. In *Normal Technology* it builds only where a faction adopted it, and there is no turn. Same map, same four factions (utility, council, company, neighbors), each with a private goal.

**How it plays.** Half the tables play each version, hotseat on one laptop, for twelve minutes. One player from each table moves to a table of the other version and watches a round. Each table then answers: what does this game claim about AI, and which verb made you feel it? Not which rule: which thing you did, or watched the AI do. The question to hold for the remix: would your change give players more choice, or make a different argument?

Hotseat means players can see each other's private goals; [Private goals on your phone](private-goals-on-your-phone.md) shows the fix, and two minutes of it first makes the point. Ten minutes may not reach the turn in *Data Center*: then reveal the hidden card and ask what would have happened.

**What it does for learning.** Procedural rhetoric in one sitting. The two versions differ in a few mechanics only, so students feel that the claim lives in the mechanics, not the theme. The AI here follows written procedures, which sets up the contrast with a model in the seat. Readings: Bogost, *Persuasive Games*; Nguyen, *Games: Agency as Art*; Bostrom, *Superintelligence*; Narayanan and Kapoor on AI as normal technology.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.**

> it's through the play that the argument is made

Marlon, Nov 14, in a voice chat with Claude, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 3). It was his reading of Bogost: procedural rhetoric "must be talking about more the sort of mechanics and actions people can take rather than the rules." In the same chat he named the tension between Nguyen's games as the art of agency and Bogost, whose games, "in limit case," indoctrinate or train the player. The game itself is the Stafford game he asked for on November 13, "a cross between Universal Paperclips and Civilization." AL's cross-audience map makes Wednesday a dry run for William's students on November 23.
