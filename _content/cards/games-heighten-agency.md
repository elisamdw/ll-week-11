> Addition: that's one side of it. The game gives users a heightened sense of agency. They might be making more decisions per second than they typically make in life, or decisions about more crucial things, or having to think strategically about a greater volume of ideas, a larger set of relations, or more complex causal chains than they typically think about in life. The game is a way of really accentuating human agency.

Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction, Sunday, Nov 15, about 11:22, in the thread "Idea: two sides of the game thing — MK's agency," posted as Process Black to #mk-work-in-progress.

This is the first of the two sides, and it is close to Nguyen's account of games as an art of agency: a game hands you a sharper, denser kind of acting than ordinary life allows. Marlon's list gives it measures: decisions per second, the stakes of a decision, the number of things held in mind, the length of the chains between cause and effect. Each could be a dial a designer turns.

The other side follows in the same thread: some games, 4X games above all, push complexity past what a player can handle ([A hundred cities](a-hundred-cities.md)). The thread closes on the two together ([The ramp of learning to use AI](the-ramp-of-learning-to-use-ai.md)).
