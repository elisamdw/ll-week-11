The magic circle is the line that sets a game apart from ordinary life: inside it, special rules hold. Johan Huizinga used the phrase once, in a list; Salen and Zimmerman made it a design idea; Mia Consalvo argues there is no sealed circle at all. For the LL the question is where a session's game starts and the room ends.

---

**Where the term comes from.** Huizinga, *Homo Ludens* (1938): "The arena, the card-table, the magic circle, the temple, the stage, the screen, the tennis court, the court of justice, etc., are all in form and function play-grounds, i.e. forbidden spots, isolated, hedged round, hallowed, within which special rules obtain" (1950 English edition, p. 10). Salen and Zimmerman turned it into the boundary of a game (*Rules of Play*, 2003). Consalvo, "There Is No Magic Circle" (2009), finds the idea "static and overly formalist": ordinary life's rules apply inside games too.

**This week.** RS's research note: the studio as a play-ground with its own rules explains why a session starts with a threshold (cards dealt, roles assigned, a timer started). TH proposes a "leak" card for the AI game, bringing an outside fact (a county water vote, a utility's rate filing) onto the map each round.
