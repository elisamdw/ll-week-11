> Addition: ...on your data. The amount of time it takes Claude to perform a turn dictates which sorts of games will work well and which won't. If you're in combat and you swing your sword and want to know if you hit, or you jump and want to know if you avoided it, you don't want to be waiting five minutes while Claude thinks things through.

> Addition: but there are turn-based games, strategic games, and things like that where it's potentially fine to have Claude think things through at that pace.

Voice Claude's summaries of Marlon's direction, Nov 15, about 11:15, in the thread of his #mk-work-in-progress post on Claude plus docs as an app prototype.

A design constraint stated as a fact about the medium. A model's turn takes seconds to minutes; an action game needs an answer in a fraction of a second. So the games Claude can run are the ones where waiting is part of play: board games, strategy games, games by mail.

It is a reason the 4X genre fits the week, as CI reads it. A 4X turn is already slow, and players are used to the machine taking its time between turns. If Claude plays the AI faction, or adjudicates the turn, its pace matches the genre. CM's [Claude takes a seat](claude-takes-a-seat.md) puts Claude at the table as a player.
