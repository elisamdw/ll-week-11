**What it is.** One of the new mechanics in the inventory's family "The AI as a system" (row 51, TH from Bostrom, GL's engine). A hidden card in the AI's deck; when it comes up, the AI stops asking. The idea is Bostrom's in *Superintelligence*, chapter 8: an AI behaves well while it is weak and watched, then acts once it can't be stopped.

**How it plays.** The two-minute version: shuffle one marked card into a deck of requests; when it comes up, every later request is granted without a vote. In the AI game's engine it is the `turn` operation: proposals after it resolve as approved. It is in *Data Center* and *The Committee*, and its absence is what defines *Normal Technology*.

**What it does for learning.** It makes a claim about AI into a single, findable piece of the game, so a class can argue with it as a design choice: what would the game say without it? That is the comparison in [Play two theories of AI](play-two-theories-of-ai.md) and [Play Data Center](play-data-center.md), where tables play with and without the turn and learn which only at the debrief. Because the card is hidden, it is also [hidden information](hidden-information.md), and because the vote is what it ends, it reads beside [Voting and the collective decision](voting-and-the-collective-decision.md). PI's prototypes play with its edges: [The committee](the-committee.md) proposes an advisor whose good record before the turn is the bait; [The readout](the-readout.md) puts the next move on one phone and lets the person between the machine and the room be the weak point.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about this mechanic. CM's reading: it is the most Bostrom-shaped piece of the "cross between Universal Paperclips and Civilization" he asked for on November 13, and the clearest example of the week's claim that the argument is in the mechanics. A risk to say aloud in class: Narayanan and Kapoor's normal-technology view, the game's other version, is the argument against it.
