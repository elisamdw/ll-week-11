**What it is.** Card 50 of GM's deck, in "Story, characters, and progress": every action gets a sound, light or shake that makes it feel good. Game designers call it "juice". The deck's example is *Peggle*.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: play rock-paper-scissors where the room cheers every win, then play it in silence. The build: every cue also fires a light and a sound on the stage screen; take it away and play again.

**Where it is in the week.** It is one of the mechanics the week's activity and prototype cards name most, mostly in the fellows' and the harness's cards. In the LLUF block, [Playtest one change](playtest-one-change.md) and [Host it, reset it, hand it over](host-reset-handover.md) treat feedback as what a station lives or dies on: does a new player see what their action did? In [The web learning game](the-web-learning-game.md), a node lighting up and the progress bar are the feedback. In [A mechanic for your Final Project](a-mechanic-for-your-final-project.md), "what the system does back" is one of the four lines on each student's card. And [The Are You Serious?! board](the-are-you-serious-board.md) is feedback for a speaker: the buzzer, the strike X.

**What it does for learning.** Feedback is how a player learns a system without reading its rules, which is why Marlon's class can play a game whose rules have disappeared into code. Its readings are also its warning. Bogost's *Cow Clicker* satirized games that are all juice and no decision, and Lantz's token test asks whether a change adds a decision or only decorates. The inventory's note on AI: juice makes an AI's loop feel good even when nothing is decided.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about feedback as a mechanic. CM's reading: it belongs to his November 2 line that interaction can be part of games and installations, which [A mechanic for your Final Project](a-mechanic-for-your-final-project.md) quotes.
