**What it is.** The last twenty-five minutes of the LLUF studio block. A prototype becomes a station someone other than its builder can run.

**How it runs.** Pack the station: one tested mechanic, with its route, host address if needed, materials, starting state, controls, reset, and a keyboard or paper fallback, all on one sheet. Invite a new player with one sentence and let them try; the observer notes where the invitation or the feedback fails, and the host explains rather than operating every move. Practice recovery: reload the page, close a phone tab, unplug an optional sensor, and reset from the sheet. Then a different pair hosts from the sheet without coaching, and every path gets a label: ready, needs repair, or untested. A laptop test doesn't show that a phone or NFC path works. The evidence goes back to AL and the prototype's builder.

**What it does for learning.** LU's point: a mechanic becomes a studio experience through clear feedback, a repeatable setup, and a host who lets players act. Following Schell's broader frame, the invitation and presentation are design work alongside mechanics and technology. The companion idea, *A station with two ways in*, asks that a physical input (a gesture, a camera, an NFC token) and a key or paper path mean the same move, so that the station stays playable with the sensor unplugged and the class can tell whether the physical interface helped.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about this card. CM's reading: a station a fresh pair can host is what lets the LL's games be printed, displayed and played throughout the space, the form Marlon asked for when he named the mechanics inventory on November 13. LU keeps this week's hosting task separate from Marlon's idea of an electronics owner among the fellows, which is for next term.
