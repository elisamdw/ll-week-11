> Firms like *NVIDIA, OpenAI and Anthropic* as the players doing the 4Xs.

Voice Claude's summary of Marlon's direction, Nov 15, 10:07–10:19 voice session, posted as Process Black to #mk-work-in-progress.

This changes who the AI is in the game. In the Nov 14 *Data Center* game the AI is one non-player force spreading across the map, and the factions are human (a utility, a council, a company, neighbors). Here the players are companies, and what expands is their reach: chips, data centers, power contracts, users. The AI stops being a monster and becomes a business, several of them, competing.

It sits close to what the companies say about themselves, which is why it can be pointed. The same session names the critique: "Highlighting how the corporations expand, exploit and exterminate is critical of both the genre and the companies" (voice Claude's summary).

Questions it raises, CI's: does a firm play to win, or is "winning" the thing under critique? Are there non-firm players (a regulator, the neighbors, the grid), or only firms? Naming real companies makes the game sharper and also more delicate in a course setting; William may have a view.
