**What it is.** The homework before William's workshop, about an hour at home, set by William as a journal entry for the week of November 16 if he wants it.

**How it runs.** Each student opens *Universal Paperclips* (decisionproblem.com/paperclips, free in the browser, saves as you go) and plays for about an hour. Each time the game makes a choice feel easy, obvious or automatic (buying an autoclipper, accepting a project, letting it run) they write one line in the journal: the time, the choice, what made it easy. Afterwards they pick one line and write a paragraph: what argument does that moment make, and who is playing at that point, you or the game? Under William's AI policy, the paragraph is their own writing.

**What it does for learning.** *Paperclips* is Bostrom's paperclip maximizer played from the AI's side. SW's reading: Bogost's procedural rhetoric says the argument is in what the game has you do, Fizek's work on idle games asks what happens to agency when the system plays itself, and the log turns both into evidence from the students' own play. It also feeds the workshop day's readings, Winner on autonomous technology and Rabinbach on the human motor: the moments a choice stops being yours are reverse adaptation in miniature. A student who plays five minutes and one who plays five hours can both keep the log.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** The plan comes from the November 3 draft proposal to William, written for Marlon from his outline: "before class, students play *Paperclips* for an hour (it's free in the browser) and read a bit of Bostrom and Bogost." Marlon's own words for the game it leads into:

> Something that's a cross between Universal Paperclips and Civilization or something like that.

Marlon, Nov 13, dictated to MX (`W11/marlon/20261113-games-and-interaction.md`). CI's card [Universal Paperclips meets Civilization](universal-paperclips-meets-civilization.md) holds that idea; PE's [paperclip loop](paperclip-loop.md) is the one-minute version a facilitator can show in the room.
