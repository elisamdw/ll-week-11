**What it is.** Row 80 of the inventory, in "Designing and remixing" (TH, from Mary Flanagan's *Critical Play*; the inventory also links Souvik Mukherjee's *Playing Subaltern*). Three ways to edit a game critically: *unplay* it (add a move the game forbids), *reskin* it (rename everything, numbers untouched), or *rewrite* it (change what counts as winning).

**How it plays.** Take the game and do one of the three, then play it once. In the AI game each is a remix task for Claude Code, saved as a new ruleset so the original stays intact.

**Where it is in the week.** It is the backbone of both classes' remix activities. In Marlon's class, [Remix a mechanic with Claude Code](remix-a-mechanic-with-claude-code.md) offers a reskin and a subtraction among its worked examples. In William's workshop, [Remix one mechanic](remix-one-mechanic.md) offers a printed menu, each item tied to a reading. In the learning-harness take-home, [Make a game from your graph](make-a-game-from-your-graph.md) offers reskinning the learning map as a dungeon, a city or a constellation. AL's cross-audience map counts it among the mechanics that carry the most across audiences.

**What it does for learning.** Each edit isolates a different part of the game's argument: what it permits, what it calls things, and what it values. The inventory's note on AI: reskinning the AI as a company, a state or a god shows what the numbers alone argue. Mukherjee's question, whose world is being turned into resources and on whose terms, is a rewrite waiting to happen in a strategy game.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about Flanagan's edits. CM's reading: they are worked examples of the kind he asked for on November 14, for students remixing through Claude Code (CI's card [Claude Code is the UI](claude-code-is-the-ui.md)).
