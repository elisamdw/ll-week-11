> On Sept 28 the students sketched databases of the course on index cards, and some groups ended up with a database of infrastructure itself. I photographed the cards. Before Nov 23 I'll build them out into a working database. In the workshop we'll start with the point that databases sit behind most of the apps students use every day: YouTube, Spotify, Final Cut, and almost every game. Then we'll design a game together on top of the database they made.

Marlon's proposal for William Stafford, Nov 3 (`NB/_context/projects/50-in-focus/william-stafford/workshop-2/proposal-for-william.md`).

On Nov 14, in the voice chat, he came back to it: he mentioned involving "all of the cards that they wrote or their database" (section 4 of the handoff back).

The idea joins the two Stafford workshops. In September the students made a database of their course on paper; in November that database becomes the ground the game is played on. Their own categories (the infrastructures they chose, the relations they drew) become the map, the resources, the places the AI wants.

It ties to the week's game-state thread. A game is data and a loop ([From board game to game state](from-board-game-to-game-state.md)); here the data comes first and the game is designed onto it. In the vocabulary from the Nov 14 voice chat, the students' cards are templates and play creates instances.

What it would take: the photographed cards (Marlon has them), a working database built from them before Nov 23 (Marlon's own task in the proposal), and a game whose places and resources are read from that database rather than written into the code. GL's game idea [Index cards that answer](../game-ideas/index-cards-that-answer.md) adds NFC stickers so each physical card shows its state in play.

*CI's reading, Nov 15. Not Marlon's words unless quoted.*
