Rules are a game's constraints: what is allowed, what happens when, and how the game ends. At a table the players enforce them; in a video game the program does, and the player may never read them. Rules draw the space a game is played in; the mechanics are what players do inside it.

---

**Where the word comes from.** Salen and Zimmerman split rules three ways: operational (the ones in the rulebook), constitutive (the logic and math underneath) and implicit (etiquette) (*Rules of Play*, 2003; their wording known only through course slides, not checked against the book). Jesper Juul on the computer game: "it is now the computer that upholds the rules," which "allows for games where the player does not know the rules from the outset" (2003). Sicart: "rules are modeled after agency, while mechanics are modeled for agency" (2008).

**This week.** No rulebook says how high Mario jumps in *Super Mario*:

> That's just the way the game works.

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat (as voice Claude handed it back). In Monopoly, collecting $200 for passing Go is an operational rule; the Free Parking jackpot is a house rule.
