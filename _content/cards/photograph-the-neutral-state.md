Photograph each of the LL's board games from overhead before anyone moves, then again a few turns in. What differs between the two photos is the part of the state the game has to remember. Do the same with a card game, where the camera can't see the hands: some state is hidden from a photo the way it is hidden from the other players.

---

> here's the game, and then take a photo of it.

Marlon, Nov 14, in a voice chat with Claude, as transcribed in the handoff back to Claude Code (section 4).

The handoff, in voice Claude's summary of what he said, gives the plan around that line: overhead camera shots of board games, turned into a data structure for the students; the games the LL has are Monopoly, Risk and Life; photograph "an initial neutral state" and then a game in progress; and do the same with a card game, cards and whatever else is on the table.

Two photos of the same game a few turns apart show what changed, which is the part of the state that matters. A card game shows the limit: a photo from above can't see a hand, so some of the state is hidden from the camera the same way it is hidden from the other players. (That second point is voice Claude's note in the handoff, not Marlon's.) For card tables he said: "let's just go with lipstick cams."

Still open, from the handoff's list: which games get photographed, and when.
