RS named Intelligence Rising as the model for a facilitated states-and-labs game on Nov 13, and the AI 2027 exercise sits beside it. Both are run by a facilitator who rules on what players propose. How long do they take, who plays whom, how is a turn resolved, and what have their designers learned?

---

**Why the week needs it.** William Stafford's workshop on Nov 23 has students co-design and play an AI strategy game with [the firms as the players](the-firms-are-the-players.md); RS's suggested chassis has "a facilitator who rules on proposed moves (Intelligence Rising)" (`W11/research/stafford-strategy-games.md`). The LL hasn't read how the facilitated games are run, only that they exist.

**What would answer it.** For each game: the roles, the length, what a turn stands for, how an action is resolved (facilitator judgment, dice, argument), group size, the debrief, and published lessons; matrix games as the wargaming method behind argument-based adjudication; anything on running them with students in under three hours.

**Where to look.** Avin, Gruetzemacher and Fox, "Exploring AI Futures Through Role Play" (AIES 2020) and later Intelligence Rising papers; the AI Futures Project's pages on its tabletop exercise; Chris Engle's matrix games.
