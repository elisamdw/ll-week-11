**What it is.** Card 2 of GM's deck, in "Goals and the loop": the short cycle of actions the player repeats all game. The deck's example is *Universal Paperclips*: make, sell, invest.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: name the three things you do over and over in a game you know, swap one, and say what changes. The build: ask Claude Code for a one-page loop with three buttons, play it for two minutes, then change one step. In every version of the AI game, the loop is the AI draws, upkeep is paid, factions act.

**Where it is in the week.** [The paperclip loop](paperclip-loop.md) is a core loop with one phase change; its tuning values sit at the top of one file so a pair could swap a step in fifteen minutes. The AI game runs two loops at once, the AI's and the factions', and the turn order decides which reacts to which.

**What it does for learning.** The loop is the smallest unit a student can design and test. Naming a game's three repeated verbs is a fast way into "mechanics are the verbs", the idea TD proposes Marlon says on Wednesday. The inventory's note on AI: the AI's loop runs on its own beside the players' loop, and the drama is where the two meet. Its readings are Fizek, Lantz's *The Beauty of Games*, Fullerton's *Game Design Workshop* and Sellers's *Advanced Game Design*.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about the core loop. CM's reading: CI's card [A game is data and a loop](a-game-is-data-and-a-loop.md) carries the idea it belongs to.
