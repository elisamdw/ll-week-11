The LL has no assigned canon of games, so it can't assume a shared reference. Marlon's answer for the fellows: everyone lists the games they've played most, perhaps five, tabletop, card and word games included, and the room looks for where the lists meet. The overlap picks the game they unpack together, and the tally tells Marlon which references will land on Wednesday.

**How it plays (KB's proposal, about twenty minutes).**

1. **Write (3 min).** Each fellow writes five games on five index cards, one per card: any medium, the ones they have spent the most hours on, not the ones they admire. Phone games and word games count.
2. **Lay out (5 min).** All the cards go on one table, sorted into piles by game. Piles of two or more are the intersections. Sort the piles again by kind (tabletop, card, word, video) and see which kind the room shares most.
3. **Pick (2 min).** The biggest pile is the game the room knows best. If it is on the LL's shelf, play it; if not, use it from memory.
4. **Take it apart (10 min).** Run the first half of *Take apart a board game* on it: rules, mechanics, game state, other elements, with the mechanics matched to the deck.

**What it is for.** The fellows get a shared reference before they test mechanics for Wednesday, and they see that taking a game apart works on any game. Marlon gets his meta game: which titles a room of students shares, which is the question behind every example he picks for class. The same twenty minutes could open Wednesday's class, with the tally as a warm-up.

**What to keep.** A photo of the piles, by game, with no names on the cards, so the tally can be kept as data (titles and counts) without recording who played what. The cards could feed a small table of the LL's reference games, which would grow every time the activity runs.

**Materials.** Index cards, markers, one clear table, a phone for the photo.
