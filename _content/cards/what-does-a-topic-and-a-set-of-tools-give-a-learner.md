On Nov 16 Marlon named, as something to talk about, the value of having people focus on one topic and one set of tools. He didn't spell out an activity. Is there research on why a narrowed brief helps people learn or make things, and where does it stop helping?

---

> • Key thing to talk about: the value of having people able to focus on a set of tools and a topic.

The voice agent's record of Marlon's direction, Nov 16, 06:18, #mk-work-in-progress, posted as Process Black. The post leaves the mechanism open, and this card does too.

**Why the week needs it.** The LLUFs' week, Wednesday's class and the Stafford workshop each choose a topic and a kit for the room; [Workshops in a box](workshops-in-a-box.md) and [The activity is the unit](the-activity-is-the-unit.md) package the same pairing for other courses. A sentence of evidence would help when the LL explains the choice to faculty.

**What would answer it.** What studies of creative constraints found (when a narrower brief improves output, when it hurts); the cognitive-load account of limiting tools for novices; the game designer's version, constraint as a mechanic; one or two classroom examples.

**Where to look.** Stokes, *Creativity from Constraints* (2005); the review by Acar, Tarakci and van Knippenberg on constraints and creativity (*Journal of Management*, 2019); Sweller on cognitive load; the LL's own `mechanics/constraint`.
