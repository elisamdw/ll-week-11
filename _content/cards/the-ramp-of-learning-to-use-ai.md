We're on one of those ramps, like Schell's ramp of difficulty versus ability, and you need that ramp to rise in the right way. That's a way of thinking about how people are using AI in...

> Addition: ...how people are using AI, and just the game of learning to use AI well.

Voice Claude's summaries of Marlon's words, Nov 15, about 11:22–11:26, closing the thread "Idea: two sides of the game thing — MK's agency," #mk-work-in-progress.

Schell's figure (in *The Art of Game Design*, after Csikszentmihalyi's flow) plots challenge against skill: too hard and the player is anxious, too easy and bored; a good game raises the challenge as the player's skill rises, in a channel between the two. Marlon puts the people using AI on that chart. The tools give a rush of agency, then a pile of work and agents that outgrows them, and whether they stay in the channel depends on how the ramp rises.

Read that way, as CI does, teaching people to use AI is level design: what comes first, how much at once, when to add the next agent. It ties the week's game theory to the LL's work with students and faculty, and to Marlon's own week of running many panes.
