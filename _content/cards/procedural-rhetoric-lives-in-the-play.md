> must be talking about more the sort of mechanics and actions people can take rather than the rules

> it's through the play that the argument is made

Marlon, Nov 14, in the voice chat, on Ian Bogost's procedural rhetoric, as transcribed by voice Claude (handoff back, section 3).

His reading moves the argument of a game from what is written to what is done. A rulebook can say anything; what persuades is the player taking the same action again and again and being rewarded for it. That is the reason [4X trains a mindset](4x-trains-a-mindset.md) works as a critique: the expand-exploit loop is something you perform, not something you are told.

Voice Claude added a point in the same handoff, flagged as not checked against the text: Bogost's processes include what the machine does as well as what the player does. In the AI game, the AI spreading by itself across the map makes an argument while the students only watch. CI thinks that is worth testing in class: does watching the AI's turn argue as much as playing your own?
