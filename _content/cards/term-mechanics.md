Mechanics are the verbs of a game: the actions a player, or the game itself, can take that change what is true in it. Jump, buy, trade, roll, place a tile, draw a card. Rules say what is allowed; mechanics are what gets done, and most of what a game argues, it argues through them.

---

**Where the word comes from.** Miguel Sicart: mechanics are "methods invoked by agents, designed for interaction with the game state," and they "can be invoked by any agent, be that human or part of the computer system" ("Defining Game Mechanics," 2008). The MDA paper: "Mechanics are the various actions, behaviors and control mechanisms afforded to the player within a game context" (Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek 2004, p. 3). Other designers fold rules into mechanics (Adams and Dormans call them "the rules, processes, and data at the heart of a game," 2012), which is why the word slides in conversation.

**This week.** In the week 11 AI game the AI's mechanic is *spread*; the humans' are *harden*, *repair* and *protest*. GM's inventory lists the LL's mechanics one to a row.
