**What it is.** Card 7 of GM's deck, in "Time": players act one at a time in a set sequence. The deck's example is chess.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: tell a story one word at a time around the table. The build: a cue list where *Go* passes the turn and lights the next player's name. In every version of the AI game, the AI's phase comes first and then each faction in turn.

**Where it is in the week.** In [Take apart a board game](take-apart-a-board-game.md) it is one of the rules students enforce themselves, and the first thing a video game takes out of their hands. [The phone plays the AI](the-phone-plays-the-ai.md) puts a non-human player in the order: the AI's turn comes after the table's every round, and nobody plays it. [A round a day](a-round-a-day.md) stretches a turn to a day. [Every tap is a row](every-tap-is-a-row.md) does without it: players take the turn by grabbing it.

**What it does for learning.** Turn order is so basic it disappears, which makes it a good place to see Marlon's point that rules belong more to pre-digital games, where people police the turns. Moving the AI in the order changes the game's argument: an AI that acts first sets the agenda; one that acts last answers. The inventory's note on AI says exactly that.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about turn order itself. CM's reading: CI's cards [Agents are turns all the way down](agents-are-turns-all-the-way-down.md) and [Claude thinks at turn speed](claude-thinks-at-turn-speed.md) carry his thinking about turns and agents, and this is the mechanic under them.
