**What it is.** Card 39 of GM's deck, in "Space and the body": the real world is the board. The deck's example is *Pokémon Go*.

**How it plays.** GM's two-minute version: hide five numbered cards around the studio, and the first to find them in order wins. The build: printed QR posters around the room, where scanning one fires a cue. Not in the AI game yet; the inventory notes the map could be built from William's students' index-card database of real infrastructure.

**Where it is in the week.** In William's workshop, [Cards become the map](cards-become-the-map.md) does that: each table lays out its own September 28 cards as places, marks what each yields and depends on, and plays a round of the AI on it. GL's idea behind [The room is the board](the-room-is-the-board.md) was the studio version: tag the projector, the network rack and the water fountain, so that acting on a place means walking to it (PB built it with a camera instead). SW's [Every resource names a place](every-resource-names-a-place.md) is the economy version.

**What it does for learning.** The inventory's note on AI: the board is the real region, the data centers, rivers and substations near you. Its readings make it an infrastructure lesson: Hogan on a data center's water, Easterling on infrastructure as power, Star on infrastructure that stays invisible until it breaks. A map of real places turns the game's outcomes into claims about somewhere the players know.

**Which of Marlon's ideas it serves.** No words of Marlon's are on record about location play itself. CM's reading: it serves his first build for the week, game mechanics printed and displayed throughout the LL (CI's card [Game mechanics printed throughout the space](game-mechanics-printed-throughout-the-space.md)), by making the space part of the game rather than its wall.
