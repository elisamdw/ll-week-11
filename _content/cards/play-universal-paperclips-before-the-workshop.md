> So the plan: before class, students play *Paperclips* for an hour (it's free in the browser) and read a bit of Bostrom and Bogost.

> *Paperclips* is to Bostrom what we'd like the students' game to be to your course: a response to the readings, made as a game.

Marlon's proposal for William Stafford, Nov 3 (`NB/_context/projects/50-in-focus/william-stafford/workshop-2/proposal-for-william.md`).

The second sentence is the reason for the first. In the same letter he explains: Bostrom's 2003 essay imagines an AI that turns the earth into paperclip factories, and Frank Lantz's game (2017) never states that claim; it puts you in the AI's place and walks you through its moves until you find you've done what Bostrom describes. Playing it before class gives the students one finished example of a reading turned into a game, the thing they will try in the workshop.

An hour matters, as CI reads it: the game changes scale twice (business, Earth, space), and the turns that make the argument come late. A student who stops at ten minutes has played a clicker; at an hour they have watched the machine take over.

The Stafford workspace drafted the activity: [Play Paperclips and log the easy moments](../activities/stafford/play-paperclips-and-log-the-easy-moments.md).
