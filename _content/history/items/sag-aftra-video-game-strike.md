# The SAG-AFTRA video-game strike (2024–2025)

Part of [Strand 4: AI in game development](../4-ai-in-game-development.md) · [Timeline](../timeline.md)

**Before.** On January 9, 2024, at CES, SAG-AFTRA announced a separate deal with Replica Studios letting performers license digital voice replicas for games, with consent and the right to opt out. Some members were furious ("We don't want AI!!!" on the union's Instagram, per NBC).

**The strike, July 26, 2024.** After more than 18 months of talks on the Interactive Media Agreement, performers struck Activision Productions, Blindlight, Disney Character Voices, EA Productions, Formosa Interactive, Insomniac Games, Llama Productions (Epic), Take 2 Productions, VoiceWorks Productions, and WB Games. Duncan Crabtree-Ireland asked for "fair compensation and the right of informed consent for the A.I. use of their faces, voices, and bodies." Fran Drescher: "We're not going to consent to a contract that allows companies to abuse A.I."

**Darth Vader, May 2025.** Epic's *Fortnite* added a conversational Darth Vader in James Earl Jones's voice, made with his estate's permission (dialogue reportedly from Google's Gemini, audio from ElevenLabs). Players got it to swear within hours. On May 19, SAG-AFTRA filed an unfair labor practice charge against Llama Productions, saying it replaced bargaining-unit work without notice and "failed and refused to bargain in good faith with the union."

**The end.** A tentative agreement came the night of June 9, 2025; the strike was suspended at noon Pacific on June 11, after 320 days. Members ratified the contract on July 9, 2025, 95.04% to 4.96%. It requires "consent and disclosure requirements for AI digital replica use", lets performers "suspend consent for the generation of new material during a strike", and raises pay more than 15% at once, with further raises in 2025, 2026, and 2027.

**Why it matters.** It was the first major labor fight in games centered on generative AI, and it set consent and disclosure as the baseline for digital replicas. The Vader case showed an estate's consent and a union's bargaining rights pulling in different directions.
