# *Façade* (2005)

Part of [Strand 2: AI inside games](../2-ai-inside-games.md) · [Timeline](../timeline.md)

**Released** free for Windows in July 2005 (Mac OS X, August 2006), by Michael Mateas and Andrew Stern under the name Procedural Arts. It won the Grand Jury Prize at Slamdance in 2006.

**The game.** You visit Trip and Grace, old friends whose marriage comes apart over one evening, and you take part by typing anything you like. A **drama manager** (a beat sequencer) chooses which of 27 main story beats runs next; an average playthrough sees about 15. The characters are written in ABL, a reactive planning language for joint action. Your typed words are mapped to about 30 discourse acts (agree, criticize, flirt, mention the marriage) by more than 800 hand-written rules. Mateas's 2002 Carnegie Mellon thesis describes it as the first published interactive drama to integrate believable agents, drama management, and shallow natural-language processing.

**After.** A sequel, *The Party*, began in 2006, was halted in 2013, and resumed in 2024.

**Why it matters.** It is the bridge from authored game AI to today's LLM characters. Players typed anything; the system had to make sense of it; a story manager kept the drama on track. Every LLM-NPC demo since 2023 is re-running *Façade*'s experiment with a different language engine, and facing its central problem: how to let a character improvise while keeping the story's shape. For the Nov 18 class on AI co-stars, it is the ancestor.
