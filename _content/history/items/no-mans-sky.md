# *No Man's Sky* and its generator (2016–2026)

Part of [Strand 4: AI in game development](../4-ai-in-game-development.md) · [Timeline](../timeline.md)

**Released** August 9, 2016 on PS4 in North America (August 10 in Europe; Windows August 12), by Hello Games, a small studio in Guildford, England.

**The generator.** Every planet, creature and plant comes from rules run on a 64-bit seed, for 2^64 (about 18.4 quintillion) planets. At GDC 2017 Sean Murray described generating realistic and alien terrain from mathematics with no artist placing it, and testing a world no one could visit in full; Innes McKendrick described the pipeline from voxels to polygons to textures to population and simulation. It is the direct descendant of *Elite*'s eight seeded galaxies (1984).

**The launch.** Reviews were mixed (Metacritic 61 on PC, 71 on PS4). Players found the planets repetitive and promised features, multiplayer above all, missing; UK physical sales fell 81% in the second week; the studio went quiet.

**The comeback.** Free updates for ten years: Foundation (November 2016), NEXT (July 2018, adding multiplayer), Beyond (2019), and Worlds Part I and II (July 2024, January 2025), which rebuilt planet generation itself. Voyagers (August 2025) brought the most Steam players since launch.

**Why it matters.** It is the boldest commercial test of procedural generation, and the clearest lesson in the gap between a generator's range and a player's sense of variety: 18 quintillion planets can still feel the same. The fix was not a bigger number but years of authored tending.
