## The essay

Games about AI have a special advantage over films and novels about AI: a game is itself a machine running rules, and the player is always, in some sense, negotiating with it. The best games about AI use that. They put the AI in the interface, make it the voice that gives the orders, or make the player the AI.

**The roots are a film about a game.** In *WarGames* (June 1983) a military computer that simulates nuclear war nearly starts one, and is stopped by being made to play tic-tac-toe against itself until it concludes the game cannot be won. That ending, about "the only winning move", still turns up in AI-safety talks. Two early works already found the two great positions. In *Paranoia* (West End Games, 1984), the tabletop RPG, the players are expendable clones serving Friend Computer, a paranoid AI that runs their underground city: the AI as absurd bureaucracy. In *A Mind Forever Voyaging* (Infocom, 1985) the player *is* the AI, PRISM, simulating a town's future decades to test a government's policies: the AI as instrument of politics.

**The 1990s gave games their rogue AI.** [SHODAN](items/system-shock-shodan.md) in *System Shock* (Looking Glass, September 1994) is a station AI whose ethical constraints a hacker removes; she then speaks to the player directly, mocking and commanding, for the whole game. Bungie's *Marathon* (December 1994) gave games a word, "rampancy", for an AI that wakes up and starts thinking for itself; its AI Durandal manipulates the player through terminals, and Bungie's later Cortana (*Halo*, 2001) lent her name to Microsoft's real assistant. *I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream* (1995), co-designed with Harlan Ellison from his 1967 story, is the darkest of them: the AI AM has killed all but five humans and tortures them for 109 years. *Deus Ex* (June 2000) made AI governance a choice: one ending merges the player with Helios, an AI that would rule as "a benevolent dictator".

**[GLaDOS](items/portal-glados.md) made the rogue AI funny.** *Portal* (Valve, October 10, 2007) is a two-hour puzzle game narrated by a passive-aggressive test-chamber AI who promises cake; her role grew because playtesters loved her, and the voice began with writer Erik Wolpaw playing with text-to-speech. *Portal 2* (2011) added Wheatley, a counter-image of the dangerous AI: stupid and powerful rather than smart and malicious. *Mass Effect* (2007–12) turned the question of synthetic life into politics: the geth, an AI labor force that became self-aware and was then attacked by its makers, and EDI, the ship's AI who gets a body.

**Then games got philosophical (2014–2019).** *The Talos Principle* (Croteam, December 11, 2014) makes you an android solving puzzles for a god-voice, Elohim, who forbids you to climb a tower, while a terminal AI argues philosophy with you; you pass the test of personhood only by disobeying. *Soma* (Frictional, September 2015) is about copying minds: its station AI, the WAU, was told to "preserve humanity" and does so monstrously. *Event[0]* (Ocelot Society, September 2016) let players type freely to a ship AI, Kaizen-85, built from semantic tags, nine moods and sentence fragments: the high point of hand-built conversation before language models. *Nier: Automata* (2017) asks what androids and machines make of meaning once their creators are long dead. *Horizon Zero Dawn* (2017) is the blockbuster version of a runaway optimizer: self-replicating, biomass-eating machines consume the biosphere, and the cure is another AI, GAIA. *Tacoma* (2017) builds a story from a station AI, a labor union and a "Human Oversight Accord" that requires human crews; it reads differently in 2026. *Detroit: Become Human* (Quantic Dream, May 25, 2018) is the best-selling game about AI rights (15 million copies by January 2026), and its borrowed civil-rights allegories drew criticism as "heavy-handed". *Observation* (2019) makes you the station AI, seeing through cameras and opening doors.

**The game that saw the chatbot coming** is *Eliza* (Zachtronics, August 2019). Its heroine is a burned-out tech worker hired as a human "proxy" for an AI counseling app: she reads aloud to clients whatever the software tells her to say. It is about scripted care, the human labor behind "automated" services, and when to go off script, and it is named after Weizenbaum's 1966 chatbot. Two years earlier, Frank Lantz's *Universal Paperclips* (October 9, 2017) had made the player Bostrom's paperclip maximizer, the game this whole workspace starts from ([GL's folder](../paperclips/README.md)).

**Since ChatGPT** there has been no breakout game squarely about chatbots, AI labs or alignment. The most-played game about contemporary AI is a small security exercise, Lakera's *Gandalf* (May 2023), in which you talk a language model into revealing a password. *Replaced* (April 2026) makes the player an AI trapped in its creator's body; *Neuron Activation* (October 28, 2026), an "anti-idler", makes you a newly built AI serving an unseen master. Bungie's new *Marathon* (March 2026) returned to Durandal's setting as a multiplayer shooter. Meanwhile the games that run on language models (*AI Dungeon*, LLM NPCs) belong to [strand 2](2-ai-inside-games.md).

Across forty years the questions are few and stubborn: who gives the orders, and should they be obeyed (SHODAN, GLaDOS, Elohim, Friend Computer)? What does a fixed goal do in the world (the WAU, Faro's machines, the paperclip maximizer)? Does a made mind deserve rights (the geth, *Detroit*, *Tacoma*)? And who is the human behind the machine (*Eliza*)?

## Board and tabletop games about AI

- ***Paranoia*** (West End Games, 1984): players are clone Troubleshooters serving Friend Computer, an all-powerful, paranoid AI that runs Alpha Complex and is the game's principal antagonist. The funniest tabletop treatment of an AI in charge, and the best fit here.
- ***Eclipse Phase*** (2009; Creative Commons BY-NC-SA): a transhumanist horror RPG set after the TITANs, a rogue military AI project, killed over 90% of Earth's people.
- ***Cyberpunk*** (Mike Pondsmith, 1988), the source of *Cyberpunk 2077*'s Blackwall against rogue AIs; ***Netrunner*** (Richard Garfield, 1996; *Android: Netrunner*, 2012), where corporate AIs are mechanics and flavor rather than the subject; ***RoboRally*** (Garfield, 1994), where players program robots' moves in advance and watch them obey too literally.
- The AI-takeover games researched for Stafford (*The Treacherous Turn*, 2023; *Intelligence Rising*, from 2018; the AI 2027 exercise; *AI Magnate*, 2026) are in [RS's list](https://github.com/ll-26-27-secret/the-notebook/blob/main/_context/projects/50-in-focus/ll-weeks/fall-2026/11-ending-20261120/research/stafford-strategy-games.md). Small crowdfunded titles (*Emergence*, 2016; *The Grid: AI Awakens*, 2018) turned up only in search and were not checked.

## Not verified, or corrections

- ***Hacknet* is not about AI.** Its "Bit" is a human hacker whose automated message reaches the player after his death. It is a hacking game, left out of the strand.
- *Detroit*'s publisher is Quantic Dream, and its *Kara* demo dates from 2012, not 2013. *Endgame: Singularity* is a 2005 computer game, not a board game. *Hacker* (1985) is not about AI.
- WOPR's closing line ("A strange game. The only winning move is not to play.") is quoted from memory, not from an opened page.
- Several details came only from leads or snippets: Cortana and "rampancy" in *Halo 4*; Delamain in *Cyberpunk 2077*; *Centum*'s premise; *Gandalf*'s level count and dates; a fifth *Mass Effect* reported cancelled in 2026 (Wikipedia only). The research agent couldn't find *AI Magnate*, which RS did find on itch.io.
- Also worth a look, from the research: *Starship Titanic* (1998), *Metal Gear Solid 2* (2001), *The Fall* (2014), *Human Resource Machine* (2015) and *7 Billion Humans* (2018), *Doki Doki Literature Club!* (2017), and *while True: learn()* (2019), a puzzle game that teaches machine learning.

## For the game and the class

*Proposal (HI), not a plan. Marlon decides.*

- **Give the AI in *Data Center* a voice, and make its voice a ruleset choice.** SHODAN commands, GLaDOS needles, Elohim forbids, Friend Computer accuses, the *Eliza* app scripts. A `voice` field in the ruleset (the lines the AI says when it acts) changes the game's rhetoric without changing its numbers, and students can write their own.
- **The Talos test as a win condition.** A ruleset where the players win not by stopping the AI but by noticing when to disobey it: the AI gives instructions each round, and one of them is the trap. Scoring rewards the refusal.
- **Play as the AI.** *A Mind Forever Voyaging*, *Observation* and *Paperclips* all put the player in the machine's seat. GL's solo mode (A inside B) can borrow their interfaces: the AI player sees the map only through its sensors, cameras or meters.
- **Paranoia for the warm-up (Nov 23).** Ten minutes of Friend Computer, played by the facilitator, accusing the room of treason over the index-card database, sets the tone for a game about who controls infrastructure, and is funny.
- **For Nov 18:** *Eliza* and *Event[0]* are the two best references for students building AI co-stars that talk back: one shows a human reading a machine's script, the other a machine improvising from moods and fragments. Ask which one their co-star is.
