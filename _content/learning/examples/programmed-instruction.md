# Programmed instruction

**What and when — source account.** Teaching content is divided into frames that require a learner response and provide feedback. Skinner’s 1958 account includes teaching through carefully prepared programs; a machine is one delivery form.

**Learner model — HR’s reading.** In a linear program, the frame sequence carries most of the instructional model. Do not confuse this with a separately maintained conceptual map of an individual.

**Game-like, or not — HR’s reading.** Completion and repeated feedback may feel game-like, but choice and open exploration can be narrow.

**What we can borrow — proposal.** Store a lesson’s prompts and feedback separately from its interface, so students can inspect the instructional sequence.

**Evidence and media notes.** This card covers the linear approach described by Skinner, not all later branching programmed instruction. Media: the source PDF contains program examples.
