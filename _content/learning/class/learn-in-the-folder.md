**What students do**

1. **Pick a topic (3 min)** small enough to hold in half an hour, and one you want: something from your Final Project research, a rule from this week's game, a concept from a reading. Examples: why a save file can bring back a whole game; what a mechanic is, as against a rule; how a camera's shutter speed changes motion blur.
2. **Find the edge (7 min).** Type `/learn <topic>`. Claude asks short questions to find where your understanding stops, then agrees a goal with you, in your words. Answer honestly; "I don't know" is useful here.
3. **One step at a time (15 min).** Claude shows a short route of ideas; change it if you like, then `/teach`. Each step: guess first, read one short step, explain it back, say how sure you are (1 to 4) and why. Aim to hold three or four ideas.
4. **Open the files (5 min).** Ask Claude to run `/graph`, then open `graph.md` and `graph/attempts.json`. Find one answer in your own words. Find one idea and what it `requires`.

**What it teaches:** that an AI tutor's memory can be plain files you own and can read, and that "learned" can mean something specific (you explained it back and it felt obviously true) rather than "the chat happened".

**Watch for:** Claude lecturing several ideas at once (say "one step at a time, and check me after each"); answers not appearing in the files (say "write what we've done to the graph"); a topic too big for half an hour (narrow the goal, not the pace).
