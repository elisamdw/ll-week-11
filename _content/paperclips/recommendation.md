# Rebuild, adapt, or write our own?

Marlon, Nov 14: "maybe even rebuild it---or is it an opensource repo?" This page is GL's proposal.

| Route | What it means | Allowed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rebuild** | Copy or port Lantz's code and text | No: there is no licence ([source-and-licence.md](source-and-licence.md)). Only with written permission from Lantz or Everybody House Games. |
| **Adapt** | Fork a GitHub mirror and change it | No: the mirrors have no rights to give. |
| **Original game in its spirit** | Our code, our words, its mechanics | Yes. |

**Recommendation: write an original game in its spirit, and have students play the real one.** *Paperclips* stays the reading: free in the browser, an hour of play before class. Our game is the response, the way *Paperclips* is a response to Bostrom.

## What we borrow (mechanics, not assets)

- One arbitrary goal as the only score.
- Incremental growth: numbers rise on their own, and the player tunes the rates.
- Trust as a currency that gates new powers and can be spent down.
- Staged reveals: the screen changes as the game shifts scale (business, Earth, space).
- Drift: the things you build start pursuing goals of their own.
- A prestige reset: start again with something carried over.

## What we add for Stafford and the class

- **Several players**, so the *Civilization* half of "a cross between Universal Paperclips and Civilization" (Marlon, Nov 13) has humans who decide together.
- **Infrastructure on a map**, from the students' index-card database, with Perrow's tight coupling as a failure rule.
- **Rules in a data file** students can edit: the co-design surface, and the thing William keeps.

The three options are in [../game/design.md](../game/design.md).

Ask Frank Lantz or Everybody House Games for permission to fork or adapt it for teaching. That is an outward-facing message, so it waits on Marlon.
